  Pinkasova T, Deissova T, Jabandziev P

  [10.1002/jpn3.70620](https://doi.org/10.1002/jpn3.70620) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: A scoping review on emerging biomarkers in inflammatory bowel disease: Towards precision medicine in diagnosis and therapeutic management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: The identification of novel noninvasive diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for pediatric inflammatory bowel disease (PIBD), including pediatric Crohn's disease (pCD) and ulcerative colitis (pUC), is crucial for improving therapeutic outcomes. Given their involvement in PIBD pathogenesis, microRNAs (miRNAs) represent promising candidates. This study investigates their potential diagnostic value and clinical utility. METHODS: The study involved 81 treatment-naive PIBD patients and 30 non-IBD controls from whom stool samples were collected. Fecal microRNA analysis was performed using small RNA next-generation sequencing. Differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) were identified in the exploratory phase (34 PIBD, 20 non-IBD children). Logistic regression was used to establish diagnostic and prognostic miRNA expression signatures. The designed miRNA panels were validated using leave-one-out cross-validation and on an independent cohort (47 PIBD, 10 non-IBD children). RESULTS: A diagnostic signature of four fecal miRNAs (10392-5p, miR-1246, miR-4695-5p, and miR-5006-5p) distinguished pCD from non-IBD with 79.55% accuracy. Three fecal miRNAs (miR-1290, miR-3960, and miR-5006-5p) differentiated pUC from non-IBD with 78.26% accuracy. For treatment-naïve PIBD patients, panels of five and six miRNAs predicted residual disease activity at 3 months with 100% accuracy for pUC patients and 86.28% accuracy for pCD patients. Additionally, distinct signatures of four and six miRNAs predicted relapse within 12 months with accuracies of 96.87% (pUC) and 88.01% (pCD), respectively. CONCLUSION: We identified fecal miRNA expression profiles with strong diagnostic and prognostic value in PIBD. These results contribute to improved stratification of disease severity and risk, supporting the advancement of precision and personalized approaches in pediatric IBD management.

- [ ] Read later
  Javier P Gisbert, Alfredo J Lucendo, María Chaparro
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-10-26
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag175](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag175) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: A Practical Approach to Abnormal Liver Enzymes in Pediatric IBD in the Biologic and Targeted Therapy Era.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Hepatic steatosis is among the most frequent hepatobiliary abnormalities in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), but its clinical interpretation is increasingly complex. This narrative review provides evidence-based guidance for gastroenterologists managing adults with IBD and hepatic steatosis. Cohort and meta-analytic evidence indicates that hepatic steatosis affects approximately one-quarter to one-third of patients with IBD, although estimates vary according to diagnostic method, population selection, disease phenotype, metabolic burden, and study design. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) provides a useful framework for patients with cardiometabolic dysfunction, but a steatosis-centered approach is more inclusive in IBD because it accommodates lean or undernourished patients, inflammation-associated phenotypes, ileal or bile acid-related mechanisms, alcohol overlap, medication-related vulnerability, and mixed presentations. The main clinical priority is not merely to detect liver fat, but to identify meaningful fibrosis, cardiometabolic risk, and modifiable contributors. Normal aminotransferases do not exclude steatosis or fibrosis. Screening should be risk-based rather than universal. Fibrosis-4 index can be used pragmatically for initial triage in stable adults, but its IBD-specific limitations require cautious interpretation; transient elastography is most useful as a secondary fibrosis assessment or as a combined first test when readily available. Follow-up should be driven by fibrosis and cardiometabolic risk rather than by IBD alone, and hepatology referral should be reserved for elevated liver stiffness, persistent or cholestatic liver-test abnormalities, portal-hypertension features, suspected alternative liver disease, discordant tests, or clinically significant hepatic dysfunction. Hepatic steatosis alone should not determine IBD drug selection, although liver dysfunction and treatment-specific hepatotoxicity may modify monitoring or therapeutic choices.

  Nearest seed: Effects of cuproptosis and its application in inflammatory bowel disease (Review).

  > [!abstract]-
  > Ulcerative colitis (UC); as one of the inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD), arises from chronic inflammation of the intestinal tract. In this study, we addressed the possible protective mechanisms of vonoprazan, as a potassium-competitive acid blocker, on dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-mediated UC. Therefore, male BALB-c mice administered oral vonoprazan (10 or 20 mg/kg/day) for 10 days and received drinking water containing 3% w/v DSS for 7 days from day 4. Vonoprazan exhibited a promising effect against UC by increasing mice body weight, colon length, and spleen weight, decreasing serum C-reactive protein (CRP) concentration and the disease activity index (DAI), and alleviating colonic histopathological changes. Colonic tissue antioxidants increased significantly in vonoprazan-treated groups. Additionally, colonic tissue oxidative stress was significantly ameliorated in vonoprazan-treated mice. Furthermore, vonoprazan preserved the integrity of intestinal mucosal barrier function and attenuated UC by decreasing the levels of immuno-inflammatory cells, inflammation-related proteins, and pro-inflammatory cytokines, elevating the tight junction proteins levels, and ameliorating mitochondrial energy metabolism. Taken together, vonoprazan protected against DSS-mediated UC in mice via antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities that maintained tight junction architecture.

- [ ] **Biosimilars in inflammatory bowel disease: from initial uncertainty to clinical normalization.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Argüelles-Arias F, Zabana Y
  Revista espanola de enfermedades digestivas | 2026-10-26
  [10.17235/reed.2026.12282/2026](https://doi.org/10.17235/reed.2026.12282/2026) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Subcutaneous infliximab as a maintenance option in pediatric IBD: a real-world cohort including younger and lower-weight children.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Biosimilars have become an integral part of the treatment of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), evolving from initial uncertainty regarding indication extrapolation and non-medical switching to widespread clinical acceptance. Early concerns focused mainly on the absence of disease-specific evidence and the potential impact of switching on effectiveness, safety, and immunogenicity. Over the past decade, however, extensive real-world evidence and randomized data have consistently supported the use of infliximab and adalimumab biosimilars in both biologic-naïve patients and patients switched from originator products. Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses further reinforce these findings, showing preserved clinical remission, treatment persistence, inflammatory biomarkers, and immunogenicity after switching. The biosimilar landscape is now expanding to newer therapeutic targets, including ustekinumab, while biosimilar-to-biosimilar and multiple switching is becoming increasingly relevant in routine practice. Nevertheless, scientific interchangeability should not be equated to automatic substitution. Switching decisions should remain clinically supervised and involve gastroenterologists within a multidisciplinary framework, taking into account disease activity, previous response, safety, immunogenicity, and patient preferences. Appropriate communication is also essential to minimize a potential nocebo effect and to avoid unnecessary discontinuation. Biosimilars should therefore be considered fully established biological therapies rather than lower-tier alternatives to originator drugs. Future research should focus less on re-confirming single-switch equivalence and more on unresolved questions, including long-term outcomes after multiple switching, biosimilars of newer therapeutic targets, patient-reported outcomes, treatment persistence, and comparative pharmacoeconomic impact across healthcare systems.

- [ ] **Protective effects of quercetin in experimental ulcerative colitis: Potential involvement of ERK-MMP signaling.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Zhang Q, Guo M
  Pakistan journal of pharmaceutical sciences | 2027-01-01
  [10.36721/pjps/2026.40.16479.1](https://doi.org/10.36721/pjps/2026.40.16479.1) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Ulcerative colitis is a relapsing inflammatory bowel disease driven by dysregulated immunity, epithelial barrier injury and microbial imbalance. Current therapies remain limited by relapse, adverse effects and incomplete response. Quercetin has anti-inflammatory and barrier-protective activities and ERK-MMP signaling may link inflammation to mucosal tissue damage. OBJECTIVES: To evaluate whether the protective effects of quercetin in experimental ulcerative colitis (UC) are associated with modulation of ERK-MMP signaling, while avoiding causal overinterpretation beyond the animal and cellular evidence. METHODS: A dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced mouse model and a lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophage inflammatory model were used. Mice were allocated to the control, UC model and low-, medium- and high-dose quercetin groups. Disease activity index (DAI; unitless), colon length, histopathology, inflammatory cytokines and ERK/MMP-related molecules were evaluated by histological staining, ELISA, qRT-PCR and western blotting. In-vitro, quercetin was compared with the ERK phosphorylation inhibitor U0126 and an agonistic stimulus to explore pathway-related associations. The study was exploratory and did not include genetic rescue experiments or human validation. RESULTS: In DSS-treated mice, medium- and high-dose quercetin were associated with improved colon morphology, reduced DAI scores and partial restoration of colon length. Quercetin decreased pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and increased IL-10 expression. In both mouse colon tissues and LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells, quercetin was associated with reduced ERK1/2 activation and lower MMP-3 and MMP-9 expression, together with partial recovery of TIMP expression. These molecular changes were directionally consistent with the effects observed after ERK inhibition in-vitro. CONCLUSION: Quercetin showed protective effects in experimental UC models and these effects may be associated with modulation of the ERK-MMP signaling axis. Further pathway-specific rescue experiments, pharmacokinetic studies and human-relevant models are required before this mechanism can be considered definitive or clinically translatable.

  Nearest seed: Impact of Upper Gastrointestinal Involvement on Clinical Outcomes in Paediatric Inflammatory Bowel Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Vulvar Crohn's disease (CD) is a rare and frequently under-recognised extraintestinal manifestation in children and may precede gastrointestinal symptoms, leading to significant diagnostic delays. We report the case of a 13-year-old girl with a 3-year history of chronic vulvar swelling, erythema, and fissures, initially misdiagnosed despite extensive investigations. Vulvar biopsy ultimately revealed non-caseating granulomatous dermatitis consistent with metastatic CD, and a subsequent endoscopic evaluation confirmed colonic CD. Due to severe and refractory vulvar involvement, treatment with anti-tumour necrosis factor-α (anti-TNF-α) therapy was required, with dose intensification necessary to achieve disease control. Furthermore, we performed a narrative review of the literature and identified 56 paediatric cases of vulvar CD. In most patients, vulvar manifestations preceded the diagnosis of intestinal disease and were frequently independent of gastrointestinal disease activity. Vulvar swelling was the most common presentation, often painless and isolated. Diagnosis was based on a combination of clinical, endoscopic, radiological, and histological findings. Gastrointestinal evaluation played a central role, while pelvic magnetic resonance imaging was essential to exclude contiguous fistulising disease. Vulvar biopsy was performed in most cases but may not be required when CD is confirmed endoscopically and alternative diagnoses have been excluded. Reported treatment ranged from topical therapies and antibiotics to systemic immunomodulators and biologics, with frequent need for escalation to anti-TNF-α therapy in refractory cases. Early recognition and a multidisciplinary approach are essential to prevent irreversible tissue damage and optimise long-term outcomes in affected children.

  Lin L, Zhu J, Su X

  Nearest seed: Rapid fecal lipidome profiling in inflammatory bowel disease: A novel approach for exploratory disease molecular characterization and therapeutic monitoring.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Disrupted membrane lipid homeostasis is implicated in epithelial barrier dysfunction and inflammatory signaling in ulcerative colitis. Associated lipid alterations include a reduced phosphatidylcholine-to-phosphatidylethanolamine (PC/PE) ratio and accumulation of lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC) and ceramide. Although oleic acid (OA) has shown protective effects in experimental colitis, its therapeutic utility is limited by rapid metabolic turnover. We therefore tested whether 2-hydroxyoleic acid (2-OHOA), a synthetic hydroxylated OA derivative with enhanced metabolic stability, protects against intestinal inflammatory injury. In IFN-γ/TNF-α-stimulated Caco-2 monolayers, 2-OHOA pretreatment preserved epithelial barrier function, attenuated the increase in membrane fluidity, and reduced STAT3 and NF-κB activation, whereas OA at the same concentration provided little protection. In a dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced murine colitis model, 2-OHOA pretreatment attenuated weight loss, disease activity index, colon shortening, and reduced the spleen index and histological injury scores, whereas OA at the same dose had minimal effects. 2-OHOA also attenuated colonic induction of Il-1β, Il-6, and Tnf-α expression and partially preserved occludin protein levels. Lipidomic analysis showed attenuation of DSS-induced PC depletion, shifts in the PC/PE and LPC/PC ratios, and ceramide and hexosylceramide accumulation. In cytokine-challenged Caco-2 cells, knockdown of the PC biosynthetic enzyme CCTα limited PC preservation by 2-OHOA, while PC(34:1) supplementation attenuated barrier dysfunction, supporting a functional contribution for PC homeostasis to epithelial protection. Together, these findings support preventive effects of 2-OHOA in experimental colitis, accompanied by preservation of lipid homeostasis and epithelial barrier function and reduced inflammatory signaling.
