- [ ] **Intravenous Versus Subcutaneous Induction with Guselkumab: Week 12 and Week 96 Efficacy in GALAXI 2 & 3 and GRAVITI.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  D'Haens GR, Afzali A, Sands BE
  Clinical gastroenterology and hepatology : the official clinical practice journal of the American Gastroenterological Association | 2026-10-27
  [10.1016/j.cgh.2026.10.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cgh.2026.10.011) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: TREAT-TO-TARGET IN INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE: PROMISE, PERILS, AND THE PATH FORWARD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND & AIMS: Guselkumab is approved for intravenous (IV) or subcutaneous (SC) induction in moderately to severely active Crohn's disease (CD). We compared outcomes and evaluated the onset of guselkumab efficacy with IV or SC induction and charted the long-term efficacy and safety of guselkumab SC maintenance following either route of induction. METHODS: Treatment effects of IV and SC induction were compared via inverse probability weighting (IPW) analysis. Clinical, endoscopic, and inflammatory biomarker outcomes in participants randomized to placebo or guselkumab in GALAXI (IV induction) and GRAVITI (SC induction) were evaluated through Week 12, including analyses across predefined subgroups. Efficacy and safety outcomes through Week 96 were reported from the long-term extensions (LTEs) of GALAXI and GRAVITI. RESULTS: IPW analysis found no statistical differences (all P > .05) between guselkumab IV and SC induction efficacy. In the per-trial comparisons with placebo, both IV and SC induction were statistically superior for Week 12 clinical response (Δ 34.5-42.8); similar efficacy was seen in Week 12 analyses of clinical remission rates (Δ 25.1-34.9), and abdominal pain (Δ 0.6-0.7) and stool frequency scores (Δ 1.2-2.0). Both routes of guselkumab induction provided rapid onset of efficacy, with clinical response and clinical remission observed by Week 4. IV and SC guselkumab induction regimens were similarly effective at Week 12 across prespecified subgroups of baseline disease location and clinical, endoscopic, and inflammatory biomarker disease activity. Adverse events during induction with SC and IV guselkumab were similar and comparable to placebo. Efficacy was maintained after 96 weeks of guselkumab therapy: long-term outcomes of clinical remission, endoscopic response, endoscopic remission, and deep remission were unaffected by the route of induction: 39.5-51.2% of guselkumab participants achieved deep remission at Week 96. Exposure-adjusted incidence rates of AEs, SAEs, and AEs leading to discontinuation through Week 96 were lower with guselkumab versus placebo and generally similar between GALAXI and GRAVITI. CONCLUSIONS: IV and SC induction with guselkumab were equally effective across a wide range of patients with CD, including those with high baseline disease activity. Efficacy with guselkumab through Week 96 was unaffected by the route of induction. No new safety concerns were identified.

  Nearest seed: Enterococcus faecium MMX derived from traditional fermented mare's milk alleviates colitis by modulating the LCN2-NLRP3 pyroptosis axis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Enterotoxigenic Bacteroides fragilis (ETBF) is a pivotal pathogenic microbe associated with intestinal dysbiosis. Its major virulence factor, Bacteroides fragilis toxin (BFT), is a zinc-dependent metalloproteinase that disrupts intestinal barrier function by degrading adhesion molecules in epithelial cells, thereby triggering inflammatory signaling cascades and inducing aberrant proliferation of intestinal epithelial cells. Beyond this direct toxin-mediated pathogenicity, ETBF-induced pathogenesis also involves intercellular signal transduction mediated by outer membrane vesicles and complex epigenetic regulatory perturbations. Collectively, these pathological processes promote the development of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and colorectal cancer (CRC). This review elaborates on the structure-activity relationships of BFT and its multifaceted roles in inducing tissue damage, remodeling the inflammatory microenvironment, and driving carcinogenic progression. We also analyze the technical limitations of current clinical diagnostic approaches for ETBF, and prospect the development of targeted detection methodologies, including integrated multi-omics analyses and molecular diagnostic systems. Furthermore, this review systematically expounds on a variety of promising targeted therapeutic strategies against ETBF, clarifies their potential clinical application value and latest research progress, and thus provides a comprehensive theoretical framework for the clinical diagnosis, treatment and prognostic management of ETBF-associated diseases.

  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-10-28
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag126](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag126) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: The VISION-AI Trial: protocol for a pragmatic randomized controlled non-inferiority trial comparing artificial intelligence-guided colonoscopy to pancolonic chromoendoscopy for neoplasia detection in adults with colorectal inflammatory bowel disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) undergo colonoscopy for diagnosis, disease assessment, and colorectal cancer surveillance. We used the UK National Endoscopy Database (NED) to evaluate the quality of IBD surveillance and identify factors associated with procedural quality.
  >
  >
  > Design
  > All colonoscopies uploaded to NED between January 1 and December 31, 2024 were analyzed. IBD procedures were identified from recorded indications and classified as assessment or surveillance. Key performance indicators (KPIs) were assessed against national guidance. Case-mix-adjusted impacts of endoscopist- and service-level factors were evaluated using multilevel mixed-effects logistic regression.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Of 588 841 colonoscopies, 2.7% (n = 15 971) were IBD surveillance. Although 56.8% of endoscopists performed at least one surveillance procedure, 18.9% performed one or more per month and delivered 57.1% of all surveillance. IBD surveillance quality was high (cecal intubation 96.1%; median withdrawal time 13 min), but adequate bowel preparation was below target (80.2%). Endoscopists performing ≥1 surveillance/month had higher adjusted polyp detection (used as a surrogate marker of mucosal inspection quality rather than true dysplasia) (odds ratio [OR] 1.26), and procedures allocated extended time had higher detection (OR 1.40).
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > These findings suggest that regular exposure to IBD surveillance, adequate procedural time, and consistent use/documentation of dye-based chromoendoscopy are associated with higher polyp detection during IBD surveillance colonoscopy. Importantly, these findings relate to polyp detection rather than histologically confirmed dysplasia detection. Further work is required to determine whether service concentration or minimum surveillance volumes improve dysplasia-related outcomes. Service-level pathways analogous to English Bowel Cancer Screening with national standards and performance monitoring may warrant consideration.

- [ ] **Hyperuricemia aggravates acute colitis through NLRP3-associated macrophage-lineage inflammatory responses**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Junyi Ji, Yize Wang, Chaohui Yu
  Inflammatory Bowel Diseases | 2026-10-27
  [10.1093/ibd/izag227](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag227) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: From signals to systems: the epigenetic-microbiome-mitochondrial axis in IBD pathogenesis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs) are chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract with rising prevalence. Hyperuricemia has been associated with IBD, but whether elevated uric acid (UA) directly aggravates intestinal inflammation and how it affects immune responses remain unclear.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > We used hepatocyte-specific Glut9 knockout mice, allopurinol intervention, and acute UA administration in wild-type mice to assess dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced acute colitis. Lamina propria immune cells were profiled by expanded flow cytometry; phagocytic/macrophage-lineage and NLRP3 pathways were perturbed with clodronate liposomes, MCC950, and Nlrp3 siRNA. Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) and public human IBD mucosal transcriptomic datasets were analyzed.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Glut9 HKO mice developed higher serum UA and more severe DSS-induced colitis than controls, whereas allopurinol reduced serum UA and ameliorated disease. Acute UA administration during DSS exposure similarly worsened colitis in wild-type mice. Hyperuricemia expanded colonic monocyte/macrophage-lineage cells during DSS challenge, increased pro-inflammatory cells, and enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome-associated signaling. Clodronate-sensitive macrophage depletion and MCC950 attenuated disease severity. In BMDMs, soluble UA enhanced LPS-primed inflammatory activation and inflammasome-associated readouts, which were reduced by NLRP3 inhibition or knockdown. In human IBD mucosal transcriptomic datasets, purine metabolism scores positively correlated with inflammatory macrophage and NLRP3 inflammasome signatures.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Elevated UA aggravates DSS-induced acute colitis and amplifies inflammatory monocyte/macrophage-lineage responses associated with NLRP3 inflammasome signaling. These findings identify hyperuricemia as a metabolic modifier of acute intestinal injury and support further investigation of UA-linked innate immune activation in IBD.

  [10.1111/apt.71075](https://doi.org/10.1111/apt.71075) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Pattern-based histologic approach in colitis without chronic architectural damage: GIPAD recommendations.

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Background
  > Elevated mast cell (MC) counts in colonic biopsies are frequently observed in patients with chronic diarrhoea or unspecific abdominal symptoms, potentially leading to a diagnosis of mast cell activation syndrome. However, the clinical relevance of MC quantification in this setting remains controversial.
  >
  >
  > Aim
  > This study aimed to determine the proportion of patients with elevated MC counts who presented with diarrhoea compared to those with normal counts. As a secondary endpoint, we assessed how often MC quantification was performed and what the underlying clinical indications were.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > We analysed a large US national pathology database with 3.3 million colonoscopies performed between January 2009 and December 2023. Screening colonoscopies for colon cancer detection were excluded. For patients with multiple colonoscopies, only the first procedure was included. This resulted in a total of 2,059,147 colonoscopies.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Colonic mast cells were evaluated in 3508 reports (0.17%). In 1167 cases the report contained either the expressions indicating increased MC, or a MC count > 20 MC/high power field (hpf). Diarrhoea was present in 58.3%, 54.1%, 54.7%, 50.0% and 33.3% of patients with MC count of < 20/hpf, 20–39/hpf, 40–59/hpf, 60–98/hpf and ≥ 99/hpf, respectively.
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > These data do not show a relationship between diarrhoea and elevated MC in the colonic mucosa. Therefore, we suggest that the incorporation of MC quantification into the histologic evaluation of colonoscopic biopsies obtained for common GI indications may not be warranted.

- [ ] Relevant

  Rachel Kuo, Ciarán O’Hanlon, Gary S. Collins
  npj Digital Medicine | 2026-10-27
  [10.1038/s41746-026-03303-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03303-z) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (CLAIM):                     Explanation, Elaboration, and Examples

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Fractures affect approximately 178 million people annually, and artificial intelligence (AI) tools for fracture detection are increasingly approved by regulators. We conducted an umbrella review and second-order meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024516414), searching six databases to 4/06/2025 for systematic reviews or meta-analyses of AI fracture detection using any imaging modality. Screening, extraction, and quality assessment were performed in duplicate. We assessed review quality using AMSTAR-2, primary-study overlap using corrected covered area, and certainty using GRADE. Fifty reviews were included (25 systematic reviews and 25 meta-analyses; 387 primary studies). Twenty-one meta-analyses (269 primary studies) contributed to second-order meta-analysis, with slight overall overlap (4.31%). Pooled AI sensitivity was 0.897 (95% CI 0.882–0.911; 95% prediction interval 0.753–0.962) and specificity 0.904 (95% CI 0.891–0.916; prediction interval 0.824 to 0.950). Comparative evidence showed no consistent differences for AI, clinicians, and AI-assisted clinicians: however, this data was sparse and inconclusive. No review reported adverse events or other safety outcomes. Certainty was very low/low for almost all outcomes; all meta-analyses were rated critically low/low on AMSTAR-2. Although published estimates suggest high diagnostic accuracy, real-world and comparative performance remain uncertain. Prospective external validation, multi-reader-multi-case studies, and randomised trials are needed before broad clinical adoption.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Guidelines recommend continuing biologics during pregnancy but patients remain concerned about foetal drug exposure. For women in sustained remission, discontinuing biologics before active placental transfer (≈GW20) may represent a compromise. We evaluated disease activity and pregnancy outcomes in this situation and measured biologic concentrations in cord blood to estimate foetal exposure. METHODS: We conducted a retrospective study including all IBD pregnancies followed at the University Hospital of Liege (2010-2023). Disease flare was defined as the need for corticosteroids, start/resumption of biologics, or surgery. Preterm birth, caesarean section, low birth weight were recorded. - [ ] **Management and Outcomes of Bleeding Ectopic Varices in Patients With Liver Cirrhosis: A Systematic Review and Pooled Analysis**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Angela Zhu, Kiran Rasaratnam, Stuart K. Roberts
  Alimentary Pharmacology &amp; Therapeutics | 2026-10-27
  [10.1111/apt.71072](https://doi.org/10.1111/apt.71072) | Score: 0.92
  Nearest seed: A Practical Approach to Abnormal Liver Enzymes in Pediatric IBD in the Biologic and Targeted Therapy Era.

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Background
  > Bleeding ectopic varices is a rare but life‐threatening complication in patients with clinically significant portal hypertension and liver cirrhosis, with limited evidence available to guide treatment decisions.
  >
  >
  > Aim
  > To evaluate the safety and efficacy of current therapies for bleeding ectopic varices.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A systematic review with pooled analysis was performed based on searches conducted in Embase, MEDLINE and Cochrane Library databases. Case series, retrospective and prospective studies including adults with a history of liver cirrhosis or portal hypertension presenting with bleeding ectopic varices were included. Individual patient data on interventions, rebleeding, complications and mortality were pooled and summarised using descriptive analysis. No conventional meta‐analysis was conducted due to the heterogeneous nature of included studies.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > 44 studies (15 non‐consecutive case series, 25 single‐arm retrospective cohort studies, 3 double‐arm retrospective cohort studies and 1 single‐arm prospective study) involving 458 patients met selection criteria. Alcohol‐related liver disease (38.7%), viral hepatitis (18.8%) and primary sclerosing cholangitis (15.7%) were the most common aetiologies. Child‐Pugh A, B and C cirrhosis accounted for 29.7%, 46.7% and 23.5% of patients, respectively, and the pooled median Model for End Stage Liver Disease score was 14.0 (IQR 10.0–20.8). The most reported sites of ectopic varices were duodenal (27.7%), rectal (27.3%) and stomal/peri‐stomal (23.1%). Endoscopic sclerosant injection appeared to have a lower rebleeding rate than band ligation in the treatment of bleeding rectal varices (3.3% vs. 33.3%). Across variceal subtypes, transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) combined with variceal embolisation, was observed to have a lower rebleeding rate than TIPS alone. The pooled all‐cause mortality following small bowel, rectal and stomal variceal bleeding were 25.0%, 42.9%, and 66.6%, respectively, over a median follow‐up period of 354 (IQR 120–780) days. Small sample sizes, missing data and significant heterogeneity between studies limited data quality.
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > Bleeding ectopic varices frequently recurs and carries high mortality. An individualised approach in conjunction with early multidisciplinary team discussions is essential in achieving favourable patient outcomes.
