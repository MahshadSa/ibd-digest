- [ ] **Switching from Intravenous to Subcutaneous Infliximab in Children with Inflammatory Bowel Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Spencer EA
  Current gastroenterology reports | 2026-10-17
  [10.1007/s11894-026-01061-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11894-026-01061-2) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Subcutaneous infliximab for maintenance therapy in children and adolescents with inflammatory bowel disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > PURPOSE OF REVIEW: Subcutaneous infliximab (IFX-SC; CT-P13 SC/infliximab-dyyb/Remsima-SC/Zymfentra) is an approved maintenance option after intravenous (IV) induction in adults with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), but it is not approved for patients < 18 years, and the pediatric evidence remains dispersed across small, largely observational cohorts. This review synthesizes the published pediatric experience of transitioning from IV to SC infliximab, examining maintenance of remission, drug concentrations, safety, and acceptability, and situates it within the adult evidence base and the distinctly pediatric concerns of access and dosing. RECENT FINDINGS: A systematic search (PROSPERO: CRD420261465026) identified six independent, non-overlapping pediatric switch cohorts (n = 173). Random-effects proportional meta-analysis yielded pooled treatment persistence of 92% (95%CI 84-98), maintained clinical remission of 93% (95%CI 84-99), and any-cause discontinuation of 8% (95%CI 2-16), most often for injection-site intolerance, disease relapse, or paradoxical dermatitis. New anti-drug antibody formation was rare among the 129 children in cohorts measuring immunogenicity (four children, none sustained) while previously positive antibodies frequently resolved after switching. Steady-state SC concentrations ran roughly 2-fold above prior IV troughs, an expected step-up reflecting sustained subcutaneous exposure. Patient and caregiver satisfaction was high due to decreased school absence and travel, and cost analyses signaled net savings from avoided infusion-center use. The principal barrier was administrative: age-based denial. In appropriately selected children, transition to IFX-SC sustains remission with predictable pharmacokinetics, a reassuring immunogenicity profile, and excellent tolerability, mirroring the adult experience. These observations constitute a well-supported real-world signal and warrant prospective validation and pursuit of pediatric labeling.

  Jha A, Paudel N, Saddique MN
  The Annals of pharmacotherapy | 2026-12-01
  [10.1177/10600280261429462](https://doi.org/10.1177/10600280261429462) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Review Article: Beyond the Therapeutic Ceiling in Inflammatory Bowel Disease—Mechanism‐Based Positioning and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Etrasimod is an oral sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor modulator evaluated for induction and maintenance therapy in moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC). This meta-analysis aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of etrasimod in adults with active UC. OBJECTIVES: To assess the efficacy and safety of etrasimod as induction and maintenance therapy in adults with moderately to severely active UC. METHODS: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing etrasimod with placebo in adults (≥18 years) with moderately to severely active UC was conducted by searching PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL, and ClinicalTrials.gov through November 2025. Dichotomous outcomes were pooled as risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using random-effects models; heterogeneity was assessed with I2 and χ2 statistics. Analyses were performed with RevMan 5.4. RESULTS: Four RCTs, including 1239 patients (etrasimod = 826; placebo = 413) were analyzed. At 12 weeks, etrasimod significantly improved clinical remission (RR = 2.92, 95% CI 1.71-4.98; I2 = 57%), clinical response (RR = 1.71, 95% CI 1.46-2.02; I2 = 19%), endoscopic improvement (RR = 1.72, 95% CI 1.30-2.27; I2 = 36%), and mucosal healing (RR = 3.11, 95% CI 1.72-5.62; I2 = 54%). Beyond 40 weeks, etrasimod increased clinical remission (RR = 4.28, 95% CI 2.72-6.73; I2 = 0%), clinical response (RR = 2.20, 95% CI 1.74-2.78; I2 = 0%), endoscopic improvement (RR = 3.51, 95% CI 2.36-5.23; I2 = 0%), mucosal healing (RR = 6.93, 95% CI 3.61-13.33; I2 = 0%), and steroid-free remission (RR = 4.28, 95% CI 2.72-6.73; I2 = 0%). Any treatment-emergent adverse event was slightly higher with etrasimod (RR = 1.15, 95% CI 1.04-1.28; I2 = 0%), while serious adverse events (RR = 0.79, 95% CI 0.39-1.61; I2 = 38%) and discontinuations due to adverse events (RR = 2.17, 95% CI 0.54-8.67; I2 = 50%) did not differ significantly. CONCLUSION AND RELEVANCE: Etrasimod is effective in inducing and maintaining clinical and endoscopic remission in moderate-to-severe UC, with an acceptable safety profile.

- [ ] **The prevalence of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis.**

- [ ] Read later
  Chen T, Zhou Z, Xiao S
  Advances in nutrition (Bethesda, Md.) | 2026-10-17
  [10.1016/j.advnut.2026.100758](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advnut.2026.100758) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: A scoping review on emerging biomarkers in inflammatory bowel disease: Towards precision medicine in diagnosis and therapeutic management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > The epidemiology of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains poorly characterized. Existing evidence is fragmented across specific conditions or confined to single regions, and no study has simultaneously assessed the principal nutritional disorders-malnutrition, overweight or obesity, and sarcopenia-in a unified framework. To address this gap, we systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and China Biology Medicine from inception to July 31, 2024 and updated on July 30, 2026. Observational studies reporting prevalence based on standard diagnostic criteria were included. Pooled estimates with 95% confidence interval (CI) and prediction interval (PI) were derived using a random-effects model. Pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted by sex, age, disease subtype and activity, diagnostic criteria, study setting and gepgraphic variation. Study quality and publication bias were also assessed. After literature screening, 201 studies across 32 countries were included. Underweight affected 11.5% (95% CI: 9.0-14.6%, 95% PI : 1.0-61.7), whereas consensus-based malnutrition [GLIM (Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition), ESPEN (European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism), MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment), SGA/PG-SGA (Subjective Global Assessment or its variant Patient-Generated SGA) affected 27.6% (95% CI: 20.5-36.1%, 95% PI : 3.6-79.7). Notably, this malnutrition estimate was derived almost exclusively from hospital-based settings (over 80%) and predominantly from a single region. Overweight and obesity affected 23.1% (95% CI : 20.6-25.9, 95%PI : 8.1-50.8) and 13.5% (95%CI : 11.7-15.6, 95%PI : 2.9-44.6), respectively. For muscle depletion, low muscle mass defined by skeletal muscle index was identified in 48.3% (95% CI: 41.8-55.0%, 95% PI : 19.7-78.1), whereas consensus-based sarcopenia [EWGSOP (European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People) or AWGS (Asian Working Group on Sarcopenia)] affected 12.6% (95% CI: 6.1-24.2%, 95% PI : 0.9-70.6). Of note, the majority of included studies for malnutrition and sarcopenia were hospital-based. Active disease was associated with significantly higher prevalence of malnutrition. No significant differences were observed between disease subtypes. In conclusion, our findings provide a broad overview of the prevalence of major nutritional disorders in patients with IBD, highlighting that nutritional care in IBD should extend beyond simple weight monitoring. However, given the wide prediction intervals, these pooled estimates should be viewed as averages across highly heterogeneous settings rather than a precise global figure. Future research, particularly population-based studies with standardized diagnostic criteria across diverse regions, is needed to validate our observation.

- [ ] **Emulsifier Intake in Healthy Controls and Patients with Inflammatory Bowel Disease: A Cross-Sectional Comparative Study with Longitudinal Follow-Up of Long-Term Outcomes.**

- [ ] Read later
  Wellens J, Debrauwere M, Sabino J
  Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics | 2026-10-17
  [10.1016/j.jand.2026.156907](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2026.156907) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Ultra-processed foods (UPFs) and dietary emulsifiers have been linked to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) pathophysiology, but intake in IBD patients compared to healthy controls (HCs) and their association with clinical outcomes remains incompletely understood. OBJECTIVE: We hypothesized that IBD patients consume higher amounts of UPFs and emulsifiers than HCs and this associates with long-term IBD-relevant outcomes including hospitalization, surgery and emergency department (ED) visits in patients. DESIGN: The study has a cross-sectional design comparing dietary intakes between HCs and IBD patients with a longitudinal extension for the IBD cohort only. PARTICIPANTS/SETTING: 186 subjects (65 HC, 59 Crohn's disease (CD) and 62 ulcerative colitis (UC) patients) were recruited and analyzed. For the cross-sectional study, dietary intake was calculated from Food Frequency Questionnaires in all subjects, complemented by two 24-hour dietary recalls in IBD patients and two prospective food records in HC recruited for an interventional study in Leuven, Belgium (November 2022-April 2023). Patients were recruited at the infusion unit of Leuven University Hospital (September 2023-April 2024) and followed up for one year for the longitudinal assessment. Eligible patients were aged 18-65 years, excluding those who had undergone terminal ileostomy or ileo-anal pouch anastomosis, as well as pregnant or breastfeeding women. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURE: We cross-sectionally compared the intake of emulsifiers and UPFs between HCs and IBD patients and longitudinally assessed associations of intake of emulsifiers and UPFs with IBD-related outcomes for IBD patients only. STATISTICAL ANALYSES: Kruskal Wallis with post-hoc Dunn test and Fisher exact test were used to compare UPF and emulsifier intake. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank tests were used to assess the association between nutritional intake and IBD-related outcomes. Adjusted analyses for confounding variables including age, sex, disease activity, energy and fiber intake were performed. RESULTS: While UPF intake (mean ± SD servings) was not different among groups, significantly higher total emulsifier intake was observed in IBD patients, with the lowest consumption in HC (5.64±4.94) followed by UC (8.50±6.13, p=0.003) and highest in CD (9.02±5.28, p=0.0004). After adjustment for confounding emulsifier intake in UC patients (adjusted p=0.002) and CD patients (adjusted p=0.00009) remained higher compared to controls. When stratified by exposure level, Kaplan-Meier estimator curves demonstrated a separation between high- and low-tier consumers, for combined IBD-related adverse related events (p=0.02, HR 2.93 CI 95% 2.34-24.26 for UPFs, and p=0.04, HR 4.85, 95% CI 1.05-22.47 for emulsifiers). In univariate continuous Cox proportional hazards model analysis, number of UPF (p=0.02), but not emulsifier (p=0.08) servings were associated with increased risk of adverse IBD outcomes. However, when modeled in multivariable Cox regression, neither UPF nor emulsifier intake remained a significant independent predictor. CONCLUSIONS: Intake of emulsifiers, but not UPFs, was higher in IBD patients compared to controls. Although unadjusted analyses suggested an association between higher UPF and emulsifier intake and adverse IBD-related outcomes, neither remained an independent predictor after adjustment for potential confounders, yet larger studies are warranted.

  Nearest seed: C-reactive protein/albumin ratio as a potential inflammatory marker for predicting clinical outcomes in patients with moderate to severe ulcerative colitis: A retrospective study.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Fecal occult blood testing is a widely used diagnostic method for the early detection of colorectal cancer and the assessment of gastrointestinal pathologies in humans. For preclinical research purposes, we developed a cost-effective, non-invasive luminol-based chemiluminescent assay to detect fecal occult blood in murine feces that can function as a predictive tool, with sufficient sensitivity to correlate with disease severity. Fecal samples were collected and weighed, solubilized in a sodium hydroxide solution, then combined with luminol and hydrogen peroxide and immediately analyzed using a luminometer. Luminol reacts with the iron in hemoglobin to produce a measurable chemiluminescent signal; therefore, known hemoglobin concentrations from mouse blood, quantified using a hemoglobinometer, were used to generate a standard curve along with feces from naïve mice. The method was validated against standard Guaiac paper slide tests for accuracy. We demonstrated a strong correlation with disease severity metrics, including colonoscopy findings and tumor burden in multiple models of colon cancer, offering an accessible, non-invasive, and inexpensive alternative for routine monitoring of disease progression. The application of this technique can be broadened to other murine models of gastrointestinal pathologies, such as peptic ulcers, inflammatory bowel disease, Crohn's disease, and gastritis.
