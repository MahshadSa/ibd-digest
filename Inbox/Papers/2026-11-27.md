  Nearest seed: Clinical features and outcomes of perianal disease in pediatric-onset Crohn's disease: A comparative analysis according to age at diagnosis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > UNLABELLED: Very early-onset inflammatory bowel disease (VEO-IBD) is characterized by marked clinical heterogeneity, a higher prevalence of monogenic disease, and an increased risk of an aggressive disease course. We evaluated clinical, genetic, and nutritional profiles and long-term outcomes in VEO-IBD and identified predictors of disease severity. This retrospective longitudinal cohort study included 49 children with VEO-IBD followed at a tertiary center between 2015 and 2025. Patients were stratified into infantile-onset (< 2 years) and non-infantile-onset (2-5 years) groups to compare clinical characteristics, nutritional status, biologic-free survival, and predictors of aggressive disease. Median age at diagnosis was 3.25 years (median follow-up: 56.6 months); 57.1% had ulcerative colitis, 32.7% Crohn disease, and 10.2% IBD-unclassified. Genetic testing was performed in 57.1% of patients and identified monogenic disease in 21.4% of those tested. Infantile-onset patients had higher rates of identifiable genetic variants (88.9% vs. 47.4%, p = 0.049) and baseline stunting (33.3% vs. 5.6%, p = 0.047). Biologic-free survival did not differ by onset age (p = 0.719). Crohn disease (OR 11.82, 95% CI 1.32-106.22) and moderate-to-severe baseline disease activity (OR 21.55, 95% CI 2.29-202.70) independently predicted an aggressive course, whereas age at onset and monogenic disease were not independent predictors of an aggressive disease course. CONCLUSION: Infantile-onset VEO-IBD was associated with more genetic abnormalities and baseline stunting. These findings support early molecular genetic evaluation in infants. Long-term disease severity was primarily determined by disease phenotype and baseline disease activity rather than age at onset or monogenic disease. WHAT IS KNOWN: • Very early-onset inflammatory bowel disease is characterized by marked clinical and genetic heterogeneity. • Monogenic disease is more frequently identified in children with very early disease onset. WHAT IS NEW: • Infantile-onset VEO-IBD was associated with a higher frequency of stunting at diagnosis. • Disease phenotype and baseline disease activity, rather than age at onset or monogenic disease, independently predicted an aggressive disease course.

  Nearest seed: Gut Microbiota Are More Strongly Associated With Impairments in Health-Related Quality of Life Than Disease Activity in Inflammatory Bowel Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Diet is a modifiable lifestyle factor of increasing interest in managing Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD). Yet, the influence of diet on inflammation, disease activity and the gut microbiome in IBD remains unclear. OBJECTIVE: This study examined associations between the Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII), clinical disease activity, intestinal inflammation and gut microbiota in Australian adults with IBD and healthy controls (HC). DESIGN: This is a secondary analysis of baseline clinical and dietary data collected from adult participants enrolled in the Australian IBD Microbiome (AIM) Study. PARTICIPANTS: /setting: Participants were aged 18-80 years and recruited into the AIM study July 2019 to September 2023. Participants included 299 adults with IBD (Crohns Disease [CD] = 155, Ulcerative Colitis [UC] = 144) and 243 HCs. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: The DII was computed from 3-day baseline food records. Faecal calprotectin (FCP) and 16S rRNA gene sequencing of the gut microbiota were measured in paired stool samples. Clinical disease activity was assessed using the Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CD) or partial Mayo score (UC). STATISTICAL ANALYSIS: Associations between the DII score categorised into quartiles and disease activity were analysed with the chi-squared test, independent t-test or one-way ANOVA. Associations between DII quartiles and gut microbes were assessed using Linear Discriminant Analysis Effect Sizes, Kruskal-Wallis and Differential Abundance Analysis using Deseq2 package. RESULTS: DII scores ranged from -5.97 (maximally anti-inflammatory) to 7.08 (maximally pro-inflammatory). The mean DII score for the total cohort was 0.17 ± 2.29 (pro-inflammatory) and was not found to be significantly different between CD, UC and HCs. As expected, median FCP was higher in IBD vs HC groups (33.8 [IQR 20.0 - 179]μg/g vs 0.0 [IQR 0.0 - 20.0]μg/g, p<0.001). No significant association was found between DII and clinical disease activity or FCP. An anti-inflammatory diet score was significantly associated with higher bacterial α-diversity scores and enrichment of beneficial genera including Blautia, Intestinimonas, Faecalibacterium, and Eubacterium in both the IBD and HC groups. CONCLUSION: Despite no relationship observed between DII and disease activity in the current study, an anti-inflammatory diet score was associated with enrichment of beneficial gut bacteria.

  > OBJECTIVES: Proximal and terminal ileal lesions are associated with poor prognosis in Crohn's disease (CD) but remain underexplored in pivotal trials because of limited endoscopic accessibility. We evaluated the safety of guselkumab in a Japanese confirmatory safety study and explored endoscopic findings in proximal and terminal ileal lesions assessed by balloon-assisted enteroscopy (BAE). METHODS: This open-label, multicenter confirmatory study enrolled patients with moderately to severely active CD showing inadequate response or intolerance to prior therapy. The primary objective was to assess safety. In a predefined subgroup, proximal and terminal ileal lesions were assessed using BAE. This post hoc exploratory analysis provides descriptive endoscopic observations of proximal and terminal ileal lesions assessed by BAE. The interpretation is limited by the single-arm design and the small number of patients who underwent BAE. These findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in large-scale prospective studies. TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04397263.

  Nearest seed: Correction to: P0562 Comparative Efficacy and Safety of Upadacitinib Versus Oral Prednisolone for the Induction of Remission in Ulcerative Colitis: A Prospective, Multicenter, Open-label, Randomized Controlled Trial (PREDUPA Trial) Protocol and P0563 Mirikizumab Demonstrated Sustained and Durable Long-Term Efficacy and Favorable Safety in Week 52 Endoscopic Responders with Crohn’s disease: 3-year VIVID-2 Open-Label Extension Interim Results

  > [!abstract]-
  > Ulcerative colitis (UC) remains difficult to manage, with many patients failing to achieve durable, steroid-free remission despite available biologics and small molecules. Obefazimod (ABX464) is a first-in-class, oral small molecule that enhances microRNA-124 (miR-124) biogenesis, suppressing STAT3 and CCL2 signaling, reducing Th17 polarization and macrophage activation. This inflammation-dependent mechanism enables targeted immune modulation without broad immunosuppression. In Phase IIa and IIb trials, obefazimod significantly improved clinical remission, clinical response, and endoscopic outcomes versus placebo, with durable efficacy maintained in a 96-week maintenance study. The pivotal Phase III ABTECT-1 and ABTECT-2 trials (n = 1275) confirmed these findings, achieving a pooled placebo-adjusted clinical remission rate of 16.4% at week 8 and meeting all key secondary endpoints, with symptomatic improvement reported as early as week 1. Across studies, obefazimod showed a consistent, reassuring safety profile, with most adverse events mild to moderate, low discontinuation rates, and no new safety signals during long-term exposure. Obefazimod represents a promising addition to the therapeutic landscape for moderate-to-severe UC. Key questions remain regarding its optimal positioning, real-world effectiveness, and potential role in rational combination regimens to further improve remission depth and durability.

  Nearest seed: A scoping review on emerging biomarkers in inflammatory bowel disease: Towards precision medicine in diagnosis and therapeutic management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > The role of the environment in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is acknowledged as being critical, yet enquiry into the exposome and IBD is limited. Recognizing the exposome as encompassing all physical, chemical, biological and psychosocial factors that influence human health over the lifespan is central to unravelling its role in IBD, as well as overlapping immune-mediated diseases. Here, we summarize the latest in exposome research in IBD, differentiate between early life and later exposures, highlight the limitations of traditional epidemiological methods and introduce lesser-explored exposures (for example, food contact contaminants). We discuss innovation in exposomics, such as high-resolution mass spectrometry platforms, integrative statistics and the relevance of microplastics and nanoplastics, a growing pollutant of concern, in IBD. Next, we highlight the prevention framework, emphasizing the relevance of the actionable exposome and differentiating between primary and primordial prevention. Although the former implies risk stratification and prevention in at-risk individuals, primordial prevention focuses on risk mitigation in the general population. We discuss individual and community-level mitigation strategies and global considerations to lower IBD risk. This Perspective provides a path to implement exposomics in IBD prevention, which might also be relevant towards other chronic health outcomes.

- [ ] **Bone tumours with wash-out on dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI: beyond giant cell tumour of bone**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Eimantas Abelkis, Ana Navas Cañete, Kirsten van Langevelde
  Insights into Imaging | 2026-11-27
  [10.1186/s13244-026-02461-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13244-026-02461-8) | Score: 0.91
  Nearest seed: MerMED-FM: Multimodal, Multi-Disease Medical Imaging Foundation Model

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) MRI is a well-known technique to characterise perfusion in bone tumours, yet the diagnostic value of the wash-out phenomenon—rapid peak enhancement followed by a decline in signal intensity—remains underappreciated in musculoskeletal imaging. Wash-out is characteristic of giant cell tumours of bone, and this enhancement pattern may aid both diagnosis and treatment response assessment. This critical review summarises current applications of DCE-MRI in bone tumours, explains its principles, and highlights key findings from recent literature. We based our results on a systematic literature search in PubMed performed in March 2025, using these keywords: (1) magnetic resonance imaging, (2) bone tumour, (3) perfusion/wash-out, and added relevant MeSH terms and synonyms. We identified nineteen different bone tumour entities for which time-intensity curves were shown. Tumours with wash-out were either benign or malignant, including osteosarcoma, multiple myeloma, bone metastases of several primary tumours, and rare neoplasms such as epithelioid haemangioma or Erdheim-Chester disease. Notably, wash-out does not reliably distinguish benign from malignant lesions due to overlapping perfusion characteristics. We highlighted the clinical utility of DCE-MRI in narrowing differential diagnoses, monitoring treatment response, and detecting recurrence, particularly in complex postoperative scenarios. We observed significant heterogeneity in time-intensity curve classification and interpretation between different studies. In summary, wash-out is an advanced MRI feature that occurs across a diverse spectrum of primary and metastatic bone tumours. Greater awareness of wash-out patterns beyond giant cell tumour of bone may serve to enhance diagnostic confidence, especially in challenging cases.
  >
  >
  > Key Points
  >
  >
  > Question
  >
  > We critically reviewed the literature on wash-out (dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) variable) in bone tumours to give an overview of entities demonstrating this perfusion characteristic.
  >
  >
  >
  > Findings
  >
  > Wash-out is not exclusive to giant cell tumour of bone and occurs in both benign and malignant primary bone tumours and in metastases.
  >
  >
  >
  > Critical relevance statement
  >
  > Wash-out on DCE-MRI is a valuable diagnostic feature present in a diverse spectrum of primary bone tumours and bone metastases. Greater awareness of wash-out patterns can enhance diagnostic confidence and help assess treatment response or tumour recurrence in challenging cases.

  > This prospective study aims to evaluate the safety and clinical outcomes of bleomycin electroembolotherapy (BEET) for treating extracranial arteriovenous malformations (AVMs).

  > Materials and methods
