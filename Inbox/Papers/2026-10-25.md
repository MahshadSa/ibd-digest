- [ ] **Fecal microbiota transplant in the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases: new perspectives on delivery, mechanism, and clinical positioning**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Anum Javed, Despoina Chrysostomou, James L Alexander
  Inflammatory Bowel Diseases | 2026-10-24
  [10.1093/ibd/izag219](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag219) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Ulcerative Colitis: Advances in Pathogenesis, Biomarkers, and Therapeutic Strategies.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > The gut microbiota, a complex and dynamic community of microorganisms, has emerged as a crucial determinant of human health and well-being in recent years. This microbial ecosystem plays a pivotal role in various physiological processes, including nutrient metabolism, immune system regulation, and intestinal homeostasis. Disruptions in the composition and function of the gut microbiota have been implicated in a wide range of diseases, such as Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) and inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). The elucidation of the fundamental relationship between human disease and the gut microbiome has positioned fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) as a transformative strategy in modern clinical practice. However, while FMT is increasingly explored for a diverse range of systemic diseases, its clinical implementation is relatively limited beyond the management of recurrent CDI. This review examines the evolving role of FMT within the therapeutic framework of chronic inflammation, focusing specifically on IBD. We compare the variable clinical outcomes in IBD with the highly successful model of recurrent CDI, highlighting mechanistic and clinical determinants of response in a different disease context. Additionally, we evaluate the progression of delivery strategies, from conventional fecal suspensions to standardized oral capsule formulations, and their implications for efficacy, safety, and scalability. Furthermore, we explore the diversifying clinical scenarios where FMT may be incorporated in the treatment paradigm of IBD, including primary treatment of active inflammation, prevention of relapse, and its place in combination with current immunosuppressive therapies.

- [ ] Relevant

  Zheng H, Bu W, Zhuang X
  Omics : a journal of integrative biology | 2026-10-23
  [10.1177/15578100261499489](https://doi.org/10.1177/15578100261499489) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Single-cell insights into drug response and resistance in inflammatory bowel disease: cellular hallmarks and precision therapy

  > [!abstract]-
  > Public mucosal transcriptomic datasets provide an opportunity to examine whether experimental epithelial barrier biology is reflected in human inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) endpoints. We constructed a prespecified barrier-injury transcriptomic score from an inositol polyphosphate multikinase-histone deacetylase 3-matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) barrier-biology gene set and epithelial junctional genes, then evaluated it across 10 public IBD mucosal transcriptomic cohorts (2172 scored biopsies) and an independent prospective adult cohort (GSE193677; 1162 participants). Adult cohorts were analyzed for mucosal healing, clinical remission, or treatment response; pediatric and early onset cohorts were analyzed separately. In UNIFI (GSE206285), a higher baseline score was inversely associated with week 8 mucosal healing (odds ratio [OR]: 0.56, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.45-0.71, p < 0.001). In GSE193677, the score was associated with IBD compared with control status (OR: 1.71, 95% CI: 1.45-2.02) and with endoscopic and histologic activity. The association was directionally consistent across biopsy-selection analyses and exceeded expression- and variance-matched random-gene estimates (empirical p = 0.034), while MMP and inflammatory comparators were at least as strong. Single-cell data localized the MMP module predominantly to stromal cells. These findings support retrospective transcriptomic stratification of mucosal injury states and define a barrier biology-motivated bulk mucosal readout shaped by stromal inflammatory remodeling in human tissue.

- [ ] **Noninfectious skin lesions in Crohn’s disease patients treated with ustekinumab: a multicenter observational retrospective GETAID study**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Gauthier Piga, Aurélien Amiot, Stéphane Nancey
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-10-25
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag177](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag177) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Corticosteroid-sparing effects of risankizumab versus ustekinumab in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease: post hoc results from the phase 3b SEQUENCE trial

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background & Aims
  > Unlike antitumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF)-induced cutaneous adverse events, skin lesions occurring during ustekinumab therapy are uncommon and poorly characterized. We aimed to describe their clinical features, management, and outcomes in patients with Crohn’s disease (CD).
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > We conducted a retrospective multicenter study across 7 Groupe d’Études Thérapeutiques des Affections Inflammatoires Digestives centers between June 2015 and December 2024. Adult CD patients who developed significant noninfectious skin lesions during ustekinumab treatment requiring dermatological assessment were included. Clinical characteristics, dermatological diagnoses, management, and outcomes were collected.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Thirty-two patients (21 women; median age 38 years) developed noninfectious skin lesions. Considering the 740 patients exposed to ustekinumab in our cohort during the study period, the estimated prevalence of such lesions was 4.3% among ustekinumab-exposed patients. A history of atopy or psoriasis was present in 40% of patients, and 65% had previously experienced anti-TNF-induced immune-mediated skin lesions. At lesion onset, 59% had active CD. Psoriasiform (40%) and eczematous (31%) eruptions were most common, followed by acneiform lesions (25%) and 1 case of cutaneous vasculitis. Lesions predominantly involved the face (43%) and trunk (40%). Topical treatments were prescribed in 81% of patients, resulting in improvement in 70% of eczematous, 50% of psoriasiform, and 37% of acneiform lesions. Ustekinumab was discontinued in 8 patients (25%). Prior anti-TNF-induced immune-mediated skin lesions were associated with an increased risk of psoriasiform eruptions under ustekinumab (OR 12.0, 95% CI, 1.3-112.7; P = .03).
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Skin lesions requiring dermatological assessment occurred during ustekinumab treatment and displayed heterogeneous clinical phenotypes. A history of anti-TNF-induced immune-mediated skin reactions may identify patients at increased risk, particularly for psoriasiform eruptions.

- [ ] **Bimekizumab in Chinese Patients with Active Non-Radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis: 52-Week Results from the Phase 3 BE MOBILE 1 Study.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Gu J, Lu L, Xu H
  Rheumatology and therapy | 2026-10-23
  [10.1007/s40744-026-00875-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40744-026-00875-w) | Score: 0.92
  Nearest seed: Corticosteroid-sparing effects of risankizumab versus ustekinumab in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease: post hoc results from the phase 3b SEQUENCE trial

  > [!abstract]-
  > INTRODUCTION: Bimekizumab, a humanised monoclonal IgG1 antibody that selectively inhibits interleukin (IL)-17F in addition to IL-17A, was efficacious and well tolerated to 52 weeks in patients with non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA) in the global phase 3 multicentre study BE MOBILE 1. In this pre-specified analysis, we examine in more detail the efficacy and safety of bimekizumab in the Chinese subpopulation of patients with nr-axSpA from BE MOBILE 1. METHODS: BE MOBILE 1 comprised a 16-week double-blind, placebo-controlled period and 36-week maintenance period. Patients with nr-axSpA were randomised to receive subcutaneous bimekizumab 160 mg every 4 weeks or placebo to week 16; thereafter, all patients received bimekizumab. Here we report efficacy and safety outcomes to week 52 for the Chinese subpopulation. No formal statistical comparisons were made; results should be interpreted as nominal. RESULTS: Overall, 36/274 patients in BE MOBILE 1 were enrolled in China (bimekizumab-randomised 18; placebo-randomised 18); 32 completed week 52. A greater proportion of bimekizumab- vs placebo-randomised patients achieved Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society (ASAS) ≥ 40% response (38.9% vs 22.2%), ASAS partial remission (33.3% vs 11.1%), and Axial Spondyloarthritis Disease Activity Score < 2.1 (77.7% vs 49.7%) at week 16. By week 52, improvements were comparable between bimekizumab-randomised and placebo-randomised patients who had switched to bimekizumab. Similar trends were observed for objective signs of inflammation, spinal pain, physical function, health-related quality of life, and peripheral manifestations. To week 52, treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 33 bimekizumab-treated patients (91.7%), with upper respiratory tract infection the most frequent (10 patients [27.8%]). Four patients (11.1%) had serious TEAEs; four had fungal infections (11.1%; all Tinea). No patients experienced adjudicated inflammatory bowel disease or uveitis. CONCLUSIONS: In patients with nr-axSpA from China, bimekizumab showed rapid and sustained efficacy improvements to week 52 and was well tolerated, consistent with the global patient population.
