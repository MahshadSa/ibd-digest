- [ ] **Microbiota transplant therapy improves ulcerative colitis symptoms independent of donor engraftment in a pilot randomized placebo-controlled trial**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Daphne Moutsoglou, William A Nickols, Byron P Vaughn
  Inflammatory Bowel Diseases | 2026-11-14
  [10.1093/ibd/izag236](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag236) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Ulcerative Colitis: Advances in Pathogenesis, Biomarkers, and Therapeutic Strategies.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Several clinical trials have assessed microbiota transplant therapy (MTT) in ulcerative colitis (UC); however, none have assessed a standardized MTT product. We developed a standardized, oral, lyophilized MTT product and previously reported the primary endpoint of engraftment at week 12. Here, we assessed clinical outcomes and the association with donor engraftment.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial of patients with UC with an endoscopic Mayo score ≥1, who received MTT or placebo for 8 weeks. Symptom and fecal assessments occurred at baseline and weeks 4, 8, and 12. Secondary outcomes included the partial Mayo score (pMS) and correlation to donor engraftment. Stool microbiota were assessed via 16S rRNA sequencing and donor engraftment measured using SourceTracker.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Participants receiving MTT (n = 13) had significantly higher donor engraftment at all measured time points compared with placebo (n = 14). Compared with baseline symptoms, participants who received MTT had significantly lower pMS at weeks 4 and 8, while no significant changes in pMS were noted in those randomized to placebo. There was no correlation between donor engraftment and pMS or clinical response. There was no significant pattern in engrafting donor taxa in those that met the threshold of clinical response at week 4 vs participants that did not meet clinical response.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Daily dosing of encapsulated MTT for 8 weeks reduces symptoms of UC as measured by the pMS. However, donor engraftment did not correlate to pMS or clinical responses.

- [ ] **Serum MMP-10 levels predict incident inflammatory bowel disease: an observational analysis from the PURE study**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Emily C L Wong, Walter Reinisch, Neeraj Narula
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-11-14
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag188](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag188) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: A scoping review on emerging biomarkers in inflammatory bowel disease: Towards precision medicine in diagnosis and therapeutic management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and aims
  > The incidence of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), is rising, particularly in middle-income countries. This study aims to identify biomarkers that predict the onset of IBD in a globally recruited population.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Data from participants enrolled in the biomarker substudy of the Prospective Urban Rural Epidemiology (PURE) study were analyzed (discovery cohort). The primary outcome of interest was the development of incident IBD. Secondary outcomes were CD and UC. Protein concentration was measured using O-link® immuno-assays. Discovery analyses consisted of logistic regression models to evaluate the relationship between biomarker concentrations and risk of IBD. A separate analysis was conducted to validate biomarkers identified from the discovery cohort. Statistical significance was defined using a Bonferroni-corrected P &lt; 1.6 × 10−5 for all analyses.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 1011 biomarkers were assessed in the discovery cohort across 11 019 participants with n = 91 incident IBD cases. In the discovery analysis, elevated levels of matrix metalloproteinase-10 (MMP-10) were significantly associated with a future diagnosis of IBD (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] per SD: 1.75, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.40-2.18, P = 7.6 × 10−7). Results were consistent in direction for development of both UC and CD. In the validation analysis, MMP-10 remained significant for the outcome of IBD (aOR per SD: 2.41 [95% CI: 1.61-4.58], P = 5.2 × 10−6).
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > These findings suggest MMP-10 is a relevant biomarker in the general global population for prediction of clinical IBD, and further work should investigate its role in early detection and prevention strategies.

- [ ] **From standards to implementation: Applying lean thinking to redesign nutritional care in inflammatory bowel disease patients - the Italian PONTE project initiative.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Guercini J, Palazzi S, Lezo A
  Digestive and liver disease : official journal of the Italian Society of Gastroenterology and the Italian Association for the Study of the Liver | 2026-12-01
  [10.1016/j.dld.2026.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dld.2026.10.008) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Updated treatment targets in inflammatory bowel disease (STRIDE-III): an initiative of the International Organization for the Study of IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Disease-related malnutrition is common in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), worsens clinical outcomes and remains inconsistently managed across Italy. The PONTE project aimed to develop a national, standardised pathway for nutritional care in IBD using Lean A3 methodology. A five-member multidisciplinary working group comprising clinical experts from the Italian Group for the Study of Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IG-IBD), the Italian Society of Artificial Nutrition and Metabolism (SINPE) and the Italian Society of Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (SINuC) and two healthcare-management engineers applied Lean A3 methodology through five structured meetings to map current care, identify root causes, develop countermeasures and define performance indicators. The analysis revealed fragmented screening, unclear professional responsibilities and regional disparities in access to nutritional care, with underlying criticalities grouped into three domains: training, organisation and governance. The working group developed a reproducible Lean-based pathway integrating systematic nutritional-risk screening, GLIM-based diagnostic assessment, a designated nutrition referent, graded management and referral to Clinical Nutrition, professional and patient education, digital documentation and performance indicators. PONTE translates quality standards into a practical, measurable framework for nutritional care in IBD, supporting earlier identification of malnutrition, more consistent referral and equitable access to nutritional services while providing a basis for prospective implementation and evaluation.

  Nearest seed: Genetics of Inflammatory Bowel Disease-What is Unsought Goes Undetected.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Somatic mutations are increasingly implicated in persistent intestinal inflammation in chronic enteropathies. This review examines how mutation-bearing immune and epithelial clones may emerge, expand and contribute to disease within the chronically inflamed gut. We focus on refractory coeliac disease, highlighting the recent evidence for lymphoma-associated mutations in expanded intraepithelial lymphocyte clones in both type I and type II disease. We then consider emerging evidence in other enteropathy, including autoimmune enteropathy, common carriable immunodeficiency-associated enteropathy and inflammatory bowel disease. Finally, we discuss how inflammation shapes clonal selection in the intestine, approaches for detecting low-frequency somatic variants, and the potential implications of mutation profiling for diagnosis, risk stratification and future therapeutic strategies.

- [ ] **Mitochondria-targeted phosphorescent iridium (III) probes for turn-on imaging of selenocysteine dynamics during inflammatory bowel disease.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Chen S, Chen M, Niu Z
  Spectrochimica acta. Part A, Molecular and biomolecular spectroscopy | 2027-02-15
  [10.1016/j.saa.2026.128930](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.saa.2026.128930) | Score: 0.93
  Nearest seed: A Metal-Free Nitroxide Radical Nanoliposome for MRI-Guided Therapy of Ulcerative Colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Selenocysteine (Sec), the 21st genetically encoded amino acid, plays a crucial role in cellular redox regulation and selenium metabolism. Abnormal Sec fluctuations are closely associated with inflammatory oxidative stress and the progression of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). However, efficient tools for real-time visualization of Sec fluctuations in living systems remain highly limited. Herein, two novel mitochondria-targeted iridium(III) complex probes, Irppy-DNP and Irpiq-DNP, were successfully designed and developed based on a thiolysis-triggered phosphorescence activation strategy for Sec-responsive "turn-on" sensing. Upon reaction with Sec to generate the corresponding hydroxylated products (Irppy-OH and Irpiq-OH), the two probes exhibited distinct photophysical responses, characterized by increased photoluminescence quantum yields (2.3 → 34.3% and 1.3 → 26.9%), prolonged excited-state lifetimes (0.23 → 0.42 μs and 0.62 → 0.70 μs), and reduced nonradiative decay rates (knr) (4.25 × 106 → 1.56 × 106 s-1 and 1.59 × 106 → 1.04 × 106 s-1), respectively. In addition, the two probes exhibited excellent analytical performance, including favorable photostability (60 min), fast response time (15/20 min), low detection limit (0.79/0.70 nM), excellent selectivity and anti-interference capability, low cytotoxicity (>70% cell viability), and visualized endogenous and exogenous Sec in living cells. Especially, the probes showed high selectivity toward Sec over Cys, Hcy, and GSH, benefiting from the greater nucleophilicity of Sec-derived selenolate at physiological pH (7.4) and its efficient thiolysis of the dinitrophenyl ether moiety. Given the key role of Sec-mediated redox regulation in IBD-associated mitochondrial ROS elevation, mitochondrial targeting is essential for monitoring pathological processes, with both probes exhibiting efficient organelle localization (PCC = 0.783/0.803). Notably, Irpiq-DNP enabled longitudinal imaging of Sec fluctuations in dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis mice, where the luminescence intensity closely correlated with disease progression as well as therapeutic response to 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) treatment. This work provides a promising phosphorescent platform for monitoring Sec fluctuations during inflammatory processes and therapeutic intervention in IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Objectives
  > To evaluate whether automated carotid plaque quantification on carotid computed tomography angiography (CTA) can serve as an adjunctive risk stratification tool for identifying patients with coexisting obstructive coronary artery disease (OCAD).
  >
  >
  > Materials and methods
  > This dual-center retrospective study included 2533 adults undergoing paired carotid and coronary CTA within 3 months. Carotid plaque components (calcified, fibrotic, and lipid) were quantified automatically. In the modeling cohort, three diagnostic models for OCAD (stenosis ≥ 50%) were developed using LASSO regression: clinical, plaque, and integrated models. Performance was assessed by AUC (with DeLong’s test), decision curve analysis, and calibration curves, with validation in an internal validation set and a temporally independent validation cohort. Sensitivity analysis was performed using severe stenosis (≥ 70%) as the outcome.
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > All carotid plaque volumes were significantly associated with OCAD (
  > p
  > < 0.001). Calcified plaque volume (OR, 2.43; 95% CI: 1.95–3.04) and fibrotic plaque volume (OR, 1.37; 95% CI: 1.01–1.85) were independent predictors. The plaque model achieved an AUC of 0.785 (95% CI: 0.761–0.810) in the training set, comparable to the integrated model (AUC, 0.800) and superior to the clinical model (AUC, 0.723;
  > p
  > < 0.001). Performance remained robust in the internal (AUC, 0.824) and external validation set (AUC, 0.813). The integrated model demonstrated good calibration. Sensitivity analysis yielded consistent results.
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > In this retrospective study, automated carotid plaque quantification on CTA provides an adjunctive risk stratification tool to identify patients at high risk for coexisting OCAD, potentially guiding decisions regarding downstream coronary examinations.
