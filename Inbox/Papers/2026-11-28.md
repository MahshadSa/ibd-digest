- [ ] **Comparative efficacy of advanced therapies in biological-exposed ulcerative colitis - a network meta-analysis of randomized trials.**
- [ ] Relevant

  Giri S, Kulkarni A, Nath P
  European journal of gastroenterology & hepatology | 2027-01-01
  [10.1097/meg.0000000000003240](https://doi.org/10.1097/meg.0000000000003240) | Score: 0.98
  Nearest seed: Comparing real-world continuous clinical remission rates of anti-tumor necrosis factor, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, tofacitinib, and upadacitinib in ulcerative colitis

  > [!abstract]-
  > Patients with ulcerative colitis who fail biological therapy represent a challenging population with limited comparative evidence to guide the sequencing of advanced treatments. The present network meta-analysis aimed to compare the efficacy of biologics and small molecules for the induction and maintenance of remission in biological-exposed ulcerative colitis. We conducted a systematic review of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) from MEDLINE, Embase, and Scopus enrolling adults with moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis and prior exposure or failure to biologicals from inception to March 2025. Outcomes included induction and maintenance of clinical remission. Frequentist and Bayesian random-effects models were applied, and comparative efficacy was ranked using surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) values. Twenty-one RCTs (n = 5417 induction; n = 3076 maintenance) were included. For induction, most advanced therapies outperformed placebo, though adalimumab, vedolizumab, ozanimod, and mirikizumab did not reach statistical significance. Upadacitinib ranked highest for induction (SUCRA: 89.3), followed by tofacitinib (88.5) and ustekinumab (77.5). For maintenance, all evaluated agents were superior to placebo. Upadacitinib ranked highest (SUCRA: 85.9), followed by vedolizumab (73.6) and etrasimod (71.8). Subgroup analysis of re-randomized studies confirmed the superiority of upadacitinib and vedolizumab. Certainty of evidence was generally low due to imprecision and publication bias. In biologic-experienced ulcerative colitis, upadacitinib demonstrated the highest efficacy for both induction and maintenance of remission, with tofacitinib and ustekinumab effective alternatives for induction, and vedolizumab a strong option for long-term maintenance. Further head-to-head trials in biologic-experienced populations are warranted.

  Becherucci G, Barberio B, Godny L
  Minerva gastroenterology | 2026-11-27
  [10.23736/s2724-5985.26.04290-7](https://doi.org/10.23736/s2724-5985.26.04290-7) | Score: 0.98
  Nearest seed: Multiple paths to health: A scoping review of dietary interventions for adults with Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Diet is a key therapeutic target in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet responses vary widely between patients. We distinguish conventional dietary therapy, applied by clinical indication; stratified nutrition, selected on predefined clinical or biological features such as phenotype or disease location; and precision nutrition, which uses individual-level clinical, biological, microbial, metabolic and digital data to predict response and adapt dietary interventions. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and Scopus were searched from 2010 to June 2026, combining IBD terms with dietary, microbiome, multi-omics, artificial intelligence (AI) and digital health terms; only English-language articles were included. Priority was given to randomized trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and landmark studies, defined as those first establishing a therapeutic principle or biological association subsequently confirmed, appraised for design, sample size, risk of bias and consistency with the literature. Exclusive enteral nutrition and the Crohn's disease exclusion diet have the strongest randomized support for inducing remission in Crohn's disease; the Mediterranean diet is best supported for maintenance, and the low-FODMAP diet improves persistent gut symptoms without changing inflammatory markers. Phenotype, disease location, microbiota, genetics, immune pathways, and environment underlie this heterogeneity, which multi-omics, AI and digital health may capture in predictive models, though most such evidence comes from non-IBD populations. Preliminary, uncontrolled studies suggest that combining diet with advanced therapies may improve selected outcomes, but synergy has not been demonstrated. Precision nutrition is a promising paradigm for personalized IBD management, but IBD-specific prospective trials, validated multimodal biomarkers and multidisciplinary implementation are required before routine use.

- [ ] **Safety and accuracy diverge across deployment conditions in clinical large language models**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Sebastian Wind, Tri-Thien Nguyen, Soroosh Tayebi Arasteh
  npj Digital Medicine | 2026-11-27
  [10.1038/s41746-026-03455-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03455-y) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Physicians and artificial intelligence diverge in evaluating large language models on real clinical cases.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Clinical large language models (LLMs) are commonly scaled by increasing model size, context length, retrieval complexity, or inference-time compute, on the assumption that higher accuracy implies safer behavior. In medicine, where a few confident or evidence-contradicting errors can outweigh average performance, this assumption may be incomplete. We introduce Safety-Focused Evaluation of Scaling (SaFE-Scale) and instantiate it on RadSaFE-200, a 200-question text-based radiology multiple-choice benchmark with clinician-defined clean evidence, conflict evidence, and option-level labels for high-risk error, unsafe answer, and evidence contradiction. Thirty-four local LLMs were evaluated across six deployment conditions spanning closed-book, evidence-grounded, standard and agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and max-context prompting. Clean clinician-written evidence, used as an upper-bound, produced the strongest joint gain, raising mean accuracy from 73.5% to 94.1% and reducing high-risk error from 12.0% to 2.6% and dangerous overconfidence from 8.0% to 1.6%. Standard and agentic RAG, max-context prompting, and inference-time compute improved selected metrics but did not reproduce this safety profile. Clinically consequential errors concentrated in a small subset of questions, and variance decomposition placed deployment condition above model family on every safety metric. Within this evaluation, clinical LLM safety did not track accuracy scaling but emerged as a deployment-level property.

  Nearest seed: Impact of Prior Biologic Mechanism of Action on Anti‐ TNF Effectiveness in Inflammatory Bowel Disease: An ENEIDA Registry Study

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Despite recent progress in advanced therapies, treatment with an anti-tumour necrosis factor (anti-TNF) drug remains a cornerstone of therapy in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). However, 13%-40% of patients experience primary non-response (PNR) and around 50% a secondary loss of response (LOR). No predictors of treatment response have yet been sufficient for clinical use, often due to small cohort sizes. AIMS: In this study, we aimed to analyse associations with and predictors of response to anti-TNF therapy in patients with IBD utilizing Danish nationwide registry-based data. METHODS: All biologic-naïve patients with IBD in Denmark who completed infliximab or adalimumab induction were included, totalling 14,072 patients. Events considered indicative of PNR or LOR were prescription of systemic corticosteroids, major IBD-related surgery or hospitalization, unsatisfactory reduction in CRP, intensification of treatment, or a different biologic. Associations tested included demographics, biochemical samples, concurrent medication, severity and behaviour of IBD. RESULTS: In total, 19.0% of patients experienced PNR and 43.2% LOR. We found an increased hazard of LOR with longer disease duration before treatment, a protective effect of immunomodulators during induction, and associations with baseline biochemical samples. Finally, we present moderately predictive models of PNR to infliximab (mean AUC 0.71) and adalimumab (mean AUC 0.66). CONCLUSIONS: In this population-based cohort of over 14,000 patients with IBD, we present novel baseline biochemical associations with response to anti-TNFs, prediction models for PNR to infliximab and adalimumab, and show findings in support of the shift from a bottom-up to a top-down approach in treating IBD.

  Nearest seed: Clinical Course and Predictors of Biologic Therapy, Hospitalisation and Surgery: A Five‐Year Prospective Study in Newly Diagnosed Inflammatory Bowel Disease

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Introduction
  > Symptoms of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) often evolve over 5 years before diagnosis, yet little is known about pain and sedative medication use during this period. We aimed to characterise prescribing trajectories before and after IBD diagnosis and examine associations of prescribing with diagnostic delays.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Using UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink‐GOLD, we identified adults with IBD diagnosed between 2010 and 2021. Among those with 5 years' follow‐up before and after diagnosis, we assessed prescribing prevalences of opioids, gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, benzodiazepines, and Z‐drugs. Among those with 5 years' follow‐up before diagnosis, Cox regression examined associations between prescribing and time from first gastrointestinal symptom to diagnosis. Time‐to‐discontinuation of long‐term prescriptions after diagnosis was examined in those with follow‐up 1 year before diagnosis and 5 years after.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Among 4433 adults with IBD, the proportion prescribed pain or sedative medications doubled from 8% to 16% during the 5 years before diagnosis and remained high (19% to 21%) throughout the subsequent 5 years. In a second cohort of 8848 individuals, median time from first gastrointestinal symptom to diagnosis was 13 months longer for UC and 14 months longer for CD among those prescribed pain or sedative medications than those who were not. Median time to discontinuation of long‐term prescriptions after diagnosis was 4–8 months.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Pain and sedative prescribing is common before and after IBD diagnosis and is associated with a longer time to diagnosis. Timely investigation for IBD should be considered in patients receiving these medications who present with suggestive symptoms.

  Journal of Magnetic Resonance Imaging | 2026-11-28
  [10.1002/jmri.70636](https://doi.org/10.1002/jmri.70636) | Score: 0.92
  Nearest seed: Quantitative mapping from conventional MRI using self-supervised physics-guided deep learning: Applications to a large-scale, clinically heterogeneous dataset

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  > Phase‐resolved functional lung (PREFUL) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides spatially resolved assessments of pulmonary ventilation and perfusion‐related function from free‐breathing proton MRI, without administering contrast agents or inhaled tracer gases. Interpreting these assessments requires a clear distinction between the measured signal and the physiological quantities inferred from it. This narrative review follows the measurement chain from pulmonary signal formation and sequence selection through acquisition, reconstruction, respiratory and cardiac phase assignment, image registration, biomarker derivation, and clinical interpretation. We examine ventilation‐related signal changes, regional flow–volume loop metrics, perfusion weighting, model‐dependent perfusion quantification, and threshold‐defined defect percentages. Retrospectively reconstructed physiological cycles combine observations obtained during different breaths or heartbeats; their smooth appearance does not guarantee physiological fidelity. Moreover, regional signal modulation is not a direct measure of airflow, and cardiac weighting is not equivalent to absolute blood flow. Evidence from test–retest studies, multicenter comparisons, and comparisons with hyperpolarized xenon MRI, dynamic contrast‐enhanced MRI, and nuclear medicine methods demonstrates useful regional information but also biomarker‐specific limits of agreement and reproducibility. We review applications in obstructive, vascular, interstitial, transplant‐related, and pediatric lung disease, illustrated by eight clinical cases. Volumetric and ultrashort echo‐time acquisitions expand opportunities for regional ventilation mapping, although their validation does not automatically extend to cardiac‐derived perfusion. Processing choices—including respiratory sorting, registration, segmentation, normalization, and defect thresholds—can alter reported values and their interpretation. PREFUL MRI thus provides a framework for deriving regional functional estimates, not an acquisition‐independent physiological standard. Reliable translation requires harmonized protocols, transparent reporting of processing decisions, and validation of individual biomarkers against appropriate physiological and clinical endpoints.
