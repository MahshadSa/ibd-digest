  Nearest seed: Review Article: Beyond the Therapeutic Ceiling in Inflammatory Bowel Disease—Mechanism‐Based Positioning and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and Aims
  > Innate and adaptive immune cell dysregulation contributes to ulcerative colitis pathogenesis. The sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulator etrasimod showed efficacy in ulcerative colitis treatment in Phase 3 ELEVATE UC studies. Changes in circulating immune cells and mucosal cell types were characterized in etrasimod-treated patients with ulcerative colitis.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Blood (etrasimod n = 525, placebo n = 257 patients) and mucosal biopsies (etrasimod n = 411, placebo n = 200 patients) from patients in the ELEVATE UC trials were used to assess cellular and molecular changes for etrasimod versus placebo in clinical remitters and non-remitters using immunophenotyping, transcriptomic and proteomic techniques.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Selective and partial reduction in circulating T- and B-cell subsets was observed for etrasimod versus placebo (Week 12), with greater effects on CD4+ T cells, including Treg and Th17 cells, than CD8+ T cells, and on naive T cells and T central memory cells than T effector memory cells, without difference between remitters and non-remitters. No significant effects on monocytes nor NK cell numbers were observed. Mucosal biopsies showed corresponding reductions in T- and B-cell subsets, myeloid cells and neutrophils accompanied by downregulation of interleukin-17 and interleukin-8 signaling in both tissue and blood, most prominently in remitters, where non-immune cell changes were consistent with tissue repair.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Etrasimod treatment of patients with ulcerative colitis drives T- and B-lymphocyte subset reductions in blood and intestinal mucosa, neutrophil reductions in tissue and mucosal downregulation of inflammatory pathways associated with ulcerative colitis pathogenesis.
  >
  >
  > Clinical trials registration number
  > ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03945188; NCT03996369).

  Nearest seed: Review Article: Beyond the Therapeutic Ceiling in Inflammatory Bowel Disease—Mechanism‐Based Positioning and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND/AIMS: Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs), including Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), show variable treatment responses. Data on the prevalence and clinical predictors of difficult-to-treat IBD (DTT-IBD) among Asians receiving advanced therapies (ATs) remain limited. This study evaluated the proportion of DTT-IBD and its risk factors in a Korean IBD cohort. METHODS: We conducted a retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained tertiary-care cohort including patients with UC or CD who received at least one AT. Kaplan-Meier analysis, log-rank tests, and Cox proportional hazards regression were used to identify factors associated with DTT-IBD. DTT-IBD was defined as multidrug failure (MF; failure after ≥2 ATs with different mechanisms) or multiple resections (MR; recurrence after ≥2 intestinal resections in CD patients). RESULTS: Among 512 patients, 130 (25.4%) met the MF DTT-IBD criteria. The cumulative probability of MF increased from 5.3% at 1 year to 51.1% at 10 years in UC patients, and from 0.4% to 37.1% in CD patients. Starting ATs within 2 years of UC diagnosis and older age increased the risk of MF DTT-IBD. In CD patients, risk factors included older age and vedolizumab as first-line AT. Among CD patients undergoing a second surgery, the cumulative risk of MR DTT-IBD reached approximately 50% at 1 year, 86.4% at 5 years, and >93% at 10 years. CONCLUSIONS: MF DTT-IBD occurred in one-quarter of patients. Timing of AT initiation and age in UC and therapy type and age in CD patients were key predictors. High MR DTT-IBD risk in CD patients underscores the need for proactive monitoring and early strategies.

  Inflammation research : official journal of the European Histamine Research Society ... [et al.] | 2026-10-12
  [10.1007/s00011-026-02390-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00011-026-02390-8) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Intestinal cancer risk in Crohn’s disease: authors’ reply on methods, data integrity, and clinical implications

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVE AND DESIGN: This narrative review examines epidemiological, genetic, mechanistic, microbial, and therapeutic evidence concerning inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and breast cancer. METHODS: Relevant literature was identified through targeted PubMed searches completed through May 2026 and reference-list screening. Evidence was selected for its relevance to epidemiology, candidate mechanisms, microbiome-related pathways, and treatment interactions. RESULTS: Pooled cohort evidence does not show a significant overall increase in breast cancer risk among patients with IBD. Several inflammatory, stromal, microbial, and endocrine pathways are relevant to both diseases, although most evidence comes from separate study populations and experimental models. Studies of patients with prior malignancy have not shown a clear increase in new or recurrent cancer during commonly used IBD therapies, but few analyses have focused specifically on breast cancer. Microbiome-directed treatments have been studied in IBD and, more recently, in oncology; their value for patients with both diseases is unknown. CONCLUSIONS: Current evidence does not support classifying IBD as a breast cancer risk state. The main clinical concern is the interaction between treatments for the two diseases, which requires individualized assessment and coordinated gastroenterology and oncology care.

  > Post-translational modification(PTM) is the terminal regulatory step of gene expression, directly regulating protein function through reversible or irreversible chemical modifications, enabling cells to rapidly respond to internal and external environmental stimuli. Among various post-translational modifications (PTMs), the dynamic balance between phosphorylation and dephosphorylation constitutes the molecular switch for inflammatory signal transduction, and disruption of this balance is an important cause of the persistent non-healing of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).   > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Background and Aim
  > We hypothesized that duodenal polyps (DP) and Barrett's oesophagus (BE) coincide in the same patients and that biliary disease, such as cholelithiasis (GS) or cholecystectomy (CCx), increases the risk for both DP and BE. We used a case–control study design to test our hypothesis.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Using the healthcare records of the University of Pennsylvania Health System (UPHS), we compared the frequency of GS or CCx in patients with DP or BE with their corresponding frequencies in a control population of patients without DP or BE. Odds ratios (OR) and their 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were calculated using multivariate logistic regression analysis.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > The UPHS electronic database from 1/2016 until 3/2026 contained a total of 175,808 unique patients with EGD or colonoscopy, of whom 3632 underwent a CCx, 11,122 had GS, 669 had DP and 4965 had BE. The OR (95% CI) values for the association between BE and DP were 4.64 (3.63–5.86). The OR values for the GS association with DP were 2.23 (1.78–2.76) and for the CCx association with DP 2.26 (1.54–3.20). The OR values for the association between GS and BE were 1.46 (1.32–1.60) and for the CCx association with BE 1.57 (1.33–1.84).
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Cholelithiasis and cholecystectomy are associated with an increased risk for duodenal polyps and Barrett's oesophagus. In support of this finding, duodenal polyps and Barrett's oesophagus are also found to coincide in the same patients.

- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Josef Urrete, Margaret Kim, Jesús Rivera-Nieves
  Inflammatory Bowel Diseases | 2026-10-12
  [10.1093/ibd/izag198](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag198) | Score: 0.94
  Nearest seed: Trained immunity: An unexplored component of IBD pathogenesis

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Loss of immune tolerance to commensal microbial antigens is a central driver of inflammatory bowel disease. While regulatory T cells are well recognized in maintaining intestinal homeostasis, the contribution of B cells—particularly IgA-secreting cells—remains incompletely defined. In the T cell transfer colitis model, cotransfer of splenic B cells attenuates disease, an effect largely attributed to IL-10–producing regulatory B cells (Bregs). However, alternative B cell–dependent mechanisms have not been fully explored. Here, we investigated whether splenic B cells regulate T cell transfer colitis through IgA-dependent mechanisms independent of Breg-associated cytokines. We show that splenic B cells from wild-type C57BL/6 (B6) mice reconstitute bone marrow and peripheral B cell compartments in RAG1⁻/⁻ recipients, undergo class switching, migrate to the colonic lamina propria as sIgA⁺ B lineage cells, and partially restore luminal secretory IgA. B cell cotransfer significantly attenuated clinical and histological colitis without increased expression of canonical Breg-associated cytokines, including interleukin-10, transforming growth factor β, and interleukin-35 subunits. In contrast, splenic B cells from IgA-deficient mice failed to attenuate colitis despite comparable peripheral B cell reconstitution. Restoration of secretory IgA was associated with increased bacterial IgA coating, reduced fecal bacterial burden, and remodeling of the colonic microbiota, including enrichment of beneficial commensal taxa. Importantly, because IgA-deficient B cells failed to attenuate colitis despite comparable peripheral B cell reconstitution, these findings support an important role for IgA in mediating the protective effect of B cell transfer. Collectively, these findings identify IgA-mediated modulation of the microbiota as an alternate mechanism by which B cells attenuate T cell transfer colitis, highlighting a Breg-independent regulatory pathway with potential relevance to inflammatory bowel disease pathogenesis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > The increased utility of quantitative metrics in medical imaging has prompted the use of colour representation of the data. Colour schemes for image display can highlight pathology and distinguish normal anatomy, thus potentially aiding image interpretation. Adding colour schemes to images may serve the sole purpose of visualising an abnormality or may seek to represent and meaningfully convey a quantitative metric. An early use of colour representation is exemplified by colour Doppler in ultrasound (US), where a two-sided linear scale is commonly applied. More recently, many schemes have arisen, ranging from a single colour to depict a segmented region to a range of colours that embrace the entire colour spectrum. These may be applied as continuous or discrete palettes, depending on the software vendor or even on individual user settings. The lack of standardisation of colour schemes across the medical community, especially in medical imaging, risks misinterpretation of quantitative data and potential unintended negative clinical consequences. The purpose of this article is to review the colour schemes used for quantitative imaging across US, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and positron emission tomography (PET), the way in which they are applied, the clinical applications where their use is desirable for enhanced interpretation, and the pitfalls where standardisation is lacking in a variety of clinical applications. Finally, we discuss some solutions that could be employed to enable standardisation and augment the use of colour in image interpretation.
  >
  >
  > Key Points
  >
  >
  > Question
  >
  > Colour schemes in medical imaging can highlight pathology, but standardisation is lacking for most imaging modalities, thus risking misinterpretation of quantitative data.
  >
  >
  >
  > Findings
  >
  > For T1, T2, and T2* maps and their inverses, colour map recommendations have recently been proposed, but are mostly lacking for other quantitative data.
  >
  >
  >
  > Critical relevance statement
  >
  > Standardised colour maps can enhance data analyses across scanners and institutions, which is important for multicentre studies and handling big data. Furthermore, standardised and harmonised colour maps could improve lesion conspicuity, cross-platform comparability, and longitudinal reliability.
