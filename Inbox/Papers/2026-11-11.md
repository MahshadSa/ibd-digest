  Nearest seed: Ultrasound Features Associated with Activity of Perianal Fistulas in Pediatric Patients with Crohn's Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: To evaluate and compare the multiparametric transperineal ultrasound (TPUS) features of perianal fistulas at baseline and upon achieving magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-confirmed radiologic healing in pediatric Crohn disease. METHODS: This retrospective study included 24 children (22 boys and 2 girls; mean age, 13.0 ± 2.3 years [range, 9-19]) with 45 perianal fistulas evaluated between January 2019 and July 2025. All patients with fistulas who underwent at least 2 TPUS and pelvic MRI assessments and achieved complete healing on MRI were enrolled. Baseline and follow-up TPUS images were evaluated in consensus by 2 radiologists for multiple parameters, including diameter, echogenicity relative to the internal anal sphincter (IAS), vascularity, internal pattern (layered versus homogeneous), anisotropy, presence of pus, air bubbles, abscesses, and adjacent inflammation. Changes in these findings were compared using paired t-tests and McNemar tests. RESULTS: At baseline (n = 45), most fistulas demonstrated higher echogenicity than that of the IAS (73%), exhibited a layered pattern (80%) and internal vascularity (94%) and frequently showed pus (40%) and adjacent inflammation (78%). Despite achieving complete MRI-defined radiologic healing, perianal fistulas remained detectable by TPUS in the majority of cases (60%, 27/45), while the other 18 fistulas (40%) were no longer detectable. However, the 27 persisting fistulas demonstrated significant multiparametric evolution, including reductions in diameter (p < .001), loss of the layered pattern (p < .001), increased anisotropy (p < .001), decreased vascularity (p = .002), and resolution of pus (p = .001) and adjacent inflammation (p < .001). CONCLUSION: Radiologic healing in pediatric perianal Crohn disease is characterized by distinct multiparametric TPUS changes, including diameter reduction, loss of layered pattern, emergence of anisotropy, and resolution of inflammatory markers. While TPUS serves as a valuable adjunct to MRI for assessing treatment response, the persistent sonographic detectability of fistulas despite MRI-defined healing warrants further validation in larger cohorts and long-term follow-up studies.

  Nearest seed: Biologics, small molecules, and extraintestinal malignancies in inflammatory bowel disease: practical questions and evidence-based answers

  > [!abstract]-
  > Despite major therapeutic advances, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains incurable, and many patients experience primary non-response, secondary loss of response, progressive bowel damage, or lifelong dependence on immunomodulatory therapy. Cellular immunotherapies offer the possibility of moving beyond chronic immune suppression toward durable immune reprogramming. Earlier approaches in IBD, including hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, mesenchymal stem cells, and regulatory T-cell-based therapies, have provided important proof-of-concept but have been limited by toxicity, inconsistent efficacy, local rather than systemic effects, or unresolved manufacturing and durability challenges. Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy represents the next-generation strategy that combines potent immune remodeling with antigen-specific targeting. Initially developed for hematological malignancies, CAR T cells have recently shown striking efficacy in refractory autoimmune diseases, particularly through CD19-mediated depletion of B-lineage cells and induction of drug-free remission. In ulcerative colitis, renewed interest in B cells has been driven by evidence of plasmablast expansion, IgG-skewed humoral responses, anti-integrin αvβ6 autoantibodies, and the limitations of conventional anti-CD20 depletion. The first reported case of multidrug-resistant ulcerative colitis treated with CD19 CAR T cells demonstrated rapid clinical, biochemical, endoscopic, and histological remission, providing proof-of-concept for deep B-cell targeting in IBD. However, major uncertainties remain, including long-term durability, relapse after B-cell reconstitution, safety, cost, feasibility, and the risk of CAR T-associated gastrointestinal toxicity. Prospective trials with mechanistic mucosal monitoring are now needed to define the therapeutic potential and appropriate positioning of CAR-based strategies in IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Patients' psychological responses to artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare depend on the functions it performs and how they interpret its assistance. This review examines how four forms of AI may engage different psychological mechanisms across physical, mental, and social health. Generative AI may facilitate cognitive offloading and foster perceived responsiveness, but risks leaving patients vulnerable to unsound medical advice or emotional isolation. Predictive AI engages risk appraisal and can validate or destabilize patients' health beliefs, while anticipated stigma may paradoxically discourage engagement with predictive AI. We argue that the psychological viability of medical AI hinges not merely on technical performance, but on how assistance is interpreted, and we outline critical directions for future research.

Predatory journals exploit this environment by sending unsolicited invitations to publish or join editorial boards, frequently using flattering language and promising unrealistic publication timelines.
