- [ ] **Pharmacological TRPM8 blockade reprograms patient-derived intestinal fibroblasts and mitigates experimental fibrosis through modulation of myofibroblast activation.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Nanì MF, Rinaldi MM, Izzo AA
  Pharmacological research | 2026-10-20
  [10.1016/j.phrs.2026.109701](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phrs.2026.109701) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Intestinal Fibrosis in IBD: Rethinking the Inflammatory Paradigm and Emerging Therapeutic Opportunities.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Intestinal fibrosis is a major long-term complication mostly prevalent in Crohn's disease (CD) and remains without approved antifibrotic therapies. Transient receptor potential melastatin 8 (TRPM8) is a pharmacologically targetable ion channel involved in inflammatory regulation, but its contribution to intestinal fibrotic remodeling is unknown. This study investigated TRPM8 association with human stenotic CD and whether pharmacological TRPM8 modulation limits fibroblast activation and experimental intestinal fibrosis. TRPM8 expression was increased in stenotic ileocolonic tissues from CD patients and in primary patient-derived intestinal fibroblasts. In these cells, the TRPM8 antagonist AMTB reduced proliferation and migration at non-cytotoxic concentration and attenuated the expression of fibroblast activation, matrix and myofibroblast-associated markers. In mice with chronic 2,4,6-trinitrobenzenesulfonic acid (TNBS)-induced intestinal fibrosis, AMTB reduced disease activity and endoscopic severity and attenuated stromal remodeling-associated gene expression. Histological analyses further showed reduced tissue injury and collagen remodeling, supported by Masson's Trichrome staining and quantitative analysis of Picrosirius Red. In contrast, pharmacological TRPM8 activation with WS12 did not significantly modify fibroblast proliferation in vitro or the evaluated clinical, endoscopic and molecular readouts in vivo. AMTB also reduced the TNBS-induced T helper 17 response without reducing interleukin-23 production by colonic mononuclear phagocytes. Finally, fibroblasts isolated from AMTB-treated fibrotic mice displayed reduced expansion, lower collagenogenic gene expression, reduced contractile/activated fibroblast markers and decreased actin cytoskeletal remodeling ex vivo. Overall, these findings identify TRPM8 as a druggable regulator of fibroblast-driven intestinal remodeling and support TRPM8 antagonism as a candidate antifibrotic strategy for stricturing CD.

  Nearest seed: Novel biomarkers and multi-omics approaches for diagnosis and management of ulcerative colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > With modest rates of endoscopic remission to anti-TNF biologics in children with moderate-to-severe Crohn’s disease (CD), there is a critical need to develop noninvasive biomarkers associated with anti-TNF response. We performed a comprehensive proteomic evaluation to identify peripheral-blood candidate biomarkers for anti-TNF response.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > The ENvISION study enrolled patients (2-19 years old) with CD prior to starting infliximab or adalimumab with outcomes prospectively monitored at Month 3 (M3) and Month 12 (M12). The SomaScan® (Somalogic, Boulder, CO, 7322 analytes) was performed prior to anti-TNF treatment (baseline) at M3 and M12. Endoscopic remission was assessed at M12 (simple endoscopic score-CD &lt; 3).
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Eighty patients with CD were enrolled with the CD Core Proteome developed from 61 prednisone-naive CD and 30 healthy controls. We identified 2999 differentially abundant proteins, 1403 with lower abundance and 1596 with higher abundance (FDR &lt; 0.05). The CD Core Proteome was then compared across the three timepoints (baseline, M3 and M12) and we found that at both M3 and M12, the Core Proteome dysregulation retains strong identity with the baseline protein signal (β = .7, P &lt; .001; β = .63, P &lt; .001, respectively). Although the proteome exhibits change over time, it does not revert to a control-like state; rather, it transitions to a distinct “treated CD” proteome. In an exploratory investigation of the change in (delta) protein abundance from baseline to M3, we identified 132 proteins nominally associated with M12 endoscopic remission (P &lt; .01). Using elastic net regression, we identified a three-protein panel (IRF1, NEUROG1, and APOA5) associated with M12 endoscopic remission (AUROC 0.94, 95% CI 0.88-1.0).
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > In an exploratory investigation, we identified a CD Core Proteome and three-protein panel whose early changes were associated with M12 endoscopic remission, supporting their use as early pharmacodynamic response markers following external validation.

  Advances in clinical and experimental medicine : official organ Wroclaw Medical University | 2026-10-20
  [10.17219/acem/225186](https://doi.org/10.17219/acem/225186) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Comparative performance of intestinal ultrasound activity indices in pediatric ulcerative colitis: a prospective study

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Crohn's disease (CD) is increasingly diagnosed in children, a population in which hepatic dysfunction and coexisting liver conditions are not uncommon. These factors may contribute to the development of hepatic fibrosis. OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to evaluate liver involvement in pediatric CD at diagnosis and to explore the utility of noninvasive markers, including fibroblast growth factor 19 (FGF-19) and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF), in detecting early fibrotic changes. MATERIAL AND METHODS: Fifty children with newly diagnosed CD and 23 healthy controls without gastrointestinal disorders were enrolled. All participants underwent liver function testing, noninvasive fibrosis scoring, serum FGF-19 and CTGF measurement, and liver elastography. RESULTS: No patient in either group demonstrated significant hepatic fibrosis (>F2 on the METAVIR scale). No statistically significant differences were found in liver elastography results between the study group and the control group (3.98 kPa vs 3.92 kPa, p = 0.430). A positive correlation was found between liver stiffness values and fecal calprotectin levels, suggesting a link to intestinal inflammation. Compared to controls, CD patients had significantly lower serum FGF-19 (87.8 pg/mL vs 123.7 pg/mL, p = 0.028) and higher CTGF concentrations (710.8 pg/mL vs 252.2 pg/mL, p < 0.001). CONCLUSIONS: Elevated liver stiffness was uncommon at the time of CD diagnosis in pediatric patients; no patients had findings suggestive of advanced fibrosis (≥F3), and only 1 patient had liver stiffness corresponding to F2 fibrosis. Altered levels of FGF-19 and CTGF may reflect intestinal inflammation and a potential fibrotic response within the gut rather than the liver. Elevated elastography values appear to be influenced by intestinal disease activity, underscoring the importance of distinguishing hepatic from intestinal sources of biomarker changes in this population.

- [ ] **Targeting pyroptosis in intestinal and systemic inflammatory diseases: mechanisms and therapeutic opportunities.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Li J, Chen Y, Zhu L
  Molecular biology reports | 2026-10-20
  [10.1007/s11033-026-12920-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11033-026-12920-6) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Autophagy and multimodal programmed cell death and their crosstalk in ulcerative colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Pyroptosis is a lytic form of programmed cell death driven by gasdermin cleavage and subsequent plasma membrane pore formation, which triggers cell swelling, lysis, and the release of inflammatory cytokines that amplify tissue injury. Once viewed as an innate antimicrobial defense mechanism, pyroptosis is now recognized as a central pathogenic pathway in both acute and chronic inflammatory diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Its execution proceeds through a cascade of druggable events-inflammasome assembly, caspase activation, and gasdermin pore formation-and it operates within an interconnected cell death network, engaging apoptosis, necroptosis, and ferroptosis under the emerging PANoptosis framework. In the intestine, this network displays striking cell type- and context-dependent duality: gasdermin-mediated death can disrupt the epithelial barrier and exacerbate mucosal inflammation, yet the same effectors support barrier integrity, epithelial repair, and controlled cytokine release. This duality may explain why pyroptosis inhibition, although robustly protective in preclinical colitis models, has so far shown limited clinical efficacy. In this review, we summarize the molecular mechanisms of pyroptotic signaling, its crosstalk with other death pathways, and its roles in intestinal and systemic inflammatory diseases. We then critically evaluate the therapeutic landscape, covering NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors, regulators of gasdermin post-translational modifications, natural compounds, and nanotechnology-based delivery systems, with emphasis on multi-indication clinical progress and the lessons from recent trial failures. Finally, we highlight priorities for translation-human disease models, tissue-restricted delivery, and biomarker-guided patient stratification-that may convert mechanistic insight into clinical benefit for patients with IBD and related inflammatory disorders.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Clinical trial data pipelines must follow Clinical Data Interchange Standards Consortium (CDISC) standards, including the study data tabulation model (SDTM) and analysis data model (ADaM). General-purpose large language models (LLMs) can produce plausible artifacts that omit required variables, misuse terminology, or invent derivation logic. Curated context may reduce these errors, but the value of organizing that context by workflow topology has not been separated from corpus access alone. OBJECTIVE: This study aimed to examine when workflow topology-guided skill loading adds value beyond a domain prompt, nonrouted curated context, or lexical retrieval, and whether bounded scheduling retains quality with less injected context. METHODS: A 45-node, 7-layer directed acyclic workflow graph was linked to 59 modular rule files (SKILL.md). A sequential exploratory evaluation examined 4 evidence regimes spanning domain-context, component isolation, lexically reachable, and topology-dependent conditions (10 tasks, 90 runs; 10 tasks, 180 and 120 runs; 24 tasks, 288 runs; and 18 tasks, 324 runs; the task, not the agent run, was the inferential unit). Task-level paired inference used bootstrap 95% CIs, exact sign-flip tests, and Holm adjustment. RESULTS: Routed context scored 4.63 versus 4.20 for prompt-only (difference 0.43 on a 1-5 scale; 95% CI 0.25-0.63; Holm-adjusted P=.01) but did not exceed flat or random loading. CONCLUSIONS: Workflow topology serves as a conditional retrieval prior. It adds little when lexical retrieval already reaches the required evidence but recovers dependency-linked evidence when task wording does not identify it lexically. These findings, from a synthetic benchmark using one generator backbone and one expert rater, support topology-guided selection as a promising approach for controlled context assembly and motivate independent factorial validation.

  Nearest seed: The Israeli IBD Research Nucleus: collaborative advancements in Israeli IBD research.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Clinical research is an essential pillar of modern inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) care, underpinning therapeutic innovation, biomarker discovery, precision medicine, and improvements in healthcare delivery. However, while international initiatives have successfully defined standards for clinical care, comparatively little attention has been devoted to the infrastructure required to conduct high-quality clinical research. Consequently, research capacity remains highly heterogeneous across centers, limiting patient access to clinical studies and reducing opportunities for collaborative research. To address this gap, a Belgian multi-stakeholder initiative involving patients, gastroenterologists, research nurses, study coordinators, and clinical researchers developed a practical toolbox for establishing and strengthening IBD clinical research units. Rather than defining rigid accreditation criteria, the toolbox distinguishes between essential components required for a functional research unit and important but non-essential elements that further improve quality, efficiency, and long-term sustainability. Structured across 3 complementary domains (administrative, operational, and collaborative) this framework provides a pragmatic roadmap that can support centers at different stages of research maturity. Beyond describing the toolbox, we discuss the concept of research readiness as a hallmark of excellence in modern IBD care and propose a roadmap for building sustainable research ecosystems. Although developed within the Belgian healthcare system, the principles presented are broadly applicable and may support the development of research capacity across healthcare systems and disease areas.

  Nearest seed: Application of large language models for assigning clear cell likelihood score v2.0 from free–text MRI reports: a feasibility study

  > [!abstract]-
  > Vision-language models (VLMs) represent an emerging class of multimodal artificial intelligence systems that integrate visual information with natural-language understanding and generation. In computational pathology, VLMs provide a framework for aligning histologic morphology from whole-slide images with pathology reports and other text-based knowledge sources. This review summarizes the technical foundations, major applications, evaluation strategies, and deployment considerations of pathology VLMs. Current pathology VLMs support a growing range of use cases, including image-text retrieval, label-efficient classification, visual question answering, abnormality localization, anomaly detection, report generation, and agentic workflow support. These capabilities are enabled by image encoders, text encoders or large language models, multimodal alignment strategies, and, in some systems, generative language components. Despite rapid progress, several barriers remain. The evaluation of pathology VLMs is constrained by limited domain-specific benchmarks, insufficient assessment of visual grounding, overreliance on text-based metrics, vulnerability to hallucination, and uncertain robustness under data shift. Clinical translation also requires validation across institutions, scanners, staining protocols, tissue types, and patient populations, together with workflow integration, regulatory oversight, data privacy, cybersecurity, and pathologist accountability. VLMs are therefore best viewed as assistive systems that may augment rather than replace pathologists. Responsible development will require close collaboration among pathologists, computational scientists, health systems, and regulatory stakeholders to ensure that VLMs improve pathology practice in a safe, interpretable, and clinically meaningful manner.
