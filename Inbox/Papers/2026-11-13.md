  Reyad Elzaanoun, Navneet Natt, Rocio Sedano
  Inflammatory Bowel Diseases | 2026-11-12
  [10.1093/ibd/izag200](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag200) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Prevalence and epidemiologic features of adult upper gastrointestinal Crohn’s disease over the last 30 years: a scoping review

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Upper gastrointestinal (UGI) involvement occurs in about 13% of patients with Crohn’s disease (CD) and is variably defined across guidelines, which complicates prognosis. We mapped adult data on prognostic factors and outcomes in UGI‑CD and identified evidence gaps.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A comprehensive search strategy was applied to MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane Central through November 26, 2025. We included studies reporting prognostic factors in patients with UGI-CD. We synthesized evidence across 3 domains: (1) patient and disease characteristics associated with UGI involvement at diagnosis, (2) the comparative prognosis of UGI-CD vs non-UGI disease, and (3) among patients with UGI-CD, factors associated with long-term adverse outcomes. Findings were synthesized narratively.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > From 3297 screened records, 732 underwent full text assessment, and 43 primary studies were included. A total of 101 prognostic factor-outcome relationships were identified. In domain 1 (patient and disease characteristics associated with UGI involvement at diagnosis), younger age at onset was the most consistently reported factor associated with UGI involvement. In domain 2 (comparative prognosis vs non-UGI disease), studies evaluating pooled L4 disease frequently associated UGI involvement at diagnosis with higher surgical risk, incident stricture development, and adverse long-term outcomes. However, studies that distinguished anatomic subtypes suggested that risk is not uniform: jejunal/proximal small bowel disease generally followed a more aggressive course, whereas esophagogastroduodenal-limited disease appeared to have a comparatively more favorable prognosis. In domain 3 (among patients with UGI-CD, factors associated with long-term adverse outcomes), a higher Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s Disease was associated with subsequent surgery in a duodenal stricture cohort on univariable analysis; an independent prognostic effect was not established.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > The available literature suggests that prognosis in UGI-CD is not uniform. Jejunal/proximal small bowel disease appears to follow a more aggressive course than esophagogastroduodenal-limited disease, although interpretation is limited by predominantly retrospective designs, heterogeneous definitions, and inconsistent adjustment for confounders.

  Nearest seed: Correction: Multimodal AI for early prediction of adverse clinical outcomes in acute pancreatitis

  > [!abstract]-
  > Acute pancreatitis (AP) has a highly variable clinical course. This narrative review focuses on computed tomography (CT) radiomics, deep learning and clinical-imaging models for severity and adverse-outcome prediction, with clinical-only models as context. A targeted PubMed search checked on 16 October 2026, with a publication cutoff of 14 October 2026, retrieved 80 unique records; ten CT-informed exemplars and eight clinical-context studies were selected for detailed comparison. This is a representative, non-exhaustive synthesis. For severe AP (SAP) prediction, reported same-center internal or temporal areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUCs) ranged from 0.852 to 0.894, and reported external-test AUCs from 0.887 to 0.916; one validation cohort of unconfirmed independence had an AUC of 0.920. These descriptive ranges do not establish model superiority or characterize all retrieved studies. CT protocols and input timing, endpoint definitions, sampling and validation designs varied. Predominantly retrospective evidence, uncertain prediction landmarks, incomplete calibration and threshold reporting, and limited prospective impact assessment constrain translation. Feature explanations describe model associations rather than causal intervention targets. Imaging artificial intelligence (AI) remains an adjunct to clinical assessment; independent validation, calibration, decision-curve appraisal and prospective workflow/outcome evaluation are needed before clinical-pathway integration.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: Pancreatic involvement in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) represents a heterogeneous group of conditions. Our study assessed the prevalence of hyperlipasemia and pancreatic imaging involvement in children with newly diagnosed IBD. We also evaluated predictors of pancreatic changes on magnetic resonance imaging in this population. METHODS: This retrospective study analysed the standard magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) protocol with additional sequences for pancreatic assessment. The serum activity of lipase, aminotransferases, and total immunoglobulin G (IgG) and its subclasses concentrations were measured at diagnosis, as well as body mass index percentile (pBMI) and IBD activity.   > Abstract
  >
  > Objectives
  > To evaluate the impact of fat saturation on T2-weighted (T2W) imaging in the assessment of small renal masses (SRMs; cT1a ≤ 4 cm) with the clear cell likelihood score (ccLS).
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > This retrospective study included 147 histologically confirmed SRMs evaluated with MRI including non-fat saturated (NFS) and fat saturated (FS) T2W fast spin echo acquisitions. Two readers (R1, R2) assigned qualitative T2 signal categories (hypo-, iso-, hyperintense), with sequence-specific ccLS scores recalculated while all non-T2 ccLS components were held constant. Agreement, sequence-related reclassification, and inter-reader agreement were assessed for T2 signal intensity and ccLS scores. Quantitative mass-to-cortex signal-intensity ratios were compared between sequences using Bland-Altman analysis.
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > Qualitative agreement between sequences was 74% and 65% for R1 and R2, respectively. FS-T2W was associated with over four-fold greater cumulative odds of a higher qualitative T2 signal category than NFS-T2W (OR, 4.68; 95% CI, 2.91–7.52;
  > p
  > < 0.001), with upgrading in 20% (R1) and 30% (R2) of lesions versus downgrading in 5% for both readers. Qualitative inter-reader agreement was substantial (κ = 0.77 for NFS-T2W; κ = 0.73 for FS-T2W). Mass-to-cortex ratios were higher on FS-T2W (median 1.30 vs. 1.15;
  > p
  > < 0.001), corresponding to a mean proportional increase of 17.9% (95% CI, 12.4–23.4%). ccLS concordance remained high between NFS-T2W and FS-T2W (R1: 94% and R2: 90%; weighted κ = 0.944 and 0.918).   > p
  > = 0.006). Inter-reader agreement for ccLS was almost perfect (weighted κ = 0.98 for NFS-T2W; 0.95 for FS-T2W).
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > Fat saturation introduces a systematic upward bias in qualitative T2 signal assessment of small renal masses.   > Abstract

  > Objectives
  > This study aimed to evaluate atrioventricular interaction in patients with chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) and to determine the effects of balloon pulmonary angioplasty (BPA) on reversible cardiac remodeling assessed by cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging.
  >
  >
  > Materials and methods
  > In this prospective single-center study, 52 patients with CTEPH (mean age: 58 ± 11 years; 34 females) and 35 healthy controls underwent CMR imaging. Twenty-five patients (mean age: 60 ± 12 years; 19 females) who underwent BPA received follow-up CMR at a mean interval of 7 ± 5 months. Cardiac structure, function, myocardial deformation, and epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) were assessed based on cine images.
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > Compared with controls, patients with CTEPH demonstrated predominant right-sided cardiac involvement, characterized by reduced right ventricular ejection fraction (
  > p
  > < 0.001), marked enlargement of right atrial (RA) and ventricular (RV) volumes, and severely impaired right heart strain (all
  > p
  > < 0.001). Left ventricular systolic function was relatively preserved, with mildly impaired global circumferential strain (
  > p
  > = 0.011) and a trend toward attenuated longitudinal strain (
  > p
  > = 0.055), whereas all left atrial (LA) strain components were markedly impaired (all
  > p
  > < 0.05), indicating altered atrioventricular interaction. Following BPA, the right heart structure, RV function and deformation, and LA conduit strain improved. EAT was comparable between groups and remained unchanged after BPA.
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > CTEPH involves marked right heart systolic dysfunction and early biventricular diastolic abnormalities associated with impaired atrioventricular interaction. BPA could reverse cardiac remodeling by improving right heart structure, function, and partially recovering atrial deformation after pressure relief.

  >
  >
  > Questions
  >
  > Atrioventricular interaction abnormalities and reversible cardiac remodeling in chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) after balloon pulmonary angioplasty (BPA) remain insufficiently characterized.

  >
  >
  > Findings

  >
  >
  >
  > Critical relevance statement
