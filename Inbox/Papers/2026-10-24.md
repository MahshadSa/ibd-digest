  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Reports on postoperative outcomes after ileocolic resection (ICR) in Crohn’s disease (CD) vary widely. To provide insights into these differences, we compared international data on preoperative characteristics and postoperative management and outcome.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > CD patients undergoing ICR were included from prospective cohort studies in the Netherlands (NL), France (FR), Canada (CAN), and United States of America (USA).
  >
  >
  > Results

  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Notable differences in preoperative characteristics and postoperative management and outcomes for ileocolic (re-)resections in CD were observed across European and North American countries.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > One-third of patients with acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) fail intravenous corticosteroids. We evaluated whether adding tofacitinib to corticosteroids reduces treatment failure in ASUC.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > In this single-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, adults with ASUC were randomized 1:1 to tofacitinib (10 mg three times daily for 3 days, then twice daily) or placebo for 5 days alongside corticosteroids. Primary outcome was treatment failure at day 6 (&lt;3-point Lichtiger reduction or total score ≥10, or colectomy). Secondary outcomes were relapse (≥3-point rise with total ≥10), rescue therapy, and colectomy at 90 days. Modified intention-to-treat analysis was performed. (CTRI/2022/11/047186)
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Of 62 randomized patients, 58 were analyzed: 33 in the tofacitinib arm and 25 in the placebo arm. The trial was terminated early because of slow recruitment and did not reach the planned sample size of 98. Day 6 treatment failure was numerically lower with tofacitinib in unadjusted analysis (24.2% vs 36.0%; RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.30-1.51; P = .33) and significantly lower after adjustment for baseline covariate imbalance (adjusted RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.24-0.93; P = .03). At 90 days, colectomy rates were similar (12.1% vs 12.0%; P = 1.0), but more tofacitinib-treated patients remained on assigned therapy (84.4% vs 54.2%) and rescue therapy was lower (HR 0.27, 95% CI 0.09-0.78; P = .01). Serious adverse events occurred in one versus three patients. No deaths occurred with tofacitinib versus two deaths with placebo.
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > Dual therapy with tofacitinib and steroids in ASUC did not significantly reduce treatment failure at day 6, unless adjustment for baseline imbalance in serum albumin was taken into account. It reduced the need for rescue therapy over 90 days without affecting colectomy risk. Larger clinical trials are required to confirm these findings.

  >
  > Background and aims
  > In perianal fistulizing Crohn’s disease (PFCD), higher infliximab (IFX) serum concentrations are associated with improved outcomes.   >
  >
  > Methods
  > Patients with PFCD and LCD starting standard-dose intravenous IFX (5 mg/kg at weeks 0, 2, and 6, thereafter every 8 weeks) were enrolled.   >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 148 patients were included (62 PFCD, 86 LCD) with 756 serum samples analyzed. IFX PK was described by a two-compartment model with linear elimination.   >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > The presence of perianal fistulas increases IFX CL in patients with Crohn’s disease, resulting in significantly lower and frequently subtherapeutic serum concentrations after standard induction dosing.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Corticosteroid-refractory acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) remains a key management challenge despite an increasing understanding of rescue strategies. Due to the relative paucity of high-quality evidence, the use of infliximab in ASUC should be informed by pragmatic application of key clinical and pharmacological principles. This review synthesizes emerging evidence of infliximab pharmacology, describes key concepts of mechanisms of infliximab failure, and suggests an optimized approach to using infliximab in ASUC.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Relevant studies investigating infliximab in ASUC and infliximab pharmacology were identified by searching MEDLINE and EMBASE.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Infliximab pharmacokinetics are altered and undergo dynamic change during ASUC. Infliximab clearance is increased due to proteolytic degradation, target-mediated drug disposition, fecal losses and possibly early anti-drug antibody formation. Current pharmacokinetic models fail to account for both the time-varying nature of clearance and the reciprocal relationship between pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics. The propensity to respond to infliximab depends on both pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic factors. Patients with high clearance are at risk of pharmacokinetic failure and may benefit from escalated induction. Pre-treatment determination of clearance can be estimated by considering factors including higher weight, male sex, lower albumin, higher inflammatory markers, and more severe endoscopic disease. On-treatment clearance calculation based on peak concentrations may be feasible to inform dose escalation. Early response assessment and careful use of sequential therapy may benefit patients experiencing infliximab pharmacodynamic failure.
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > The use of infliximab in corticosteroid-refractory ASUC may be optimized by pre-treatment risk assessment, dose escalation, on-treatment response assessment, and consideration of sequential therapy.

Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) analysis showed that predictions were driven by relevant retinal regions, particularly the macular hole and adjacent layers. CONCLUSIONS: This study demonstrates the feasibility of an end-to-end AI workflow coordinated by a workflow orchestration agent for robust OCT-based MH diagnosis with a large language model for structured report generation.
