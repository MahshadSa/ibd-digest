  [10.1016/j.dld.2026.09.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dld.2026.09.025) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Subcutaneous infliximab as a maintenance option in pediatric IBD: a real-world cohort including younger and lower-weight children.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Subcutaneous infliximab is a convenient therapeutic option with potential long-term benefits. This study aimed to compare outcomes of subcutaneous infliximab (SC) versus intravenous double-dose infliximab (Double) in Crohn's disease patients requiring treatment optimization. METHODS: We conducted a prospective comparative cohort study of 49 young adult patients with Crohn's disease who had previously received intravenous infliximab and required treatment optimization because of pharmacokinetic underexposure, defined as an infliximab trough level <3 µg/mL. Patients selected one of two treatment strategies: switching to SC infliximab (n = 25) or continuing intravenous infliximab at a doubled dose (n = 24). Treatment outcomes were assessed at baseline and at weeks 24 and 52. RESULTS: Trough levels were significantly higher in the SC group at both week 24 (22.36 vs 7.12 µg/mL, p < 0.0001) and week 52 (20.6 vs 7.72 µg/mL, p < 0.0001). Predefined trough level thresholds were more frequently achieved with SC infliximab (≥5 µg/mL: 96.0% vs 70.8%, p = 0.023; ≥10 µg/mL: 96.0% vs 29.2%, p < 0.0001). At week 52, mucosal healing was achieved in 40.0% of the SC group and 37.5% of the Double group (p = 0.678). CONCLUSION: These exploratory findings suggest that subcutaneous infliximab may provide a more stable pharmacokinetic profile, while no statistically significant between-group difference in mucosal healing was observed.

- [ ] **An engineered probiotic for diagnosis and mucosal healing in inflammatory bowel disease.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Zhao Y, Sun Y, Zhou J
  Cell reports | 2026-10-20
  [10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118171](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118171) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: A scoping review on emerging biomarkers in inflammatory bowel disease: Towards precision medicine in diagnosis and therapeutic management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Early diagnosis and timely treatment of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) are critical to limit disease progression, yet integrated diagnostic-therapeutic strategies remain limited. Here, we design a synthetic probiotic system that responds to reactive oxygen species (ROS) to enable noninvasive detection of intestinal inflammation. We further engineer EcN-IL22, a therapeutic strain that conditionally expresses interleukin 22-fragment crystallizable (IL-22-Fc) fusion protein in situ to mitigate colitis-associated tissue damage. Mechanistically, EcN-IL22 activates epithelial IL-22 receptor 1/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (IL-22R1/STAT3) signaling, promoting epithelial barrier restoration and intestinal stem cell proliferation. We identify thyroid hormone receptor-interacting protein 13 (Trip13) and fidgetin-like 1 (Fignl1) as IL-22R1-responsive candidate genes associated with epithelial repair. Importantly, replacing the diagnostic output of the ROS-responsive circuit with IL-22-Fc enables inflammation-triggered therapeutic delivery and significantly alleviates colitis. Finally, EcN-IL22 demonstrates robust protective efficacy in a piglet colitis model, supporting its translational potential. Together, this work establishes an integrated probiotic platform for IBD diagnosis and regenerative therapy.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Dietary fibers modulate host-microbiota interactions. Whether fiber promotes epithelial repair through microbiota-dependent mechanisms remains unclear. We determined how alginate influences colitis recovery and whether the pathway is relevant to ulcerative colitis (UC). METHODS: We screened fibers in dextran sodium sulfate (DSS)-induced murine colitis and assessed survival, inflammation, barrier function, and epithelial regeneration. Microbiota dependence was evaluated using antibiotics and bacterial supplementation. Metabolites were profiled using capillary electrophoresis-time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS), and epithelial programs were analyzed using RNA-seq and colonic organoid injury assays. Slc6a6-/- and Cyp2c55-/- mice were used to determine mechanistic requirements. Translational relevance was examined in a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of UC patients receiving antibiotic-primed fecal microbiota transplantation (A-FMT) followed by alginate or placebo. In the clinical trial, the primary endpoint was not met. However, alginate supplementation was associated with biological changes, including fecal taurine levels and colonic CYP2C18 expression, consistent with preclinical mechanisms. CONCLUSION: Alginate engages a fiber-microbiota-taurine-epithelial signaling pathway that promotes recovery in preclinical models. The clinical trial did not meet its primary endpoint; however, the biological changes warrant further investigation of alginate as a microbiota-targeted intervention.   Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract, driven by immune dysregulation, oxidative stress, and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Histamine, an important mediator in gut inflammation, has immunomodulatory properties. Ebastine, a second-generation histamine H1-receptor antagonist, is primarily used for allergic conditions, but its potential role in managing IBD has not been fully explored. This study aimed to evaluate whether ebastine could prevent colitis induced by dextran sodium sulfate (DSS) in mice. The severity of the condition was assessed using the disease activity index (DAI), body weight loss, stool consistency, and rectal hemorrhage. Immune modulation was investigated by measuring serum IgE levels and inflammatory biomarkers like C-reactive protein (CRP). The study also examined the impact of ebastine on oxidative stress markers and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6), alongside histopathological analysis of colon tissue. Swiss albino mice were induced with experimental colitis using 3% DSS and divided into five groups, including a normal control, DSS control, standard treatment (sulfasalazine), and two ebastine-treated groups (2 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg). Clinical, histopathological, and molecular markers were analyzed. Ebastine significantly improved clinical parameters, reduced DAI, inhibited oxidative stress, and lowered TNF-α and IL-6 levels. The high dose showed more potent anti-inflammatory effects, while the low dose demonstrated better antioxidant activity. Ebastine offers protection against DSS-induced colitis by modulating inflammation and oxidative stress, suggesting its potential as an adjunctive treatment for IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Cycas revoluta Thunb. has traditionally been used for the management of gastrointestinal and inflammatory disorders, but its bioactive constituents and anti-inflammatory potential remain insufficiently characterized. This study aimed to optimize the extraction of phenolic-rich compounds from C. revoluta leaves and evaluate the anti-inflammatory and coloprotective effects of the optimized extract against experimental ulcerative colitis (UC). Four extraction techniques, namely Soxhlet extraction, maceration, ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE), and microwave-assisted extraction (MAE), were compared based on extraction yield, total phenolic content (TPC), total flavonoid content (TFC), antioxidant, and antibacterial activities. Moreover, it reduced TNF-α, nitric oxide, and VEGF levels while increasing HO-1 expression.   > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: The clinical course of primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)-associated inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) after liver transplantation (LTx) varies significantly across studies. This meta-analysis aims to provide a comprehensive estimation of incidence rates (IR) for IBD-related outcomes and PSC recurrence. METHODS: Online databases (January 2000-June 2025) were searched for studies on PSC patients with or without IBD at LTx. Studies reporting posttransplant outcomes of interest were included.
