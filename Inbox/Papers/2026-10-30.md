  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Multiple advanced therapies are available for moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis (UC), but comparative efficacy data are limited. We compared the 1-year efficacy and safety of 7 therapies: the anti-tumor necrosis factor agents infliximab, adalimumab, and golimumab (GLM); the anti-integrin vedolizumab (VDZ); the anti-interleukin-12/23 ustekinumab (UST); and the JAK inhibitors tofacitinib (TOF) and upadacitinib (UPA).
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > In a retrospective, multicenter, propensity score–matched study, all UC patients treated with the 7 advanced therapies between 2014 and 2024 were included. The primary endpoint was the 1-year rate of continuous clinical remission (CCR) for each drug (defined as the induction and maintenance of steroid-free clinical remission, without the need for drug optimization or additional therapy due to relapse, with no adverse events requiring drug discontinuation).
  >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 1406 patients were included (VDZ = 376, infliximab = 341, adalimumab = 207, UST = 150, GLM = 114, TOF = 103, UPA = 115). UST appeared the most effective therapy in bio-naive patients, whereas other biologics achieved similar CCR rates. As second-line therapy, UPA achieved the highest CCR, followed by UST. As third-line therapy, UPA outperformed UST, VDZ, and TOF. UST was associated with the lowest rates of adverse events. Although UPA was associated with the highest rates of adverse events, drug withdrawal was low.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > In this large multicenter observational cohort, UST was more effective than anti-tumor necrosis factor agents and VDZ as both first- and second-line therapy. In bio-exposed patients, UPA was the most effective advanced therapy.

  >
  > Background
  > Fecal calprotectin (FC) can help to differentiate inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) from other causes of lower gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms. This study aimed to study FC testing in primary care, associated factors, subsequent secondary care referrals and IBD diagnoses.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A retrospective cohort study was conducted using primary care data in England. Adults aged 18-60 with lower GI symptoms between 2015 and 2023 were included. FC testing 30 days before to 3 months after a recorded lower GI symptom was examined. A random-effects logistic regression model explored patient-level factors associated with FC testing.   >
  >
  > Results
  > Of 830 936 symptomatic patients, 34 944 (4.2%) had FC testing. FC testing increased from 1.6% of symptomatic patients in 2015 to 7.7% in 2023 (P = .001).   > [!abstract]-
  > PURPOSE OF REVIEW: Crohn's disease typically presents with primarily, luminal disease that progresses transmurally, producing structural bowel damage (strictures and fistula). This review examines the rationale for early treatment in Crohn's disease, summarizing the clinical evidence supporting early intervention to prevent transmural progression, an increased inflammatory burden and associated refractory disease. RECENT FINDINGS: Translational studies show that transmural inflammation shifts from a reversible to an irreversible, fibrotic process over time. Future efforts should refine the biological definition of early disease, improve patient selection, and move toward disease interception.
