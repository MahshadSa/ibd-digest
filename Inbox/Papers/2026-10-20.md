- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Turner D, Ricciuto A, Dignass A
  The lancet. Gastroenterology & hepatology | 2026-10-19
  [10.1016/s2468-1253(26)00292-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2468-1253(26)00292-x) | Score: 0.98
  Nearest seed: STRIDE-II: An Update on the Selecting Therapeutic Targets in Inflammatory Bowel Disease (STRIDE) Initiative of the International Organization for the Study of IBD (IOIBD): Determining Therapeutic Goals for Treat-to-Target strategies in IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Since Selecting Therapeutic Targets in Inflammatory Bowel Disease-II (STRIDE-II), new evidence has become available across inflammatory and patient-centred outcomes. We aimed to update the STRIDE recommendations for Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. We searched MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL for studies published after the STRIDE-II search period. Findings were integrated with surveys of International Organization for the Study of IBD (IOIBD) members and paediatric gastroenterologists from the Porto group of the European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition. Statements were subsequently voted on using a modified Delphi approach; consensus was defined as ≥75% of participants rating a statement 7-10 on a 10-point scale. Of 13 071 records identified in the systematic review, 1018 were included. Surveys were completed by 60 of 79 IOIBD gastroenterologists and 23 of 30 paediatric gastroenterologists in the Porto group. STRIDE-III defines three conceptually distinct categories of treatment targets. Actionable targets include clinical response and remission, which reflect patient priorities, and objective inflammatory targets (C-reactive protein normalisation, faecal calprotectin within target range, and endoscopic remission), supported by interventional evidence demonstrating benefits of target-guided treatment modification. Adjunctive targets (ie, urgency, histological remission in ulcerative colitis, transmural remission in Crohn's disease, and growth) are supported by studies showing association with favourable outcomes and guide treatment optimisation and tighter monitoring but do not necessarily require a change in therapeutic class. Patient-centred targets (ie, disability, fatigue, and quality of life) directly reflect patients' well-being and are impaired in a substantial proportion of patients with inflammatory bowel disease; impairment should prompt assessment of inflammation and alternative causes and management through shared decision-making. Targets are further classified as immediate, short-term, intermediate, and long-term. STRIDE-III also defines treatment-specific times to target achievement, new faecal calprotectin thresholds, and adaptations for resource-limited settings. STRIDE-III distinguishes targets that should prompt treatment change from those that primarily guide optimisation and monitoring, while establishing a separate patient-centred pathway based on shared decision-making. This framework supports consistent, evidence-based treat-to-target care.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background & Aims
  > PRV-300 is a fully human IgG4κ monoclonal antibody that binds the extracellular domain of human toll-like receptor 3, which is overexpressed in the inflamed colonic epithelium in patients with inflammatory bowel disease. This study aimed at proving its safety and efficacy in patients with ulcerative colitis (UC).
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Patients with moderate-to-severe UC (Mayo Clinical Score [MCS] 6-12) were randomized to receive 7 doses of PRV-300 intravenously or matched placebo (PBO). After a 12-week treatment period, participants underwent an 8-week follow-up. The primary endpoint was to assess the safety and tolerability of PRV-300. Secondary endpoints included assessment of pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity, and endoscopic disease activity.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > In total, 38 patients were randomized (28 in PRV-300 arm, 9 in placebo arm), of whom 32 completed the trial (24 and 8, respectively). Although TEAEs and TE-SAEs were numerically higher with PRV-300 than placebo (50.0% vs 11.1% and 39.3% vs 0.0%, respectively), the drug was well-tolerated, with only 7.1% (2/28) discontinuing therapy due to AEs and most events being mild, non-specific, or related to underlying disease. In colonic biopsies, PRV-300 had a greater, albeit not statistically significant, effect on the downregulation of TLR3 expression, but not on a UC gene expression signature. No differences in MCS and endoscopic severity were observed.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > While PRV-300 demonstrated an acceptable safety profile, TLR3 blockade did not translate into clinical benefit despite exploratory evidence of target engagement. Our results are challenging a pivotal role of the mode-of-action for the pathogenesis of UC (funded by Provention Bio)
  >
  >
  > Clinical trial registration number
  > ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03849599).

- [ ] Read later
  Pu J, Ding Y, Liu C
  Food & function | 2026-10-19
  [10.1039/d6fo03442j](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6fo03442j) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is driven by a complex interplay among epithelial barrier breakdown, immune dysregulation, microbial dysbiosis, and redox imbalance. Curcumin, a major curcuminoid from turmeric (Curcuma longa L.) and a widely used food ingredient, has attracted sustained interest as a dietary bioactive for IBD prevention and adjunct management. Notably, curcumin shows low systemic bioavailability, allowing a substantial fraction to reach the distal gut, where it can be biotransformed by the gut microbiota and act locally on the mucosa. This review synthesises recent evidence on: (i) curcumin's gastrointestinal fate, including host conjugation and microbial reduction to more stable metabolites; (ii) the bidirectional curcumin-microbiota relationship, whereby baseline microbial functions shape curcumin metabolism and response, while curcumin remodels microbial ecology and metabolite outputs (short-chain fatty acids, bile acids and tryptophan-derived indoles); and (iii) downstream host pathways linked to barrier integrity, NF-κB and MAPK signalling, immune cell polarisation, and redox-sensitive programmes (Nrf2 and ferroptosis susceptibility). We further discuss food-grade formulation and delivery strategies that tune colonic exposure and microbiota engagement, and outline research priorities for evidence mapping, responder stratification and standardised biomarkers to accelerate translation of curcumin-based functional foods for IBD.

  Ngo A, Tian R, Youens K
  Journal of pathology informatics | 2026-11-01
  [10.1016/j.jpi.2026.100718](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpi.2026.100718) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Can agentic AI dent Eroom's Law? Large reasoning models across the drug discovery and development pipeline.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Artificial intelligence (AI) in anatomic pathology is dominated by image-based diagnostic applications, but much of the specialty's cognitive work is textual: reviewing clinical history, drafting reports, coordinating testing, and communicating results. Large language models can be useful for many of these tasks, and agentic systems extend their capabilities by retrieving external information, invoking software tools, following task-specific instructions, tracking workflow states, and carrying out complex multi-step tasks under human oversight. This conceptual review outlines key components of agentic AI systems including retrieval augmented generation, tool use, skills, and the so-called "agentic loop"-and applies them to anatomic pathology workflows. Three illustrative design sketches use reflex testing support, workload balancing and case routing, and ancillary test decision support to explain how agentic systems can combine lab information system data, trustworthy knowledge sources, local policy, and explicit human approval gates. We examine considerations for clinical deployment, including interoperability and integration challenges, lifecycle governance and validation, security and safety concerns, and cost and sustainability. Agentic AI offers a plausible and constructive near-term vision for AI in pathology: better-supported diagnosis that allows pathologists to spend more of their time on the judgment, accountability, and genuine care that remain distinctly human.

  European journal of pediatrics | 2026-10-19
  [10.1007/s00431-026-07512-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-07512-2) | Score: 0.94
  Nearest seed: Impact of Upper Gastrointestinal Involvement on Clinical Outcomes in Paediatric Inflammatory Bowel Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > UNLABELLED: Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection is prevalent worldwide, and its potential protective role in allergic and immune diseases has been a topic of speculation. This systematic review aims to analyse recent literature to establish a consensus on this relationship. Articles published in the past 12 years regarding association of H. pylori and various allergic diseases were screened. Thirty-five articles from three databases were assessed for bias using the ROBINS-E tool. Objective data from each article were collected for this review. Among the reviewed articles, 14 focused on asthma, 10 on eliac disease(CeD), 5 on eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), 7 on inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and 8 on mixed conditions. In the asthma meta-analysis, H. pylori infection was not significantly associated with asthma overall (OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.54-1.05; I2 = 67.3%). Subgroup analyses showed an inverse association in studies using UBT/SAT, whereas histology-based studies showed no association. Low-risk-of-bias studies likewise showed no significant association. Results for celiac and IBD were mixed. No clear trends were observed for the other conditions analysed. CONCLUSION: Current paediatric evidence does not demonstrate a significant overall association between H. pylori infection and asthma. Apparent inverse associations were confined to specific diagnostic and risk-of-bias subgroups. Evidence for celiac disease, IBD, EoE, and other immune-mediated disorders remains inconsistent. More prospective paediatric studies are needed to understand the immune modulation of H. pylori infection. WHAT IS KNOWN: • The relationship between Helicobacter pylori infection and allergic or immune-mediated diseases remains complex and incompletely understood. • Previous studies, including mixed studies with adult and paediatric populations, have reported inconsistent and sometimes conflicting associations. WHAT IS NEW: • This is the first comprehensive paediatric review covering multiple conditions simultaneously, synthesising evidence on the association between Helicobacter pylori infection and allergic and immune-mediated diseases. • Analysis of paediatric data found no overall association between H. pylori infection and asthma risk. Histology-based studies confirmed this finding, whereas a slight inverse association was observed only in studies using non-invasive diagnostic methods (UBT or SAT) and in those with a higher risk of bias.
