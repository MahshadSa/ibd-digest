  Nearest seed: Effects of Age on Inflammatory Bowel Disease Presentation and Management in Older Adults.

  > [!abstract]-
  > PURPOSE OF REVIEW: The purpose of this review is to synthesize the recent evidence regarding efficacy and safety of advanced therapies and surgical interventions in older adults with IBD, as well as examine how geriatric factors (frailty, multimorbidity, polypharmacy, and patient priorities) should influence the selection of therapies in this population. FINDINGS: Ongoing inflammation is often a key driver of adverse outcomes in older adults with IBD, and the efficacy of biologics and small molecules is generally preserved in this population. Treatment selection should therefore prioritize efficacy while also accounting for comorbidities, infection and malignancy risk, and potential drug interactions; chronological age alone should not limit their use. Early surgery may also benefit appropriately selected patients, while functional assessment can identify those suitable for immediate intervention and those who may benefit from preoperative optimization. Across treatment decisions, the geriatric 5Ms, multicomplexity, medications, mobility, mind, and what matters most, can help guide individualized care. Treatment of older adults with IBD is complex and multidimensional. Management paradigms should shift away from therapeutic hesitancy on the basis of chronological age alone and towards an individualized approach that prioritizes effective disease control and symptom relief, thereby preserving function, independence, and promoting healthy aging for older adults with IBD.

- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Javier P Gisbert, María Chaparro
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-11-20
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag198](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag198) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: ECCO Topical Review on Optimisation of Therapy for Inflammatory Bowel Disease

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Acute severe ulcerative colitis is an emergency in which treatment selection and timing are inseparable. This narrative review synthesizes published response trajectories and proposes treatment-specific futility windows for inpatient therapies, including intravenous corticosteroids, cyclosporine, infliximab, Janus kinase inhibitors, calcineurin-inhibitor bridging strategies, and sequential rescue. We propose that trajectory should override any protocol day. Deterioration, systemic toxicity, worsening abdominal findings, organ dysfunction, or radiologic complications should prompt immediate escalation and surgical reassessment rather than waiting for a scheduled checkpoint. Conversely, objective improvement without remission may justify cautious continuation. Therapeutic stagnation requires integrating stool frequency, bleeding, systemic features, C-reactive protein, albumin, hemoglobin, imaging, informative endoscopy, pharmacokinetic exposure when available, and surgical risk. For intravenous corticosteroids, assessment starts at admission; day 3 guides rescue preparation; if no deterioration occurs beforehand, lack of meaningful objective improvement by day 5 should prompt rescue therapy or surgery. Cyclosporine usually declares its trajectory within the first week. For infliximab, days 5-7 are the principal clinical decision window; stable patients with inadequate exposure may undergo optimization, with later reassessment reserved for partial responders. Tofacitinib and upadacitinib may act rapidly; off-label rescue evidence remains observational, and absent improvement by days 5-7 is concerning. When a calcineurin inhibitor (CNI) bridges to a maintenance biologic, acute decisions should be based on response to the CNI, not the maintenance agent. Sequential third-line rescue should be exceptional, multidisciplinary, time-limited, and never delay colectomy when deterioration or a surgical indication is present. Across therapies, reassessment must be paired with continuous surgical involvement.

- [ ] **Stem Cells and Derivatives in Inflammatory Bowel Disease: Mechanisms, Sources, and Therapeutic Perspectives.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Cappa Spina A, Puca P, Lopetuso LR
  Stem cell reviews and reports | 2026-11-20
  [10.1007/s12015-026-11274-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12015-026-11274-7) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Mesenchymal stromal/stem cells in inflammatory bowel disease: Crosstalk with innate and adaptive immunity.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD), including Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, are chronic, relapsing disorders of the gastrointestinal tract for which a substantial proportion of patients fail to respond to current therapies, underscoring the need for novel strategies. Mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) and their derivatives have emerged as a promising frontier in regenerative medicine, owing to their self-renewal capacity, multilineage differentiation, and potent immunomodulatory properties. MSCs modulate the intestinal microenvironment by suppressing effector T-cell responses, expanding regulatory T (Treg) and B (Breg) cells, polarizing macrophages toward anti-inflammatory phenotypes, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines, and shaping the gut microbiota. Most of these effects are mediated by paracrine signals carried by the MSC secretome and extracellular vesicles (EVs), which deliver cytokines, microRNAs, and proteins that promote tissue repair, angiogenesis, and resolution of inflammation. Cell-free approaches based on conditioned medium or exosomes offer further advantages over direct cell transplantation, including improved safety, easier standardization, and reduced immunogenicity. MSCs derived from bone marrow, adipose tissue, and perinatal tissues each show distinct biological and therapeutic features. The treatment of complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease is the most clinically advanced application, with darvadstrocel demonstrating safety and efficacy in clinical trials. Additional emerging indications include intestinal fibrosis and tissue regeneration. Key challenges remain, including cellular heterogeneity, lack of standardized manufacturing, and optimization of dosing and delivery. Future perspectives point toward a paradigm shift from cell-based therapies to next generation cell-free strategies, including secretome-derived formulations and engineered extracellular vescicles, which may provide safer, more scalable and ultimately disease-modifying therapeutic options for patients with IBD.

  Nearest seed: Longitudinal transcriptomic analysis of mucosa in ulcerative colitis after anti-tumor necrosis factor withdrawal compared to continued treatment

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and Aims
  > Despite an expanding therapeutic landscape, an efficacy ceiling persists with advanced monotherapies in ulcerative colitis (UC). We used longitudinal whole-blood transcriptomics to characterize molecular compatibility, therapy-specific effects with potential complementarity, and potential cross-therapy resistance correction.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Adults with endoscopically active UC (Mayo endoscopic sub-score [MES] ≥2) initiating anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF), vedolizumab (VDZ), tofacitinib (TFC), or ustekinumab (UST) underwent whole-blood RNA sequencing at baseline and/or post-induction endoscopy. Endoscopic improvement was defined as MES ≤1. Response- and resistance-associated signatures were compared across differentially expressed genes (DEGs), enriched biological processes, and transcription factor (TF) activities. Potential correction required an opposite-direction response effect ≥50% of the corresponding resistance effect.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Among 314 treatment cycles from 234 patients (514 RNA samples), 47.6% achieved endoscopic improvement. Anti-TNF, VDZ, and TFC response-associated signatures were broadly concordant across molecular layers while retaining therapy-specific features. UST showed no significant response-associated DEGs but retained distinct pathway- and TF-level signals. In parallel, cross-therapy response-associated effects opposed resistance-associated features, with correlations of −0.89 to −1.00 for genes, −0.64 to −0.84 for biological processes, and −0.52 to −0.76 for TF activities. Against anti-TNF resistance, VDZ met the potential-correction criterion for 70% of DEGs, 79% of biological processes, and 96% of TF activities, including all 20 resistance processes with the largest absolute enrichment scores.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Whole-blood transcriptomics identified compatible shared responses, therapy-specific molecular effects, and potential cross-therapy compensation. These findings generate hypotheses for biologically informed combination strategies requiring prospective validation, rather than evidence of efficacy or synergy.

  Nearest seed: Changes Over Time in the Lémann Index and the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Disability Index in a Prospective Cohort of Patients With Crohn's Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: Children with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), experience a significantly lower health-related quality of life (HRQoL) compared to healthy children. Over the past decade, advancements in IBD care may have influenced HRQoL. The aim of this study was to compare HRQoL between two independent pediatric IBD cohorts from distinct time periods. METHODS: Two pediatric IBD cohorts, T1 cohort (2014-2015) and T2 cohort (2022-2024), completed the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory 4.0 (PedsQL) and the IBD-specific IMPACT-III questionnaire. Total and scale scores of both questionnaires were compared between the two cohorts. Moreover, the HRQoL of T2 was compared to the general population (GP). RESULTS: In total, 117 (T1: 72, T2: 45) patients completed both questionnaires. The PedsQL total and scale scores did not differ between T1 and T2 for both patients with CD and UC. IMPACT-III total and subscale scores showed no differences for patients with CD and the total IBD-cohort. In UC, the IMPACT-III total score and its subdomain social functioning were higher in T2 (p = 0.049 and p = 0.013, respectively). Children with IBD in the T2 cohort scored lower in the physical (p = 0.001) and school (p = 0.001) functioning subscales when compared to the GP cohort. CONCLUSION: This study shows no overall difference in HRQoL for pediatric patients with IBD over the past decade but indicates an improvement in HRQoL for children with UC.

  Nearest seed: EXPRESS: Inflammatory bowel disease and cardiovascular risk: A meta-analysis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > UNLABELLED: Although inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) may lead to an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, evidence regarding cardiovascular and lipid parameters in childhood is limited. We compared vascular markers, blood pressure and lipid levels between patients with pediatric or childhood-onset IBD and controls. This study was performed according to the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and adhered to PRISMA guidelines. A search of major databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus and Cochrane Library) was conducted from inception until April 24, 2025 (CRD420251039588). The study quality was evaluated using the modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; statistical analysis was conducted using R software. Reactive hyperemia index (RHI) was lower in three studies, involving 109 participants (MD - 0.25, 95% CI - 0.48 to - 0.02; p = 0.03). No statistically significant difference in carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) was detected based on six studies. Primary systolic blood pressure (SBP) pooling across ten studies, was also not statistically significant (MD 0.84 mmHg, 95% CI - 0.67 to 2.35; p = 0.28). The exclusion of one influential study resulted in a small difference in SBP (MD 1.89 mmHg, 95% CI 1.17 to 2.61). Primary analyses detected no statistically significant differences in diastolic blood pressure or lipid levels. CONCLUSIONS: Lower RHI suggests possible early endothelial dysfunction, but the evidence is preliminary. No difference was observed in cIMT, DBP and lipid levels between the two groups. Future prospective cohort studies should investigate the longitudinal changes in these parameters, in order to elucidate whether early alterations lead, in the long-term, to increased cardiovascular morbidity in adulthood. WHAT IS KNOWN: • Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is characterized by persistent gastrointestinal inflammation and immune activation. • IBD may lead to an elevated risk of cardiovascular disease. However, limited data exist regarding cardiovascular parameters on pediatric IBD patients. WHAT IS NEW: • Lower Reactive Hyperemia Index (RHI) suggests possible endothelial dysfunction; carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) and lipid levels remain inconclusive. • No statistically significant difference in cIMT was detected between participants with IBD and controls; primary systolic blood pressure (SBP) pooling was not statistically significant; a small increase appeared only after exclusion of one study.

RECENT FINDINGS: The importance of vaccination for prevention of VPDs has been highlighted in multiple societal guideline updates. Herein we have highlighted risks of infection, the contributing role of immunosuppressive therapy, and comprehensively reviewed recommendations for and approach to vaccination.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Patient portal messaging carries a substantial share of outpatient communication, and rising message volume has slowed clinical response to urgent concerns embedded in routine traffic. Whether large language models (LLMs) can triage these messages, and whether locally hosted open-weight models can match cloud-hosted ones, remains unclear. We evaluated six LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, GPT-5 Mini, Llama 4 Scout, GPT-OSS 20B, Gemma 3 27B) against a physician reference standard in three cohorts: 500 primary care messages enriched for urgent cases, 503 epilepsy clinic messages from the same health system, and 300 patient-authored messages from public platforms outside it. Under Majority adjudication in primary care, GPT-5 missed no urgent message, and four of six models, including one deployable on-premises, reached sensitivity 0.97 or higher and negative predictive value 0.98 or higher. Rank ordering held across cohorts. Both cloud-hosted and on-premise LLMs can rule out urgent messages with high sensitivity, though specificity remained limited.
