- [ ] **Filgotinib in Ulcerative Colitis: Long-term Outcomes and Treatment Positioning.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Zhong H, Ma X, Jiao Y
  Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE | 2026-10-16
  [10.3791/74528](https://doi.org/10.3791/74528) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Review Article: Beyond the Therapeutic Ceiling in Inflammatory Bowel Disease—Mechanism‐Based Positioning and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

  > [!abstract]-
  > The therapeutic landscape of ulcerative colitis (UC) has expanded substantially with biologics and small-molecule agents, yet durable disease control, safety, and treatment sequencing remain important challenges. Filgotinib is an oral inhibitor with preferential activity for Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) relative to JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2. The pivotal SELECTION trial established induction and maintenance efficacy, and long-term extension and real-world studies have reported sustained clinical and patient-reported benefits in selected populations. Direct head-to-head trials remain scarce; most comparative evidence derives from network meta-analyses and nonrandomized cohorts that are vulnerable to heterogeneity and channeling bias. Rare adverse outcomes also remain imprecisely estimated. This narrative review integrates pivotal trials, long-term extension data, full peer-reviewed real-world studies, comparative analyses, guidelines, and regulatory information. It emphasizes cautious interpretation of JAK1 preference, clinically relevant risk assessment and monitoring, reproductive considerations, and geographic variation in therapeutic positioning. Filgotinib should be considered one option within individualized treatment sequencing rather than a uniformly preferred therapy.

- [ ] **Aucubin Alleviates Ulcerative Colitis in Mice by Repairing the Intestinal Barrier and Modulating the Microbiota-Metabolism Axis.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Bi Y, Chen P, Cheng S
  Food science & nutrition | 2026-10-01
  [10.1002/fsn3.72436](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.72436) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Effects of cuproptosis and its application in inflammatory bowel disease (Review).

  > [!abstract]-
  > Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease with a complex pathogenesis, necessitating the development of safe and effective therapeutic interventions. This study aimed to investigate the protective effects and underlying mechanisms of Aucubin (Auc) against dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced UC in mice. Using a DSS-induced colitis model, we systematically evaluated the multifaceted effects of Auc by assessing the Disease Activity Index (DAI), colon length, histopathological changes, immunohistochemical staining, ELISA results, proteomic profiles, 16S rRNA sequencing data, and untargeted metabolomic analyses. Compared with the DSS group, Aucubin high-dose (Auc-H, 100 mg/kg) treatment significantly attenuated DSS-induced body weight loss, DAI score elevation, and colon shortening (all p < 0.05). The therapeutic efficacy of Auc-H was comparable to that of the positive control drug 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA). Mechanistically, Auc restored intestinal barrier integrity by stimulating mucin 2 (Muc2) secretion and upregulating the expression of zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) and Occludin, which in turn significantly reduced serum lipopolysaccharide (LPS) concentrations (p < 0.001). Proteomic analysis identified 2180 DSS-induced differentially expressed proteins, of which 679 were significantly counter-regulated by Auc-H, mainly involving the complement and coagulation cascades and immune-inflammatory pathways. Furthermore, gut microbiota analysis showed that Auc promoted the growth of beneficial bacteria, improving microbial balance. Untargeted metabolomics further revealed that Auc-H significantly restored the levels of 120 DSS-induced differential metabolites, including 86 upregulated and 34 downregulated metabolites, among which were protective tryptophan-derived indole metabolites (p < 0.05). Consequently, Aucubin exhibits significant clinical potential in alleviating UC via a multifaceted mechanism. These effects include reduced inflammation, restored epithelial barrier integrity, and modulated microbial metabolism. These findings provide crucial experimental evidence supporting the potential application of Auc as a preventive therapy for UC.

  Nearest seed: AI-generated guidance for non-specialist physicians managing inflammatory bowel disease: expert evaluation and case-based testing of GPT-4 and DeepSeek.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Large Language Models (LLMs) may support clinicians by facilitating information management, but their usability in real-world oncology practice remains understudied. This study evaluated the workflow performance of Artificial Intelligence-based First Consultation Preparation (AI-FCP), generating structured summaries from unstructured clinical documentation, co-created with end users. In a prospective single‑group pre‑post design, nine clinicians completed a person-level baseline, followed by 33 consultations with AI-FCP. Case numbers varied by clinic scheduling and enrollment timing, potentially introducing survivorship bias. Usability was evaluated using validated questionnaires and study-specific instruments. Post AI-FCP, overall perceived workload (NASA-TLX) was 28.9% lower than baseline (Hodges-Lehmann estimate difference −12.3; 95% CI –21.7 to –3.3), with self-reported average time savings of 3.26 min (SD = 2.65) per case. Preparation succeeded in 84.9% of consultations; 63.6% of summaries were complete. These exploratory findings suggest clinicians perceived lower workload and reported improved workflow efficiency with AI-FCP. Further research is needed to determine generalizability.
