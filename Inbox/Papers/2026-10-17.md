- [ ] **Defining and measuring disease burden in Crohn's disease: a systematic literature review.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Radford SJ, Olsen BJ, Moran G
  Scandinavian journal of gastroenterology | 2026-10-16
  [10.1080/00365521.2026.2745974](https://doi.org/10.1080/00365521.2026.2745974) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Changes Over Time in the Lémann Index and the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Disability Index in a Prospective Cohort of Patients With Crohn's Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic, progressive inflammatory bowel disease with evolving disease behaviour, cumulative bowel damage, and long-term impact on patients and health systems. While treat-to-target strategies focus on inflammatory control, disease burden extends beyond active inflammation to include functional, psychosocial, and economic effects. This review aimed to synthesise how disease burden in CD is defined and measured across the literature. METHODS: A systematic literature review and narrative synthesis were conducted (PROSPERO protocol CRD420251182987). MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, and PsycINFO were searched on 3 November 2025 for primary studies involving adults with CD that referenced disease burden. Screening and data extraction were performed in duplicate. Study quality was appraised using Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) tools and considered during narrative synthesis. RESULTS: Twenty-eight studies from diverse regions and study designs were included. Disease burden was variably defined, encompassing clinical activity, cumulative complications, patient-reported symptoms, functional and societal impact, healthcare utilisation, and population-level epidemiology. Measures clustered into five domains: disease activity and inflammation, patient-reported burden, functional and societal impact, healthcare utilisation and treatment burden, and population-level burden. These domains were typically assessed in isolation, and no study integrated objective clinical measures with patient-reported or quality-of-life outcomes. CONCLUSIONS: Disease burden in Crohn's disease is inconsistently defined and measured, reflecting its multidimensional nature but highlighting the absence of an integrated framework. Development of longitudinal, multidimensional tools combining biological, patient-reported, functional, and economic measures is needed to better capture disease burden and support holistic clinical care.

  Nearest seed: Application of large language models for assigning clear cell likelihood score v2.0 from free–text MRI reports: a feasibility study

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Objectives
  > To evaluate whether large language model assistance improves radiologists’ agreement with a text-based reference standard in inferring the modified American Heart Association carotid plaque classification from free-text MRI reports, and whether a lightweight model can perform well under criteria-guided prompting.
  >
  >
  > Materials and methods
  > This retrospective study included 433 patients from three institutions. In a report-based reader study, four radiologists classified MRI reports with and without large language model assistance. In standalone evaluation, a rule-based baseline and 10 models were compared under naive and criteria-guided prompting. The reference standard was expert consensus based on the report text.
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > Among 433 patients (mean age, 65.9 years ± 13.4; 248 males), AI assistance improved six-class agreement with the text-based reference standard. At the vessel level, junior radiologists improved from 61.75% to 83.12% (Qwen3-8B) and 87.75% (DeepSeek-R1); senior radiologists from 77.00% to 87.50% and 92.25%, respectively (all Holm-corrected
  > p
  > < 0.001). Patient-level agreement increased from 41.0% to 78.8% (junior) and from 59.8% to 87.8% (senior) with DeepSeek-R1. Agreement increased (quadratic-weighted
  > κ
  > : 0.231 without AI vs 0.775 with DeepSeek-R1). In standalone evaluation, criteria-guided prompting improved Qwen3-8B agreement from 38.91% to 81.52% (95% CI: 78.87–84.06%), remaining 5.89 percentage points below DeepSeek-R1 (87.41%; 95% CI: 85.10–89.61%).
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > In this report-based classification setting, large language model assistance improved radiologists’ agreement with the text-based reference standard, with larger gains in junior radiologists. When prompts explicitly encoded AHA criteria, an 8B local candidate model performed well, supporting its potential use as a standardization aid for text-based workflows.
  >
  >
  > Key Points
  >
  >
  > Question
  >
  > Report-based modified AHA classification of carotid plaques is experience-dependent and difficult to standardize, as mapping multi-sequence imaging findings to classification conclusions imposes substantial cognitive load.
  >
  >
  >
  > Findings
  >
  > Large language model assistance improved agreement with a text-based reference standard and raised inter-reader consistency (weighted κ 0.231–0.775), narrowing the junior–senior gap.
  >
  >
  >
  > Critical relevance statement
  >
  > Embedding explicit AHA criteria in the prompt let an 8B model approach 671B-model agreement (38.91–81.52%), supporting standardized text-based workflows without fine-tuning.

- [ ] **A multi-stage validation framework for trustworthy large-scale clinical information extraction using large language models**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Maria Mahbub, Gregory M. Dams, Ioana Danciu
  npj Digital Medicine | 2026-10-16
  [10.1038/s41746-026-03341-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03341-7) | Score: 0.94
  Nearest seed: The evidence challenge facing large language models in medicine

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Large language models (LLMs) show promise for extracting clinically meaningful information from unstructured health records, yet real-world deployment is constrained by limited scalable, trustworthy validation approaches. Conventional evaluation relies on annotation-intensive reference standards and incomplete structured data, limiting population-scale use. We propose a multi-stage validation framework for LLM-based clinical information extraction that enables rigorous assessment under weak supervision by integrating complementary validation strategies. The framework combines prompt calibration, rule-based filtering, semantic grounding, judge-LLM evaluation, expert review, and predictive validity analysis to quantify uncertainty without exhaustive manual annotation. We applied this framework to extract substance use disorder (SUD) diagnoses across 11 substance categories from 919,783 clinical notes. Rule-based filtering and semantic grounding removed 14.59% of LLM-positive extractions that were unsupported or structurally implausible. For high-uncertainty cases, the judge LLM showed substantial agreement with subject matter expert. Using judge evaluations as references, the primary LLM achieved an F1 score of 0.80. LLM-extracted SUD diagnoses predicted subsequent engagement in SUD specialty care more accurately than structured-data baselines. By unifying complementary validation strategies, this work presents a generalizable human-in-the-loop framework for establishing the trustworthiness of LLM-based clinical information extraction at scale while minimizing the need for exhaustive manual annotation.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Symptom burden and impaired quality of life (QoL) are major challenges for patients with cancer. Ecological Momentary Interventions (EMIs) offer real-time, context-sensitive, and personalized support, enabling more precise management of patient needs. To explore the effectiveness of EMIs in improving symptoms and QoL in patients with cancer, a systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted. Following PRISMA 2020, a comprehensive search was conducted in nine databases from inception to December 8, 2025. The RoB 2 tool and GRADE were used to assess risk of bias and certainty of evidence, respectively. The R (version 4.4.1) and the “meta” package were used for statistical analysis. A total of 17 studies involving 4,160 randomized participants were included. The results suggested that EMIs may improve depression in patients with cancer, but were not associated with significant improvements in fatigue, pain severity, pain interference, sleep disturbance, anxiety, or QoL. Given the limited certainty and robustness of the available evidence, further adequately powered, allocation-concealed randomized controlled trials with blinded outcome assessment, where feasible, are needed to strengthen the evidence base for EMIs in oncology care.
