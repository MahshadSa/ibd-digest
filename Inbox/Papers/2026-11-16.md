- [ ] **Ampelopsis grossedentata restores the homeostasis of Th17/Treg cells to mitigate DSS-induced ulcerative colitis through TLR1-2/NF-κB signaling pathway.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Xu J, Zhao D, Xu Y
  Journal of ethnopharmacology | 2026-11-15
  [10.1016/j.jep.2026.122618](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2026.122618) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Multi-Omics-Driven Network Pharmacology and Experimental Validation Reveal the Molecular Mechanism of Bupi Yichang Pill in Treating Ulcerative Colitis Through Regulation of the PI3K/Akt/IKK/NF-κB Pathway.

  > [!abstract]-
  > ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Ampelopsis grossedentata (AG), a traditional medicine from Southwest China, is frequently used to treat inflammation, anemopyretic cold, pharyngitis, hepatitis, and dysentery. Pharmacological studies confirmed that primary active constituents of AG, including flavonoids, profoundly affect anti-inflammatory processes and immunomodulation. However, the therapeutic effect and mechanisms of AG for ulcerative colitis (UC) are still unclear. AIM OF THE STUDY: This study aimed to elucidate the mechanisms of AG treatment of UC. MATERIALS AND METHODS: UPLC-MS and network pharmacology were employed to identify the active components of AG and their potential anti-UC mechanisms. A 4% dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced UC mouse model was established for assessing therapeutic efficacy of AG based on body weight, disease activity index, colon length, and histopathology of colon tissue. RT-qPCR, immunofluorescence assays and D-lactate levels were used to evaluate the inflammatory state and epithelial barrier integrity. Transcriptomic, proteomic, and flow cytometry analyses were performed to examine the underlying mechanisms. Molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA), western blot and the TLR1/2 knockdown assays were used to identify and verify candidate targets. RESULTS: Five flavonoids in AG were characterized via UPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS, with dihydromyricetin, taxifolin, myricitrin, and myricetin quantified by HPLC-DAD. Network pharmacology revealed 38 UC-related targets, enriched in IL-17/TNF signaling pathways. In vivo, AG alleviated UC-related symptoms, improved colon pathology, reduced the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6), upregulated tight junction proteins (ZO-1, claudin 5), and inhibited the nuclear translocation of NF-κB (P65 and P50). Transcriptomic, proteomic, and flow cytometry analyses indicated that AG regulated Th17/Treg balance via the NF-κB signaling pathway. Structure-based molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, CETSA western blot and the TLR1/2 knockdown assays revealed that AG may act through the TLR1-TLR2 heterodimer receptor. CONCLUSION: AG effectively attenuates DSS-induced UC, potentially by restoring Th17/Treg balance and inhibiting NF-κB signaling through the TLR1-TLR2 heterodimer. AG is thus a therapeutic candidate for UC treatment.

- [ ] **Probiotics and Prebiotics in Restoring Gastrointestinal Microbiota Dysbiosis: Emerging Alternatives to Antibiotic Therapy.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Goa T, Wolde T, Sahilu E
  International journal of food science | 2026-01-01
  [10.1155/ijfo/6956918](https://doi.org/10.1155/ijfo/6956918) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Bioengineering a Probiotic to Bloom During Colonic Inflammation Promotes Reliable Efficacy in Translational Models of Colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > The human gastrointestinal (GI) microbiota is a complex and dynamic microbial ecosystem that plays a fundamental role in maintaining host metabolism, immune homeostasis, intestinal barrier integrity, and protection against pathogenic microorganisms. Disruption of this microbial community, known as gut dysbiosis, has been implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous GI and systemic diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), Clostridioides difficile infection, obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, colorectal cancer, and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease. Although antibiotics remain indispensable for the treatment of bacterial infections, their indiscriminate use frequently disrupts gut microbial composition, reduces microbial diversity, promotes opportunistic pathogen overgrowth, and accelerates the emergence of antimicrobial resistance. These limitations have stimulated increasing interest in microbiota-targeted interventions as safer and more sustainable therapeutic strategies. Probiotics and prebiotics have emerged as promising approaches for restoring gut microbial homeostasis through complementary mechanisms. Probiotics inhibit pathogen colonization, enhance intestinal barrier function, produce antimicrobial metabolites, and modulate host immune responses. Prebiotics selectively stimulate the growth and metabolic activity of beneficial microorganisms, promoting the production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) that support intestinal and systemic health. Increasing evidence from experimental studies, randomized clinical trials, and meta-analyses demonstrates that these microbiota-directed interventions can reduce antibiotic-associated diarrhea, improve symptoms of IBD and IBS, enhance Helicobacter pylori eradication, decrease recurrence of C. difficile infection, and contribute to the management of several metabolic disorders. Despite these promising findings, important challenges remain regarding strain specificity, optimal dosage, treatment duration, product standardization, regulatory oversight, and interindividual variability in treatment response. Emerging advances in metagenomics, metabolomics, artificial intelligence, and precision medicine are facilitating the development of personalized microbiome-based therapies that may further enhance clinical outcomes. This review critically summarizes the current understanding of GI microbiota dysbiosis, discusses the mechanisms through which probiotics and prebiotics restore microbial balance, evaluates their clinical applications as alternatives or adjuncts to antibiotic therapy, and highlights existing challenges and future research directions for microbiome-based therapeutics.

  [10.1016/j.smrv.2026.102383](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2026.102383) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: From signals to systems: the epigenetic-microbiome-mitochondrial axis in IBD pathogenesis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), is increasingly recognized as being associated with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). Recent cohort studies and meta-analyses identify IBD as an independent risk factor for OSA, supporting the need for OSA screening in patients with IBD and management of modifiable risk factors. However, these associations come primarily from observational studies with virtually no confirmatory mechanistic studies. Further, the available clinical and experimental evidence of an association between OSA and IBD is highly heterogeneous and comes from multidisciplinary sources, suggesting that the association may reflect multiple shared biological mechanisms rather than simple disease coexistence. IBD-associated inflammation, epithelial barrier dysfunction, immune dysregulation, gut dysbiosis, and OSA-associated intermittent hypoxia, oxidative stress, immune activation, gut barrier dysfunction, and gut dysbiosis appear to be the key reciprocal processes establishing a self-reinforcing cycle that links intestinal and respiratory pathophysiology. This theoretical review synthesizes multidisciplinary heterogeneous evidence across clinical, experimental, and mechanistic studies to develop the hypothesis-generating Gut Barrier-Microbiota-Immune-Hypoxia (GBMIH) framework. This hypothesis-generating systems theory highlights intestinal barrier disruption, dysbiosis, immune activation, and intermittent hypoxia as interconnected biological nodes that may mediate this bidirectional relationship and define future biomarker and therapeutic development.

- [ ] **In Vivo Ankle Tendon Quantification Using Ultrashort Echo Time Magnetization Transfer Imaging**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Georg C. Feuerriegel, Damian Mialkowskyj, Reto Sutter
  Investigative Radiology | 2026-11-16
  [10.1097/rli.0000000000001322](https://doi.org/10.1097/rli.0000000000001322) | Score: 0.92
  Nearest seed: ESR Essentials: susceptibility-weighted imaging—practice recommendations by the European Society for Magnetic Resonance in Medicine and Biology

  > [!abstract]-
  > Objectives:
  > To quantitatively assess in vivo ankle tendons using less magic angle-sensitive 3-dimensional ultrashort echo time magnetization transfer (UTE MT) imaging and to compare its intersubject variability with UTE T2* mapping in healthy volunteers.
  >
  >
  > Materials and Methods:
  > Thirty healthy volunteer ankles were examined on a 3T MRI system using a standardized clinical protocol with additional 3D UTE MT and 3D multi-echo UTE T2* sequences (acquisition times: 4:16 and 9:52 minutes, respectively). Nine major ankle tendons were semi-automatically segmented to extract quantitative UTE MTR and UTE T2* values for each tendon. Variability for each sequence was assessed using the coefficient of variation (CV), and differences in CV were evaluated primarily with a paired bootstrap of the CV ratio and secondarily with a modified signed log-likelihood ratio (MSLR) test, both with Holm correction. Within-tendon angular dependence was assessed using local tendon fiber angles relative to B0.
  >
  >
  > Results:
  >
  > Absolute quantitative values differed between tendons, with the Achilles tendon demonstrating the lowest T2* values (2.15±0.43 ms) and highest MTR values (0.44±0.08), whereas smaller positional tendons showed higher T2* and lower MTR values. UTE MTR demonstrated lower overall variability compared with UTE T2* values across the 9 assessed tendons (overall CV UTE MTR: 18.0%±4.2% vs. UTE T2*: 33.7%±8.9%, mean±SD, n=9). In the paired bootstrap analysis with Holm correction, UTE MTR showed significantly lower variability than UTE T2* in 6 of 9 tendons, with the largest differences in the extensor digitorum longus, peroneus brevis, and flexor hallucis longus tendons (T2*/MTR CV ratio, 2.74, 2.60, and 2.48; all Holm-adjusted
  > P
  > ≤0.015); MSLR results were consistent. Between 0 degree and the magic angle, UTE T2* increased by 79% (95% CI: 64%-95%), whereas UTE MTR decreased by 9% (95% CI: 7%-11%). UTE MTR consistently demonstrated reduced dispersion and narrower value distributions compared with UTE T2*.
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusions:
  > In vivo ankle tendon quantification using UTE MT is feasible within clinically acceptable acquisition times and shows significantly lower intersubject variability than UTE T2* mapping in most tendons, with reduced susceptibility to the magic angle effect. UTE MT represents a promising approach for quantitative assessment of tendon structure in vivo.
