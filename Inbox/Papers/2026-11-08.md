  > Abstract
  >
  > Background & Aims
  > We conducted a retrospective cohort study comparing the risk of perianal penetrating complications with advanced therapies (AT) in patients with non-penetrating Crohn’s disease (CD).
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Using an administrative claims database (OptumLabs Data Warehouse), we identified adult patients with CD who initiated treatment with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) antagonists, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, IL-23p19 antagonists or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors between 2016 and 2022. Patients with any CD-related penetrating complication, abdominal or perianal, during the baseline 1 year and up to 3 months post AT initiation were excluded.   >
  >
  > Results
  > We identified 10 482 patients with non-penetrating CD who initiated TNF antagonists (n = 4463), vedolizumab (n = 2372), ustekinumab (n = 3027), IL-23p19 antagonists (n = 480) and JAK inhibitors (n = 140), of whom 360 (3.4%) developed perianal penetrating complications over a mean follow-up of 18.511.2 months.   >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Among patients with non-penetrating CD starting an AT, TNF antagonists and ustekinumab were associated with a lower risk of developing perianal penetrating complications compared to vedolizumab.

  > BACKGROUND: Drug-induced enterocolitis (DIC) consists of a heterogeneous group of gastrointestinal inflammatory disorders with clinical, endoscopic, and histological features that often overlap with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Given the increasing recognition of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced enterocolitis and the lack of standardized management algorithms, the European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) convened a multidisciplinary panel to review DIC, with a particular focus on ICI-induced disease.   >
  >
  > Methods
  > Patients aged ≥16 years with newly diagnosed CD and who had no prior exposure to advanced therapies were enrolled.   >
