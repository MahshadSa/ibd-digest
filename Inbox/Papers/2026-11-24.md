  Nearest seed: Trained immunity: An unexplored component of IBD pathogenesis

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Western-style dietary patterns are associated with an increased risk for the development of inflammatory bowel disease. However, the relative impact of consuming a Western diet in early life on susceptibility to chronic intestinal inflammation is not known. Using the interleukin (IL)-10–deficient murine model of spontaneous colitis, we aimed to determine the effect of diet timing on disease course, T cell responses and gut microbiome composition.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A Western-type diet (WD) was fed to conventional IL-10–deficient mice either in early life or in late adolescence. Colitis progression was characterized by antimicrobial peptide levels, gene expression patterns, and histopathological features. Gut-related lymphocyte populations were phenotyped by flow cytometry and fecal microbiome composition was determined by 16S rRNA sequencing.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > WD feeding in early life but not in late adolescence led to an accelerated onset of colitis in IL-10–deficient mice. Both prior to and during disease, early life WD-fed mice presented with an altered pool of regulatory T cells and T helper subsets, in line with the early life WD feeding window overlapping with the establishment of colon memory CD4 T cell populations. A few long-term changes in the fecal microbiome correlated with colitis severity; the most prominent of which, an increase in Flavonifractor plautii, was also related to Crohn’s disease risk in a predisease cohort.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Exposure to dietary risk factors in early life can have long-term effects on the gut immune-microbial landscape and disease susceptibility. Future studies investigating the effect of dietary components on inflammatory bowel disease risk should prioritize the inclusion of early age groups.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND AIM: Most of the available studies assessed relapse rate in the 12 months after anti-TNF withdrawal in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). We assessed long-term outcomes after discontinuation of a biological agent in patients with IBD. CASE: This was a retrospective, single-center study. All adult (>18 years) patients, who have stopped biologic treatment for at least one year were included. Long-term relapse was defined as the restart of an advanced therapy for IBD more than one year after treatment discontinuation. A total of 57 patients were included in this study. Majority of patients had Crohn's disease (78.9%). The median duration of follow-up for the entire cohort was 64.6 [51.8] months. At time of treatment withdrawal, 77.2% were treated with anti TNF, 17.5% with vedolizumab, and 5.3% with ustekinumab. The median time between discontinuation of treatment and relapse was 23.5 [25.7] months. No predictors could be identified. Of these 38 patients retreated for relapse, 85.7% were in clinical remission at 6 months with anti-TNF, 80% with ustekinumab, and 50% with vedolizumab. CONCLUSION: Two thirds of patients with IBD experienced relapse and a quarter underwent surgery more than one year after discontinuation of a biological agent. These results suggest that biologics should not be discontinued or that a tight monitoring should be initiated in case of withdrawal.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Infliximab (IFX) is highly effective for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD); however, anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) driven by immunogenicity can compromise treatment safety and efficacy. As ADA onset and persistence vary among individuals, short-term clinical trials may not fully capture long-term real-world immunogenicity. The aim of this study was to characterize ADA development in Japanese patients with IFX-treated IBD using long-term real-world data. Based on each patient's latest serum sample, the prevalence of ADA was 4.0%. In ADA-positive patients, serum IFX concentrations were below the lower limit of quantification in almost all samples.
