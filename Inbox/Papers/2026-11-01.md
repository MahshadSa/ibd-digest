  Nearest seed: Metal-free nitroxide radical-based theranostic agent for MRI-guided therapy of ulcerative colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Ulcerative colitis (UC) remains challenging to diagnose and treat because of the limited sensitivity of current imaging techniques, the safety concerns associated with metal-based magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) contrast agents, and the insufficient efficacy of conventional anti-inflammatory therapies. Here, we report a metal-free theranostic nanoliposome (A@Clip) that integrates nitroxide radical-containing phospholipids (TEMPO-lipids) and 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) into a biocompatible liposomal platform for MRI-guided therapy of UC. The liposomal architecture enhances the T1 MRI performance of TEMPO radicals while enabling efficient reactive oxygen species scavenging and anti-inflammatory drug delivery. A@Clip exhibits excellent biocompatibility, promotes cellular uptake, and effectively suppresses oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in vitro. In a murine colitis model, A@Clip enables sensitive visualization of inflamed colon tissue and markedly alleviates disease progression, intestinal injury, inflammatory responses, and gut microbiota dysbiosis without detectable systemic toxicity. This work establishes a metal-free nanotheranostic strategy for integrating non-invasive MRI with anti-inflammatory therapy and provides a versatile platform for the precision management of inflammatory diseases.

  Nearest seed: Sarcopenia as a prognostic marker in inflammatory bowel disease: Implications for endoscopic outcomes and loss of response to biologic therapy—A systematic review with narrative synthesis

  > [!abstract]-
  > INTRODUCTION AND AIM: The development and exacerbation of fatigue in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) have not been widely studied. Our aim was to evaluate the association of sarcopenia, anthropometric parameters, and body composition with fatigue, in patients with IBD. METHODS: The Inflammatory Bowel Disease-Fatigue questionnaire was applied to assess fatigue. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated, and body composition was evaluated through Biodynamics® tetrapolar bioimpedance. RESULTS: In 106 patients with IBD, the prevalence of fatigue was 83%, regardless of disease activity (p > 0.05). Patients who were employed (86.4% vs 61.1%, p = 0.011), presented with ulcerative colitis (UC) (58% vs 22.2%, p = 0.006), or with Crohn's disease (CD) diagnosed between 17 and 40 years of age (73% vs 42.9%, p = 0.045), had a greater prevalence of fatigue. There was no statistically significant association between fatigue and sarcopenia or fatigue and body composition (p > 0.05). The regression analysis indicated no risk of fatigue for low weight, overweight, lean mass, or phase angle, or for sarcopenia criteria, hand grip strength, or gait speed (p > 0.05). CONCLUSIONS: The prevalence of fatigue was high in patients with IBD, predominantly in adults, even in the disease remission phase, especially in patients with UC and CD (the latter diagnosed between 17 and 40 years of age). Body composition was not associated with fatigue.

  Nearest seed: Chronotherapy in Inflammatory Bowel Disease: Biological Rationale, Human Evidence, and a Clinical Implementation Framework.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Sleep disturbance is highly prevalent in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), affecting up to 70% of patients with active disease and 40% in remission, and is associated with worse clinical and patient-reported outcomes. Understanding of the multifactorial drivers of sleep disturbances in IBD remains limited, and as such, there is an absence of clear guidance for evaluating and managing sleep problems in this population. We aimed to identify factors associated with sleep disturbance and characterize patient beliefs, self-management strategies, and interest in sleep-focused testing and treatments. METHODS: Adults with Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis completed a cross-sectional survey assessing disease activity, sleep quality, stress, chronotype, fatigue, and mood. A study-specific questionnaire evaluated sleep-related beliefs, behaviors, and treatment interest. Descriptive analyses characterized sleep patterns and behaviors, and multivariable regression identified factors associated with poor sleep. RESULTS: Among 204 participants, 76.5% reported poor sleep quality and 55.9% had clinically significant sleep disturbance, which was more common in active disease than remission (80.9% vs 46.0%, P < .001). Active disease (odds ratio [OR], 4.31), higher perceived stress (OR, 1.21), and anxiety (OR, 2.63) were associated with increased odds of clinically significant sleep disturbance, while intermediate (OR, 0.16) and morning chronotypes (OR, 0.07) were associated with lower odds of clinically significant sleep disturbance compared with evening chronotypes. Most participants reported self-management behaviors related to sleep, with widespread use of sleep hygiene and over-the-counter strategies but minimal exposure to cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (0.5%). Interest in sleep-focused interventions was high, particularly for wearable monitoring, home-based testing, and behavioral and light-based therapies. CONCLUSION: Sleep disturbance in inflammatory bowel disease is common and reflects interacting inflammatory, psychological, and circadian factors. Use of evidence-based therapies remains extremely limited, highlighting a substantial gap in care.
