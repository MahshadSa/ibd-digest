- [ ] **Mechanisms and therapeutic perspectives of mucosal glycosylation-innate immunity crosstalk in inflammatory bowel disease (Review).**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Liu X, Zhang T, Han X
  International journal of molecular medicine | 2026-12-01
  [10.3892/ijmm.2026.6032](https://doi.org/10.3892/ijmm.2026.6032) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Ulcerative Colitis: Advances in Pathogenesis, Biomarkers, and Therapeutic Strategies.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a group of chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract, driven by a complex interplay of genetic susceptibility, environmental exposures, intestinal microbiota dysbiosis and immune dysregulation. In recent years, mucosal glycosylation has emerged as a fundamental molecular determinant of intestinal barrier integrity and function and is increasingly recognized as a key regulatory interface linking the microbiota with host immune responses. Concurrently, the innate immune system serves an important role in mucosal defense and inflammatory responses through pattern recognition receptor‑mediated signaling, and its aberrant activation represents a key driver of persistent inflammation in IBD. The present review systematically summarizes the bidirectional regulatory crosstalk between mucosal glycosylation and innate immunity. The mechanistic roles of glycosylation modifications are highlighted in pathogen recognition, inflammatory signal transduction, inflammasome activation and maintenance of mucosal barrier integrity. Conversely, it is discussed how innate immune activation regulates glycan biosynthesis and remodeling through inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress and major signaling pathways, thereby reshaping the mucosal glycome in disease contexts. On this basis, the dynamic alterations of glycosylation‑innate immunity interactions across different stages of IBD, including disease initiation, progression and relapse, are delineated, emphasizing their important role in sustaining chronic inflammation and driving tissue remodeling. In addition, emerging therapeutic strategies targeting glycosylation pathways, glycan‑lectin interactions, mucosal barrier restoration and microbiota modulation are summarized. The potential of glycomics, multi‑omics integration and glyco‑immune biomarkers in disease stratification and precision medicine are also discussed. By integrating perspectives from glycobiology and immunology, the present review aims to provide a conceptual framework and novel directions for mechanistic research and targeted therapy in IBD.

- [ ] **Prevalence and epidemiologic features of adult upper gastrointestinal Crohn’s disease over the last 30 years: a scoping review**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Daniel Lupas, Navneet Natt, Rocio Sedano
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-10-30
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag168](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag168) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Impact of Upper Gastrointestinal Involvement on Clinical Outcomes in Paediatric Inflammatory Bowel Disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Crohn’s disease (CD) is an inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract. Although upper gastrointestinal (UGI) manifestations are well recognized in pediatrics, UGI-CD in adults has historically been underestimated. Methodological limitations, including selective endoscopy and inconsistent classification, have contributed to variable prevalence estimates.
  >
  >
  > Aims
  > Our goals were to summarize the prevalence of UGI-CD in adults with CD, understand demographic variations, and summarize knowledge gaps.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > We conducted a scoping review of MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL (1995–November 2025) using PRISMA-ScR and JBI methodology. Eligible studies reported adult UGI-CD prevalence or related epidemiologic data. Extracted variables included prevalence estimates, site-level involvement, and demographics. Systematic reviews were included to summarize review-level epidemiologic evidence.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > In total, 117 primary studies and eight systematic reviews were included. Across 95 cohorts with prevalence data, the median prevalence of UGI-CD was 8.8% (IQR 3.99%-14.5%). Higher rates were observed in cohorts using systematic endoscopy and biopsy, while population-based cohorts reported lower values. Isolated UGI-CD was rare, with a median prevalence of 1.37% (IQR 0.87%-3.02%). Prevalence varied geographically and ethnically, with lowest rates in South Asian cohorts and higher rates in East Asian and Middle Eastern cohorts. Site-specific reporting was inconsistent, and few studies detailed modifiers such as smoking or family history.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Reported prevalence estimates of adult UGI-CD remain highly variable due to inconsistent definitions and detection strategies. Standardized, site-specific adult definitions and systematic diagnostic protocols are needed to enable cross-study comparisons and thus improve prevalence estimates and address demographic reporting gaps.

  Barros TF, Vila Nova GG, Abreu AG
  Chemistry & biodiversity | 2026-11-01
  [10.1002/cbdv.71886](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.71886) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Propolis is a resinous, aromatic substance collected by worker bees from various plant species. Evidence suggests that propolis is a promising candidate for the treatment of ulcerative colitis due to its antioxidant properties and ability to modulate immune responses and the gut microbiota. This review aimed to analyze studies on the efficacy of propolis and its main active compounds in ulcerative colitis, highlighting mechanisms of action, safety and limitations. A literature search was conducted in the PubMed Central, ScienceDirect, and Scopus databases using the keywords "propolis" AND "colitis". Original articles presenting in vivo analyses, published in English up to June 2025, were included. Propolis extracts improved clinical and histopathological parameters, particularly by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, inflammatory cell infiltration, and restoring epithelial barrier integrity. These effects mainly involve modulation of inflammatory signaling pathways, particularly suppression of NF-κB activation, resulting in reduced expression of inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-1β, and iNOS. Propolis also reduced TLR4 expression, potentially contributing to the control of the exacerbated immune response characteristic of ulcerative colitis. These findings support the potential of propolis as an adjuvant agent in disease management; however, its clinical efficacy in humans remains to be validated in well-controlled and standardized studies.

  > Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) affect up to 40% of patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors, yet identification requires laborious, inconsistent manual chart review. Here we show that agentic artificial intelligence coupled with human verification could enhance efficiency, performance and consistency for irAE assessment. We developed and evaluated an agentic large language model system to extract the presence, temporality, severity grade, attribution and certainty of six irAE types from clinical notes.   > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Many digital health and AI initiatives show promising results in pilot settings but do not progress to sustainable services. Pilots can operate within a predefined collaboration, whereas operational services must accommodate changes in participating organizations, available resources, and analytical capabilities while preserving established governance and technical arrangements. OBJECTIVE: This study examines the pilot-to-service transition as a problem of governed federation evolution and evaluates whether Open Health can admit new forms of participation while preserving bounded capabilities, organizational sovereignty, and auditable decision evidence. METHODS: Open Health applies federated computing (FC) as a governance model separated from the analytical runtime. A governed federation context is established before execution, participants receive holder-bound capabilities, and an admission gateway produces explicit ALLOW or DENY decisions before requested operations proceed. The proof of concept uses the Flower federated learning framework and the Pathology Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology (PathMNIST) dataset from the MedMNIST collection with an initial federation comprising hospitals A and B. Mode 1A introduces hospital C as a sponsored contributor permitted to submit locally computed training updates but not invoke the resulting federated model for inference. Mode 1B examines bounded AI-agent participation, where restricted results may be transformed through policy-authorized operations into derivatives whose consumption is governed independently from the source. Evaluation considers admission conformance, signed decision evidence, analytical effects of the additional contribution, and admission overhead. RESULTS: The demonstrator admitted valid member operations and hospital C's sponsored contribution (mode 1A) while denying model consumption outside hospital C's capability, scope violations, holder-binding mismatch, and federation-envelope mismatch. Mode 1B extended the same bounded-governance pattern to AI-agent participation, admitting bounded inference and governed transformation but denying unauthorized source consumption and privileged governance access. Under matched training conditions, cancer-related macro recall increased from 50.2% in the A+B control to 67.4% in A+B+C (+17.2 percentage points), while noncancer macro recall changed from 85.5% to 86.3% (+0.8 percentage points). Recall increased from 19.5% to 39.9% for cancer-associated stroma and from 80.9% to 94.9% for colorectal adenocarcinoma epithelium. Median (IQR) full admission latency was 1.210 (1.141-1.333) ms for ALLOW, 1.195 (1.136-1.273) ms for capability-scope DENY, and 0.884 (0.837-0.921) ms for holder-binding DENY. Each evaluated request generated signed decision evidence recording the decision, reason, participant and request bindings, governance context, and related model run. CONCLUSIONS: Open Health demonstrates how a federated health-data collaboration can evolve through differentiated participation while preserving membership boundaries and bounded capability relations. The principal contribution is an executable governance boundary preserving capability relations and producing independently verifiable decision evidence separately from the federated-learning runtime. The analytical experiment showed that governed admission of an additional contributor produced a measurable effect on the intended cancer-related classes while preserving noncancer performance. The proof of concept establishes governance behavior under controlled conditions; clinical effectiveness and production-scale performance require further evaluation.

  Nearest seed: Artificial intelligence in clinical trials-state of the evidence, gaps, and next steps.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Recruiting participants for clinical trials is a challenge, often limited by inefficient methods for identifying eligible candidates. Clinical trial recruitment support systems (CTRSS) can enhance recruitment efficiency by leveraging data from electronic health records (EHR). We evaluated two CTRSS—recruIT and KAS+—to quantify their real-world impact on enrollment. Within the German MIRACUM consortium, we conducted a multicenter segmented interrupted time-series (ITS) evaluation across six university hospitals. Twenty-nine trials contributed 814 observation weeks spanning a control phase (usual practice) and an intervention phase (CTRSS active). Weekly enrollments were compared using segmented negative binomial mixed models estimating level and slope changes at implementation, complemented by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. A criteria revision covariate captured iterative refinement of computable cohort definitions. In pooled ITS analyses, we observed neither a statistically significant immediate level change nor a statistically significant post-implementation slope change in weekly enrollments. Effects were highly heterogeneous across trials and specialties; implemented eligibility logic most often relied on diagnosis, age, and visit data, while laboratory values and structured observations were seldom available. Higher values of the criteria revision covariate were associated with higher recruitment in some settings, with the most exploratory signal observed at the single KAS+ site. Despite the potential benefits of CTRSS, their effectiveness depends on careful customization and implementation. Overall, our findings suggest that CTRSS impact is context-dependent and that measurable benefit is more likely driven by iterative computable phenotyping and local workflow fit rather than a simple “switch-on” effect. Context-specific adaptation and continual revision of eligibility logic appear critical to realizing benefit. Future studies should reduce trial heterogeneity, strengthen compliance incentives, and employ granular outcomes, such as identification-to-enrollment latency, to better capture workflow-mediated effects.
