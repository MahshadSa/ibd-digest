  Nearest seed: Intestinal ultrasound for the detection of postoperative recurrence in Crohn’s disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Validated intestinal ultrasound (IUS) activity indices for assessing postoperative recurrence (POR) of Crohn's disease (CD) are lacking. The study sought to evaluate the diagnostic performance of IUS activity scores for detecting endoscopic POR and to identify optimal cutoff values. Secondary objectives included assessing the association between individual ultrasound parameters and recurrence severity. METHODS: This was a cross-sectional study of patients with CD and a prior ileocecal or right-sided colonic resection resulting in an ileocolonic anastomosis who underwent colonoscopy and IUS for POR assessment. Endoscopic recurrence was defined as a Rutgeerts score ≥ i2a. Ultrasound activity was evaluated using the Simple Ultrasound Score (Simple U.S. Score), International Bowel Ultrasound Segmental Activity Score (IBUS-SAS), Simple Ultrasound Score for Crohn's Disease (SUS-CD), and Bowel Ultrasound Score (BUSS). RESULTS: A total of 143 patients were included; 74.1% had POR. All IUS activity scores were significantly higher in patients with POR than in those without recurrence (P < .001). Diagnostic performance was favorable for all scores, with areas under the curve of 0.88 for the Simple U.S. Score, 0.83 for SUS-CD, 0.87 for IBUS-SAS, and 0.85 for BUSS. Optimal cutoffs for POR diagnosis were ≥4.5 for the Simple U.S. Score (sensitivity 85.8%, specificity 86.0%), ≥3 for SUS-CD (sensitivity 71.7%, specificity 91.9%), ≥18 for IBUS-SAS (sensitivity 86.8%, specificity 81.1%), and ≥3.1 for BUSS (sensitivity 86.7%, specificity 81.1%). For severe recurrence (≥i3), IBUS-SAS and the Simple U.S. Score achieved better areas under the curve than SUS-CD and BUSS (0.82 vs 0.84 vs 0.74 vs 0.63, respectively). Bowel wall thickness and parietal hyperemia were significantly associated with recurrence severity (P < .001). CONCLUSIONS: IUS activity scores demonstrate high diagnostic accuracy for POR and provide practical cutoff values for diagnosis and severity stratification, supporting their use as noninvasive tools in postoperative CD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND: Endoscopic remission (ER) is a key therapeutic goal in Crohn's disease (CD), yet mucosal healing is less frequent in the ileum than in the colon. While longer disease duration reduces the likelihood of ER, whether this effect differs by location is unclear. METHODS: We conducted a post hoc pooled analysis of individual patient data from four trials (EXTEND, VISIBLE-2, VERSIFY and SEAVUE) via Vivli protocol #10706. A total of 422 patients with baseline Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease (SES-CD) >3 treated with adalimumab, vedolizumab or ustekinumab and with week-52 endoscopic data were included. Patients were categorized as ileal (n = 332) or colonic (n = 303). ER was defined as SES-CD = 0 or <3. Associations between disease duration (<1, <2, <3 years vs. longer) and ER were analyzed using chi-square tests and logistic regression adjusted for treatment. RESULTS: Shorter disease duration was associated with higher ER rates overall (SES-CD <3: 35.9% vs. 24.1%, p = 0.018; adjusted OR [aOR] 0.57, 95% CI: 0.32-0.99, p = 0.049). In ileal disease, shorter duration modestly increased ER (31.8% vs. 21.3%, p = 0.048) but was not significant after adjustment (aOR 0.68, 95% CI: 0.39-1.19, p = 0.18). In colonic disease, the association was stronger (SES-CD <3: 39.5% vs. 18.9%, p < 0.001; aOR 0.44, 95% CI: 0.24-0.78, p = 0.005). CONCLUSIONS: Shorter disease duration predicts higher ER rates, particularly in colonic CD. These findings suggest earlier initiation of biologic therapy may improve healing in colonic disease, whereas ileal healing may be limited by location-specific factors.
