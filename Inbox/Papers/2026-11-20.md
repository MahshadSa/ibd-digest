  Anil Prabhu, Rena Cao, Simon Travis

  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag184](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag184) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Single-cell insights into drug response and resistance in inflammatory bowel disease: cellular hallmarks and precision therapy

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Aims
  > Treatment response in ulcerative colitis (UC) remains highly variable, with no evidence-based rationale as to why patients with apparently similar disease activity respond very differently to the same therapy. Understanding the cellular underpinnings may help us to resolve this variation and drive us towards rationalized therapy approaches. We aim to synthesize the current evidence on the cellular determinants of treatment response and non-response across major medical therapy classes in UC, focusing on experimental and translational human studies.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A comprehensive literature review was conducted, informed by PRISMA-ScR guidance, with a narrative synthesis of findings.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Across therapy classes, treatment response was generally associated with a closer match between drug mechanism and dominant tissue biology, together with epithelial restitution and restoration of homeostatic immune function. In contrast, non-response often mapped to persistent innate, myeloid and stromal inflammatory programs, including neutrophil, macrophage, and fibroblast-associated signatures, persistent T-helper (Th) 1 and Th17 states, as well as alternative inflammatory circuits not targeted by the relevant therapy.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Treatment responsiveness in UC is determined more by the broader inflammatory ecosystem than by isolated cellular determinants. Associative and small-scale evidence to date provide a foundation for future biomarker development rather than a basis for immediate precision prescribing. Data also suggest that mechanism-based treatment selection in UC is both biologically plausible and clinically worth pursuing.

  Nearest seed: Handheld intestinal ultrasound and the point-of-care question in pediatric IBD: a commentary.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Intestinal ultrasound (IUS) is a noninvasive, radiation-free tool that is well-tolerated and frequently preferred by children and families. Its ability to provide real-time assessments that facilitate timely decision-making is driving its adoption as an increasingly important monitoring standard in comprehensive pediatric inflammatory bowel disease care. IUS programs follow a common trajectory: begin by identifying and supporting a physician lead, then scale through additional trained physicians, a consultation service, and, where volume supports it, sonographer or hybrid models. Institutional planning should enable this growth while preserving the point-of-care delivery that defines IUS's clinical value. Sustainability requires institutional commitment to equipment, protected time, training (ideally embedded in fellowship), standardized documentation, and a reimbursement strategy. Equipment is attainable, not aspirational: a moderately priced cart-based system is sufficient and should be framed to leadership as essential infrastructure. Centers that adopt IUS with rigorous documentation and join collaborative registries from the outset will be positioned to generate the outcomes data needed to further advance the field.

  Nearest seed: Enterotype-based stratification identifies clinically distinct microbial subtypes in inflammatory bowel disease.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND & AIMS: Coupled with well-characterized host genetic and environmental risk factors, alterations of gut microbial communities contribute to risk and severity of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and its subtypes, Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). In a rapidly advancing field in which diverse multinational cohorts and molecular methods have been created, highly resolved microbial traits such as protein function and strain genetics can now be investigated through meta-analysis. METHODS: We integrated 2371 stool metagenomes from 542 individuals with IBD and their referent counterparts from the United States, Canada, and Europe, using all 7 IBD cohorts in the Human Microbiome Bioactives Resource, which we interrogated using taxonomic, functional, and strain profiling. RESULTS: We systematically identified the mass expansion of proinflammatory, oral-predominant taxa in the IBD gut, such as Veillonella and Streptococcus spp. We also accurately discriminate CD from UC, a clinically challenging problem, using highly resolved microbial strain genetics (prediction area under the curve = 0.69). Further, we observed disease-specific shifts in carbohydrate metabolism, a likely consequence of small bowel dysfunction in CD, but not UC, as well as perturbations in mucin use, increased microbial virulence and invasion cassettes, and loss of carnitine degradation pathways in IBD. Finally, we observed novel and significant differences in the gene carriage among both IBD- and non-IBD-associated taxa, suggesting that strain-specific functional variation may contribute to pathogenesis and disease-related bacterial fitness. CONCLUSIONS: Microbial clades responsible for IBD-linked dysbiosis are not uniform, and their functionality in IBD and CD/UC subsets are driven by species and strain lineage-specific variants.

  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-11-19
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag186](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag186) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Assessing the proinflammatory potential of sterile fecal microbiome filtrate from ulcerative colitis patients using an intestine-on-chip platform and automated image analysis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Background and Aims: Epithelial barrier alterations contribute to the pathogenesis and progression of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Barrier healing may predict long-term outcomes more accurately than endoscopic, histological or even transmural healing, and persistent epithelial abnormalities despite mucosal healing may underlie residual symptoms. Reliable tools are therefore needed to determine whether this therapeutic goal has been achieved and to support the development of barrier-directed therapies. We systematically reviewed the methods available to assess intestinal epithelial barrier structure and function in IBD.
  > Methods: MEDLINE and Scopus were searched to July 25, 2025, for studies assessing epithelial barrier structure or function in patients with IBD. Animal, in vitro and ex vivo studies, and those without a clearly described assessment method, were excluded. Two reviewers independently screened records and extracted data on tools, thresholds, and associations with disease activity, treatment and outcomes.
  > Results: Of 12 307 records screened, 124 studies were included: 73 evaluated permeability probes, 30 endogenous biomarkers and 25 invasive techniques, with four studies assessing both permeability probes and endogenous biomarkers. Probe-based testing relies on urinary recovery of orally administered sugars, polyethylene glycols, iohexol, radiolabelled compounds or fluorophores. Biomarkers reflect epithelial injury, microbial translocation, or the host response to it. Invasive techniques allow real-time, segment-specific assessment, principally probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy, whose scores predict relapse and major adverse outcomes; endocytoscopy combined with multispectral spatial imaging is an emerging alternative.
  > Conclusions: A broad range of functional, molecular and morphological tools is now available. Validating thresholds, standardising outcome measures and better defining barrier healing are the next steps before it can become a therapeutic target.

  Nearest seed: A Multi-Target Approach to Colitis: Ergothioneine Promotes Recovery by Restoring the Intestinal Barrier, Reducing Inflammation, and Remodeling the Microbiota.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Importance
  > Inflammation-associated oxidative stress can limit the viability and efficacy of obligate anaerobic probiotics such as Bifidobacterium. Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is characterized by sustained intestinal inflammation accompanied by elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS), creating an unfavorable niche for oxygen-sensitive commensals.
  >
  >
  > Objective
  > To evaluate the antioxidant, epithelial barrier–protective, and therapeutic potential of a hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)-resistant Bifidobacterium longum strain (S2).
  >
  >
  > Design
  > Experimental study involving in vitro functional assays, whole-genome sequencing, and three murine colitis models.
  >
  >
  > Setting
  > Laboratory-based cell culture and animal experiments.
  >
  >
  > Participants
  > A bacterial strain isolated from healthy adult feces and mice with experimentally induced colitis.
  >
  >
  > Interventions or Exposures
  > Oral administration of S2, compared with vehicle or the reference strain ATCC 15697.
  >
  >
  > Main Outcomes and Measures
  > H2O2 tolerance, antioxidant activity, epithelial permeability, aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) activity, fecal recovery, survival, clinical indices, histopathology, and mucus barrier markers.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > In vitro, S2 was significantly less inhibited by H2O2 and exhibited stronger radical-scavenging activity. S2 also reduced TNF-α- or LPS-induced epithelial permeability and increased aryl hydrocarbon receptor reporter activity. Whole-genome sequencing (2 365 469 bp; 59.94% G + C) placed S2 within the B. longum species and revealed genetic divergence from the H2O2-sensitive reference strain ATCC 15697. Consistent with robust survival under H2O2 exposure, genomic analysis showed a conserved thioredoxin/AhpC-centered peroxide detoxification system with additional redox-active enzymes. In vivo, oral administration of S2 resulted in higher fecal recovery than ATCC 15697, and viable recovery remained increased in RAG1-deficient mice, indicating that S2 persistence is not solely dependent on adaptive immunity. Importantly, across DSS, DNBS, and T-cell transfer colitis models, oral gavage of S2 significantly improved the survival rate, clinical indices, and histopathology while preserving goblet cells and mucin and increasing barrier-associated gene expression.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions and Relevance
  > Collectively, these results identified B. longum S2 as an oxidative stress–tolerant probiotic candidate with barrier-protective and immunomodulatory effects, supporting a strategy of selecting oxidative stress–tolerant bifidobacterial strains to improve probiotic performance in ROS-rich intestinal inflammation such as IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > The ANCA Laboratory Practices Meeting, a satellite of the 22nd International Vasculitis Workshop (Melbourne, 22 February 2026), convened laboratory scientists, pathologists and clinicians to discuss current ANCA testing practices. Delegates described divergent practices. Three themes emerged. First, high-quality MPO- and PR3-ANCA immunoassays are the preferred first-line test for ANCA-associated vasculitis. At diagnosis, harmonisation of ANCA interpretation through likelihood ratios and test-result intervals may offer a practical way to improve result interpretation and comparability despite differences in analytical platforms. Third, testing in low pre-test probability population consumes laboratory resources, and increases the risk of non-specific results. Choosing the appropriate indication for ANCA testing, whether diagnosis of vasculitis, monitoring of established disease, or investigation of gastrointestinal and hepatobiliary disease, ultimately rests with the requesting clinician. Laboratories can offer distinct requesting items to direct each request to the most appropriate testing algorithm. However, adaptation is limited by insufficient clinical information, costs, clinician education, and in some health systems, historical reimbursement rules.
