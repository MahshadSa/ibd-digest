  Sahu PK, Jain P, Ghosh V

  Nearest seed: Microenvironment-adaptive drug delivery systems for ulcerative colitis: from pathological constraints to rational design.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory bowel disease characterised by continuous inflammation and ulceration of the colonic mucosa, resulting from complex interactions among genetic susceptibility, environmental factors, intestinal barrier dysfunction, gut microbiota dysbiosis, and dysregulated immune responses. Although conventional therapies, including aminosalicylates, corticosteroids, immunomodulators, biologics, and small-molecule drugs have improved disease control, their clinical utility remains limited by incomplete therapeutic responses, adverse effects, loss of efficacy, and difficulties in achieving sustained remission. The present review offers an in-depth view of the emerging trends in molecular pharmacology and future drug-delivery systems for UC. UC-relevant molecular targets and pathological features of the colonic microenvironment be matched to site-specific or stimulus-responsive delivery strategies to improve local therapeutic exposure while limiting systemic toxicity, and clinical translation have been described. We discuss recent therapeutic advances targeting key inflammatory pathways (e.g. TNF-α, JAK-STAT, TYK2, IL-23/Th17, S1P receptors and PPAR-γ) and microbiome-directed approaches (e.g. faecal microbiota transplantation, microbial metabolites and postbiotics). Biomarker-driven approaches, such as faecal calprotectin and microbiota profiling, are emphasised as key components of precision medicine and treatment monitoring. Advanced Drug delivery mechanisms such as liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, solid lipid nanoparticles, hydrogels, microparticles, nanogels and multifunctional theranostic systems provide opportunities for colon-specific drug delivery, controlled release, improved bioavailability, improved mucosal targeting and decreased systemic exposure. New approaches based on RNA-based therapeutics, CRISPR/Cas technologies and artificial intelligence-assisted drug delivery system design further increase the possibilities of personalised treatment. To summarise, the combination of molecularly targeted pharmacology with intelligent and site-specific drug-delivery technologies is an exciting avenue for enhancing therapeutic efficacy and reducing treatment-related toxicity and for furthering personalised management of UC. However, translation to widespread clinical practice will need to overcome challenges related to biocompatibility, gastrointestinal variability, scalability, manufacturing, controlled release, and regulatory approval.

- [ ] **Early histological remission and prognosis in inflammatory bowel disease: a nationwide cohort study**

  Karl Mårild, Jonas Söderling, Jonas F Ludvigsson
  Journal of Crohn's and Colitis | 2026-11-21
  [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag195](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag195) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: All-cause and cause-specific mortality in inflammatory bowel disease across the biologic era: a population-based matched cohort study

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background & Aims
  > Early remission in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is increasingly recognized for providing long-term prognostic benefits, despite limited supporting data. We examined the prognostic value of histological remission within 12 months of diagnosis.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A nationwide cohort study of 14 019 Swedish IBD patients diagnosed in 1990-2022. These patients underwent ileo-colorectal biopsies showing histological remission or inflammation within 1-&lt;12 months post-diagnosis. We estimated adjusted hazard ratios (aHRs) for treatment failure (IBD-related surgery, hospitalization, third corticosteroid dispensation, or second-line advanced therapies), colorectal cancer (CRC), and death until 2023. We adjusted for sociodemographic characteristics, comorbidities, IBD phenotype, and medication history.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Early histological remission was more prevalent in patients with higher educational attainment or those diagnosed since 2000, but less common in childhood-onset (&lt;18 years) and elderly-onset (≥60 years) IBD. Average follow-up extended to 13.7 years. Compared to early remission, histological inflammation within 12 months was linked to an increased risk of treatment failure (aHR = 1.47, 95% CI = 1.39-1.54), particularly hospitalization (aHR = 1.46, 95% CI = 1.39-1.54) and second-line advanced therapy (aHR = 1.60, 95% CI = 1.34-1.91). Histological inflammation (vs early remission) was also linked to a heightened CRC risk (aHR = 1.65, 95% CI = 1.21-2.24), with similar risks in Crohn’s disease (CD: aHR = 1.66, 95% CI = 0.94-2.93) and ulcerative colitis (UC: aHR = 1.77, 95% CI = 1.20-2.60). Mortality risk was elevated in CD (aHR = 1.21, 95% CI = 1.01-1.45) but not UC (aHR = 0.96, 95% CI = 0.85-1.08). Associations were similar for corticosteroid-free histological remission within 6 and 12 months.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Sustained histological inflammation (vs remission) 12 months after IBD diagnosis predicted a higher risk of treatment failure and CRC, indicating that early histological remission may improve long-term disease prognosis.

  [10.1016/j.isci.2026.118246](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.118246) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: AI for screening in healthcare: promise and challenges.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Autonomous AI agents can now run end-to-end scientific analyses, yet no general method ensures those analyses are correct, and one upstream error can propagate across hundreds of downstream results at machine speed. We present CASCADE, an agentic validation framework built on mandatory, quantitatively gated analytical steps. Its central principle, specification-implementation separation, fixes what must be validated and to what standard while leaving how to the agent, so conclusions depend on the protocol rather than the model executing it. Every analysis is preserved as human-readable code with a complete decision log. Derived from a ∼25,000-patient clinical cancer genomics cohort and applied unmodified across three settings (13,424 patients), CASCADE recovered established signal, reported insufficient power instead of a false result where data were inadequate, and reversed a clinically consequential artifact (hazard ratio 0.84, corrected to 1.25). Findings were replicated in two independent multi-institutional cohorts, and three AI systems reproduced them with high fidelity.

  Pèlach A, Calafat M, Domènech E
  European journal of clinical nutrition | 2026-11-20
  [10.1038/s41430-026-01857-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-026-01857-5) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Current perspectives and future horizons in predicting response to nutrition therapy in children with Crohn's disease - a narrative review.

  > [!abstract]-
  > INTRODUCTION: Vitamin B12 (B12) is absorbed at the terminal ileum. Patients with Crohn's disease (CD) resection of the terminal ileum (RTI) are at risk of B12 deficiency. No standard recommendations on the monitoring and supplementation of B12 after non-extensive RTI are available. Our aims were to describe B12 supplementation modalities in clinical practice in patients with CD and RTI and to identify potential factors associated with B12 deficiency in this population. METHODS: Retrospective, single-centre study that included patients with CD who underwent RTI between January 2008 and October 2022. B12 levels, supplementation, indication and route of administration after RTI were recorded. RESULTS: One hundred and ninety-seven patients were included. Thirteen (7%) patients were on B12 supplementation prior to RTI. Seventy-nine patients (40%) were not supplemented and did not develop B12 deficiency. B12 supplementation was prescribed for 60% of patients during follow-up (6% of whom were already supplemented before surgery), 53% preventively after surgery and 47% after B12 deficiency. Intramuscular administration was the most common (97%, mainly monthly), and oral administration was limited (11%, mainly weekly). During follow-up, changes to the B12 supplementation occurred in 36% (mainly prolonging the interval of intramuscular administration). RTI length over 20 cm (OR 1.944, 95% CI 1.019-3.709; P = 0.042) was the only factor associated with developing B12 deficiency following surgery. CONCLUSIONS: B12 supplementation in patients with CD and RTI is heterogeneous. Preventive supplementation might be driven by ileal resection length, being recommended in those greater than 20 cm. Recommendations on supplementation and monitoring are needed to optimize clinical practice.

- [ ] **Recoverability-Centered Trajectory Governance for Long-Horizon Clinical AI Agents.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Liu J, Liu S
  Journal of medical Internet research | 2026-11-19
  [10.2196/110563](https://doi.org/10.2196/110563) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Agentic AI in Medicine: Challenges for Responsible Development and the Case for Clinical Testing Harnesses.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Clinical AI is moving from systems that generate discrete predictions and recommendations toward agents capable of pursuing clinical objectives through repeated tool use, communication, planning, and state-changing actions. Emerging systems support multistep electronic health record workflows, and increasingly envision longitudinal care across repeated encounters. This transition expands safety assessment beyond discrete outputs and actions. Through its actions, an agent can help create the clinical state on which its subsequent decisions depend. Consequently, individually defensible actions may compose into an unsafe trajectory, even when no single action is clearly erroneous. We propose recoverability-centered trajectory governance as a clinical synthesis of established safety approaches, linking patient-specific corrective capacity to the continuing justification for delegated authority. Its central objective is clinical recoverability: preserving the opportunity for the human-AI care system to redirect care before clinically acceptable corrective pathways are exhausted. We define recovery margin as the remaining opportunity for such correction, and reauthorization boundaries as conditions requiring delegated authority to end or narrow pending renewed human judgment. This framing distinguishes reversibility, primarily a property of an action, from recoverability, a property of an evolving clinical trajectory. The operational framework combines persistent obligation tracking, independent monitoring, explicit reauthorization, authority contraction, graceful degradation, independent escalation, and verified human takeover. Oversight should concentrate at consequential trajectory junctions. Evaluation should combine trajectory stress testing, component-specific recovery-margin indicators, interruption-and-takeover testing, and population-level surveillance. Trajectory governance complements product lifecycle oversight by examining whether continued patient-specific delegation remains justified as care evolves. Because corrective capacity depends partly on access to timely monitoring and human rescue, recoverability is also an equity issue. The defining safety question is therefore not only whether an action is acceptable but whether continued delegation remains justified. The proposed indicators and governance mechanisms require domain-specific operationalization and empirical validation, including comparison with established safeguards.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > AI-powered cardiac screening could evolve sports medicine by boosting accuracy, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness, with automated ECG interpretation based on foundation models becoming key for large-scale athlete screening. Before deployment, it is crucial to verify whether these clinically developed models are applicable to this population. We evaluated whether ECG foundation models trained on general clinical populations can diagnose athlete-specific ECG findings. We fine-tuned three models (ECG-FM, HuBERT-ECG, ECGFounder) on PhysioNet/CinC 2021, 87,662 samples, and tested them on 43 expert-annotated ECGs from highly trained/national-level athletes. ECGFounder performed best (AUROC 0.84, AUPRC 0.72, Sensitivity 0.57) and detected the most frequent findings, Sinus Bradycardia (AUROC 0.87) and Sinus Arrhythmia (AUROC 0.95), well. Per-label estimates for rare classes must be interpreted cautiously, given the small cohort. This work contributes to developing athlete-focused AI frameworks for reliable ECG-based screening.
