  Nearest seed: Management of pediatric Crohn’s disease: an ECCO-ESPGHAN guideline update

  > [!abstract]-
  > While current paediatric management increasingly favours early intensive treatment for children and adolescents with Crohn's disease (CD) at high risk of disease progression, a smaller subset of patients presents with a potentially 'low-risk' disease phenotype. Identifying these children is clinically challenging, as overtreatment may expose them to unnecessary risks and costs, whereas undertreatment may allow irreversible bowel damage and complications to develop. This narrative review summarises the current evidence on the definition, assessment, treatment, and monitoring of low-risk paediatric CD, highlighting existing knowledge gaps and future research priorities. At present, no consensus definition of low-risk paediatric CD exists, and disease classification relies largely on the absence of recognised high-risk features rather than validated criteria. Similarly, unlike adult practice, no paediatric prognostic model is available to identify children likely to follow a persistently mild disease course. Current assessment requires integration of clinical indices, inflammatory biomarkers, endoscopy, cross-sectional imaging, and evaluation of growth and nutritional status. Dietary treatment (Exclusive Enteral Nutrition and emerging dietary strategies, including the CD Exclusion Diet [CDED] and the Tasty & Healthy [T&H]] remains the commonest induction therapy. Budesonide or antibiotics (azithromycin and metronidazole) represent an appropriate alternative for selected children. CDED and the T&H diet may also represent attractive medium term treatment options, although robust maintenance data are still awaited. Prospective studies are needed to establish a consensus definition, develop paediatric-specific prediction models, optimise risk stratification, and determine the most effective long-term therapeutic and monitoring approaches for the 'low-risk' population.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND/AIMS: The growing prevalence of mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis (UC) among elderly individuals ( ≥60 years) poses unique clinical challenges due to age-related vulnerabilities, while real-world evidence on outpatient management and age-based differences remains limited. METHODS: This multicenter cross-sectional study recruited 429 patients with mild-to-moderate UC from 47 inflammatory bowel disease centers. A Delphi-developed questionnaire was applied to assess clinical profiles and medication adherence. All statistical analyses were performed using R 4.5.0, with P-value < 0.05 set as the significance level. RESULTS: Older patients had a longer median disease duration (6.0 years vs. 4.0 years; P= 0.001) and a higher proportion with moderate disease activity (60.4% vs. 51.8%; P= 0.029) compared with non-older patients. Diagnostic efficiency, disease extent, and extraintestinal manifestations did not differ between groups. Use of 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) remained the first-line therapy in both groups, with lower corticosteroid use among older patients (8.8% vs. 16.2%; P= 0.035). Older patients reported higher satisfaction with 5-ASA across several care domains (P= 0.019). CONCLUSIONS: Older adults with mild-to-moderate UC demonstrate distinct clinical phenotypes and divergent treatment priorities. Timely therapeutic escalation, the diagnostic process, and perceived treatment effectiveness are closely associated with medication adherence. These findings highlight the need for age-adapted management strategies in elderly UC patients.

  Nearest seed: Emerging insights into TL1A biology may guide precision therapy for IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > TNF-like cytokine 1A (TL1A) and its receptor DR3 form a key regulatory axis within mucosal immunity, integrating signals that promote Th1/Th17 responses, modulate innate lymphoid cells, and influence epithelial repair. Beyond inflammation, TL1A directly activates intestinal fibroblasts and contributes to extracellular matrix deposition, positioning the TL1A-DR3 pathway as a central driver of both chronic inflammation and fibrosis in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Genetic variants in TNFSF15, which encodes TL1A, further support a causal role, linking increased TL1A expression with susceptibility to Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, and fibrostenotic complications. Recent clinical trials of TL1A-neutralizing antibodies, including afimkibart, tulisokibart, and duvakitug, have demonstrated encouraging efficacy and safety in moderate-to-severe IBD, with emerging biomarker strategies suggesting potential for personalized treatment. Collectively, current evidence highlights TL1A blockade as a promising dual-pathway therapeutic approach targeting inflammation and fibrotic remodeling, with ongoing studies expected to define its long-term impact on disease modification.

However, these definitions remain heterogeneous, and the clinical significance and actionability of remission depth remain uncertain.
