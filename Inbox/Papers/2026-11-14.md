  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVES: In this study, we compared full protocol MR enterography (including contrast-enhanced images) and modified non-contrast protocol in assessment of CD activity. MATERIAL AND METHOD: In this retrospective cross-sectional study, we included 182 patients with histologically confirmed CD who underwent MR enterography examination in ANONYMISED.   > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > In corticosteroid-resistant acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC), cyclosporine is frequently used in biologic-exposed patients, but data on maintenance strategies after cyclosporine response remain limited. We aimed to describe 12-month outcomes after a cyclosporine-to-biologic bridge and to explore outcomes according to maintenance therapy.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > This single-center retrospective study screened all patients treated with cyclosporine for ASUC between 2014 and 2024. Patients who responded to cyclosporine and subsequently received vedolizumab or ustekinumab as maintenance therapy were included. The primary endpoint was 12-month colectomy-free survival. Comparisons between maintenance therapies were exploratory.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Among 67 patients treated with cyclosporine for ASUC, 44 responders were included (35 vedolizumab, 9 ustekinumab, and 96% biologic-exposed). Median follow-up was 39 months. In exploratory analyses, colectomy-free survival appeared lower with ustekinumab than with vedolizumab (47% vs 79%, log-rank P = .03). At 12 months, overall treatment persistence was 46% and combined clinical/biological remission was 34%. Adverse events were predominantly renal or neurological.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > In this predominantly biologic-exposed, cyclosporine-responsive ASUC cohort, a bridge to vedolizumab or ustekinumab was associated with 73% colectomy-free survival at 12 months and an acceptable safety profile. Comparisons between maintenance therapies remain exploratory.
