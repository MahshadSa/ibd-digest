- [ ] **Gut-active polysaccharide carriers for colonic 5-ASA delivery in ulcerative colitis.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Ariaee A, Prestidge CA, Joyce PM
  Expert opinion on drug delivery | 2026-11-08
  [10.1080/17425247.2026.2761122](https://doi.org/10.1080/17425247.2026.2761122) | Score: 0.97
  Nearest seed: Probiotic-Derived Vesicles Rectally Delivered via Thermo-Gelling Copolymer Promote Mucosal Healing and Reduce Inflammation in Ulcerative Colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > INTRODUCTION: Ulcerative colitis (UC) affects approximately 4.9 million people worldwide, with incidence rising globally and prevalence approaching 1% in industrialized nations. 5-Aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) is first-line therapy for mild-to-moderately active UC, yet pooled endoscopic mucosal healing averages only approximately 37% and 10-year relapse approaches 67%. A central reason is ineffective drug delivery: current technologies release 5-ASA into the bulk lumen, and pH-dependent coatings fail in active UC, where intracolonic pH is variable. Mucosal tissue concentration, not luminal concentration, determines efficacy, and concurrent gut dysbiosis sustains inflammation that current 5-ASA formulations fail to address. AREAS COVERED: UC-associated gut dysbiosis, the limitations of current colonic 5-ASA delivery, and gut-active polysaccharide carriers (inulin and modified inulin, pectin, guar gum, konjac glucomannan [KGM], resistant starch, and hyaluronic acid [HA]) applied specifically to 5-ASA. Advanced nanoparticle and hydrogel platforms and translational and regulatory priorities are also discussed. EXPERT OPINION: Gut-active, enzyme-responsive polysaccharide carriers are a rational formulation strategy for UC. By coupling colonic 5-ASA release to microbial fermentation at the mucosal interface, they deliver drug closer to the epithelial target while generating short-chain fatty acids (SCFA) that may attenuate dysbiosis. Since release is enzyme-triggered rather than pH-dependent, these systems can overcome the dissolution failure that limits current 5-ASA delivery.

- [ ] **Deployment of a Multilingual, Embodied, Holographic Artificial Intelligence Concierge in Ambulatory Care: Early Implementation Lessons From the Artificial Intelligence Responsive Interactive Agent.**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Meltzer EC, Staal JT, Bhagra A
  Mayo Clinic proceedings. Digital health | 2026-12-01
  [10.1016/j.mcpdig.2026.100427](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcpdig.2026.100427) | Score: 0.94
  Nearest seed: Multimodal artificial intelligence agents in healthcare: a scoping review

  > [!abstract]-
  > Health care environments can be difficult to navigate, particularly for individuals with limited language proficiency, hearing impairment, low digital literacy, or unfamiliarity with the facility. Conversational artificial intelligence (AI) systems may support accessibility and navigation, although descriptions of real-world implementation in health care settings remain limited. As a descriptive, single-site quality-improvement pilot, this report characterizes the experience of implementing a conversational AI agent in the lobby of a large, outpatient, academic practice. The Artificial Intelligence Responsive Interactive Agent (ARIA) is a multilingual, 3-dimensional, holographic conversational AI concierge developed to provide real-time assistance with navigation, digital onboarding, and information regarding amenities and services. ARIA leverages natural language processing and large language model technology to provide voice-enabled interactions in English, Spanish, Mandarin, and Arabic, with real-time closed captioning. The system can also generate quick response codes that push information directly to users' smartphone devices. Piloted in a large ambulatory lobby from October 21, 2025, to February 2, 2026, ARIA recorded 1429 device-activation sessions. Of these, 689 sessions (48%) contained analyzable user interaction data. Wayfinding and general information requests were the most common use cases. Deployment surfaced operational challenges, including audio capture, network reliability, interaction timing, workflow integration, quick response transfer, and content governance. As the use of AI in clinical settings expands, there may be a role for an embodied agent serving as a digital concierge if these barriers can be addressed. Future evaluations should prospectively define implementation outcomes, use systematic utilization denominators, and assess user experience and operational impact with validated measures.

- [ ] **Can CT Phantoms Produce Realistic CT Images of Human Anatomy? Physics-Based Evaluation of Multimaterial 3D-Printed CT Phantoms From CT Data**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Sören Jasper, Cécile Jeukens, Joachim E. Wildberger
  Investigative Radiology | 2026-11-09
  [10.1097/rli.0000000000001319](https://doi.org/10.1097/rli.0000000000001319) | Score: 0.91
  Nearest seed: Towards a unified foundation model for medical imaging

  > [!abstract]-
  > Objectives:
  > Patient-specific computed tomography (CT) phantoms can be produced using a novel 3D-printing technique that aims at replicating measured CT image volumes with high anatomic fidelity and accurate CT values. The purpose of this study is a comprehensive physics-based evaluation of both technical phantoms and a full-size adult abdominal phantom with regard to image-quality metrics and the reproducibility of CT values. In addition, the visualization of abdominal pathologies will be assessed.
  >
  >
  > Materials and Methods:
  > The study was conducted in 3 steps. First, a geometric reference disc phantom (40 mm thickness, 150 mm diameter) was 3D-printed using multimaterial jetting and scanned on a commercial photon-counting detector CT (PCD-CT) to assess the CT values of the base materials used for printing and to evaluate the influence of the printing process on spatial resolution and noise properties. Second, CT images of the reference phantom reconstructed with a medium-sharp and a sharp kernel were used as templates to print 2 CT-derived disc phantoms using multimaterial jetting with voxel-based material mapping. These phantoms were then scanned to assess how template image sharpness propagates into the image-quality properties of the final printed phantoms. Spatial resolution was evaluated using edge spread function (ESF) analysis and edge rise distance (ERD), whereas noise characteristics were assessed using noise power spectrum (NPS) analysis. Third, as a proof of concept, a full-size adult abdominal phantom was 3D-printed with a patient CT data set as template. Phantom images were compared with patient images regarding CT values, spatial resolution, noise properties, and the depiction of pathologic structures.
  >
  >
  > Results:
  > Evaluation of the CT attenuation of the base materials showed that CT values ranging from −37 HU to 995 HU in virtual monoenergetic images at 60 keV can be printed.
  > For the reference disc, measured ESF curves showed close agreement with theoretical expectations across all reconstruction kernels, indicating preservation of spatial resolution by the printing process. CT-derived discs showed minor edge smoothing compared with the reference for smooth and medium-sharp reconstructions. For sharp reconstructions, edge smoothing was severe, with an ERD increase of up to 32% compared with the reference. Edge smoothing was more pronounced for the disc based on a sharp template. NPS were highly consistent across all discs, with only minor differences in peak amplitude. In the abdominal phantom images ESF and NPS closely matched those of the patient CT images. Measured attenuation agreed well for tissues with positive CT values up to 1000 HU. Fat was represented with increased CT values because of print material limitations. Clinically relevant pathologic structures, including a large hepatocellular carcinoma and a smaller focal liver lesion, were displayed comparably in the phantom images.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions:
  > CT phantoms generated from CT image volumes using 3D printing with multimaterial mapping on a voxel level accurately reproduce the template images in terms of image sharpness, noise characteristics, and attenuation properties of tissues with positive CT values not exceeding 1000 HU. The achievable spatial resolution is limited to medium-sharp levels and does not improve by using sharper template images. These findings support the use of image-based multimaterial 3D printing to produce realistic anthropomorphic phantoms for advanced CT image-quality assessment and optimization, and for validation of reconstruction algorithms and quantitative CT imaging methods.
