  Nearest seed: AI/ML-Enabled Multi-Omics Integration of Host Genetics, Immunity, and the Gut Microbiome in Crohn's Disease: From Diagnosis to Theranostics.

  > [!abstract]-
  > INTRODUCTION: The clinical course of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is highly heterogeneous, yet the biological mechanisms underlying this variability, along with robust predictive biomarkers of disease course and treatment response, remain elusive. Most existing multi-omic cohorts recruit patients after IBD diagnosis and following therapeutic exposure, limiting the ability to distinguish between primary disease biology and downstream consequences of chronic inflammation and/or treatment. There is, therefore, a critical unmet need for large-scale, treatment-naïve inception cohorts integrating deep clinical phenotyping with longitudinal multi-omic profiling from the point of IBD diagnosis. METHODS AND ANALYSIS: Open-IBD is a prospective, multicentre UK inception cohort study recruiting up to 2000 adults referred with suspected IBD, prior to diagnosis and initiation of IBD-specific therapy. Baseline assessment includes detailed clinical metadata collection, patient-reported outcome measures and collection of blood, stool and intestinal biopsy samples. 1000 participants subsequently diagnosed with IBD will be followed for up to 48 months, with repeated clinical assessments, questionnaires and biosampling. Participants not diagnosed with IBD will form a symptomatic control cohort with longitudinal clinical record review. Multi-omic analyses will include human genetics, single-cell transcriptomics of blood and intestinal tissue, stool and intestinal mucosal microbiome characterisation, metabolomics and proteomics. Integrated longitudinal clinical and multi-omic datasets will be generated to characterise early-stage treatment-naïve IBD, identify biomarkers associated with long-term outcomes and nominate causal factors driving IBD pathophysiology. ETHICS AND DISSEMINATION: Ethical approval has been obtained from the Wales Research Ethics Committee 1 (24/WA/0338). All participants provide informed consent before enrolment. Recruitment is ongoing. Study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, presentations at scientific meetings and lay summaries. STUDY REGISTRATION NUMBER: ISRCTN15535639.

  Nearest seed: Pharmacokinetic model selection for infliximab based on inflammatory bowel disease phenotype and severity: Toward model-informed precision dosing.

  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVE: Translating population pharmacokinetic (popPK) models of adalimumab in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) into clinical practice remains challenging. We aimed to develop a parsimonious popPK model using real-world therapeutic drug monitoring data and perform dosing simulations to guide model-informed precision dosing (MIPD). METHODS: We conducted a retrospective, longitudinal study of adult IBD patients treated with adalimumab between 2022 and 2024. Serum adalimumab concentrations from routine therapeutic drug monitoring were analysed using nonlinear mixed-effects modelling. Covariates were selected using a stepwise likelihood ratio approach. Model performance was evaluated using goodness-of-fit (GOF) plots, prediction-corrected visual predictive checks (pcVPC), normalised prediction distribution errors (NPDE), sequential Bayesian forecasting analysis, and bootstrap analysis. Monte Carlo simulations estimated the probability of target attainment (PTA) for steady-state trough concentration thresholds of ≥ 8, ≥ 10, and ≥ 12 mg·l⁻1 across different body weight, serum albumin, and dosing scenarios. RESULTS: From 201 patients (56.2% male; 48.6 (±16.2) years), 709 trough concentrations were analysed. A one-compartment model with first-order elimination and absorption (fixed at 0.151·day⁻1) adequately described the pharmacokinetics. Population estimates were apparent clearance (CL/F) 0.288 l·day⁻1 and apparent volume of distribution 5.0 l. Body weight and serum albumin were significant covariates on CL/F, reducing interindividual variability from 59.2% to 42.0%. Model performance was consistent across evaluation methods. Individual parameter estimation was reliable with low CL/F shrinkage (11.1%). Predictive performance was robust: minimal bias (mean prediction error [MPE]: -0.30 mg·l⁻1) and high precision (root mean square error [RMSE]: 3.86 mg·l⁻1). pcVPC demonstrated good agreement (91.0% coverage), and NPDE indicated no structural misspecifications. Sequential forecasting analysis showed that predictive performance improved as more data were incorporated (MPE decreased from 0.37 to 0.03 mg·l⁻1), and precision increased (RMSE decreased from 4.86 to 4.39 mg·l⁻1). Under standard maintenance dosing, PTA ranged from 65.0% to 98.5%, from 36.5% to 93.5%, and from 20.5% to 86.5% for ≥ 8 mg·l⁻1, ≥ 10 mg·l⁻1, and ≥12 mg·l⁻1 respectively. PTA decreased with increasing body weight, lower serum albumin, and more stringent concentration thresholds. CONCLUSION: This popPK model adequately characterizes adalimumab pharmacokinetics in IBD patients. Its robust predictive performance, which progressively improves as more individual data are incorporated, together with the model-based simulations, supports its potential utility in MIPD, particularly in patients with higher clearance or when higher concentration thresholds are targeted.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and aims
  > Vedolizumab (VDZ) blocks integrin α4β7-MAdCAM-1 interactions, limiting leukocyte entry into the gut, yet peripheral-blood immune correlates of clinical response in ulcerative colitis (UC) remain unidentified.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from healthy controls (HCs) and patients with UC, stratified by VDZ exposure and clinical response, were analyzed using mass cytometry (cytometry by time-of-flight). A 38-marker panel enriched for integrins, chemokine receptors, and activation markers was applied. Major immune populations were interrogated using lineage-specific opt-SNE and FlowSOM clustering.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > The dataset included 26 HCs, 30 VDZ-naive UC (Nancy index ≥ 2), 8 VDZ-treated non-responders (Nancy index ≥ 2), and 8 VDZ-treated responders (Nancy index &lt;2). Integrin-pairing analysis demonstrated that these B-cell populations contained both α4β7+ and α4β1+ cells. In contrast, VDZ non-responders showed enrichment of an α4β7+ atypical-like IgA+ memory B-cell subset relative to responders, VDZ-naive UC, and HCs.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Clinical response and non-response to VDZ in UC are associated with distinct circulating T- and B-cell immune phenotypes. Peripheral-blood immunophenotyping may identify correlates of response and non-response. Prospective studies are needed to determine whether these signatures are predictive before VDZ initiation or reflect pharmacodynamic changes after treatment.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Many foundation models that have entered medicine are language models, although foundation models for imaging and other modalities have also emerged. A second class is now emerging from robotics and autonomous driving, trained not to produce text but to predict how an environment evolves under action, and marketed under the designation world foundation model. We argue that medicine should approach this class with a specific form of caution. Medicine already possesses world models in the mechanistic simulators and digital twins built from physiology and physics, and the constraint on those has never been the volume of available data. The constraint is identifiability. A simulator learned from observational clinical records inherits the treatment policies that generated them, and rolling such a model forward under an alternative treatment recovers the practice pattern rather than the underlying physiology. We distinguish the settings where learned world models are likely to succeed from those where they will reproduce confounding at scale, and set out what validation would require.

  > Abstract
  > Despite advances in pain management, pain remains a major global health challenge in both oncological and non-oncological patients. Long-term pharmacological treatment is often restricted by insufficient therapeutic benefit and adverse events, highlighting the need for alternative approaches such as image-guided pain therapies. The idea of treating pain with cold has been recognized for centuries, and today cryotherapy has emerged as a valuable minimally invasive option for pain management. Cryoablation refers to the destruction of tissue with extreme cold and has an increasing role, especially in the treatment of benign (e.g., osteoid osteoma), malignant bone (i.e., metastases), and soft tissue tumors, either with a curative or palliative intent. Cryoneurolysis is a related but distinct application, focusing either on peripheral nerve modulation or nerve destruction. Cryoneurolysis may be highly effective for the treatment of acute or chronic pain, including sympathetic neurolysis in cancer and non-cancer-related pain syndromes. The general approach in patients with tumor-related pain depends on whether the lesion itself can be ablated or not. In non-oncologic patients, cryoneurolysis of the responsible nerve is typically performed following a positive response to a diagnostic nerve block. Potential indications for cryotherapy are constantly evolving; however, evidence, especially for cryoneurolysis, is still limited and based mostly on single-center case series and retrospective studies. Standardized ablation protocols are lacking, and for the duration of cycles, most experts rely on imaging visualization and validated protocols. Equally important, successful integration of cryoablation and cryoneurolysis into daily practice requires structured workflows across different patient pathways and interdisciplinary management.
