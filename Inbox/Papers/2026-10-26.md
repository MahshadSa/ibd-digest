  Nearest seed: Pharmacokinetic model selection for infliximab based on inflammatory bowel disease phenotype and severity: Toward model-informed precision dosing.

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Background and Aims
  > The value of therapeutic drug monitoring during infliximab induction remains uncertain. We evaluated whether baseline infliximab clearance and early exposure metrics identify candidates for potential early proactive therapeutic drug monitoring during induction in inflammatory bowel disease.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > We analysed a multicenter Phase 4 study in which adults with Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis initiated standard infliximab therapy (5 mg/kg at Weeks 0, 2, 6, 14, 22 and 30) with predefined peak, intermediate and trough sampling. A population pharmacokinetic model characterized disposition and covariates. Logistic regression assessed exposure–response relationships. The primary outcome was week‐30 combined remission (Crohn's disease: Harvey–Bradshaw Index ≤ 4; ulcerative colitis: Partial Mayo score ≤ 2; with faecal calprotectin < 100 μg/g and C‐reactive protein ≤ 5 mg/L).
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > Seventy‐five participants (33 female; median age 39 years, interquartile range 28–52) contributed pharmacokinetic data. Sixty‐three were evaluable for exposure–response analyses. Twelve participants (19%) achieved combined remission (7/35 Crohn's disease; 5/28 ulcerative colitis). Lower serum albumin, higher body weight, and antibodies to infliximab increased clearance. In ulcerative colitis, baseline clearance and model‐predicted week‐6 trough concentration showed strong discrimination for combined remission (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.88, 95% confidence interval 0.75–1.00; and 0.86, 0.69–1.00, respectively). Baseline clearance > 0.28 L/day and week‐6 trough ≤ 21.6 mg/L identified high‐risk subgroups with few remissions (0/18 and 1/20, respectively,
  > p
  > < 0.05). No robust predictors were identified in Crohn's disease.
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > A pragmatic, risk‐stratified, model‐informed approach centered on baseline clearance and early troughs may guide personalized infliximab induction in ulcerative colitis. External validation is warranted.
  >
  >

  > EudraCT number: 2015‐004618‐10

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease‑associated colorectal cancer (IBD‑CRC) arises in the setting of long‑standing chronic intestinal inflammation, in which immune dysregulation cannot be readily attributed to the dominance of a single inhibitory pathway or to global immunosuppression. Persistent inflammatory signaling sustains a state of prolonged immune engagement, yet with constrained effector output, potentially compromising antitumor immune surveillance and limiting the clinical benefit of immunotherapy. The present review synthetises the accumulated knowledge on the long‑term immunological alterations induced by chronic inflammation, including the integration of immune signaling, shifts in activation thresholds, metabolic reprogramming, and functional polarization of immune cells. These changes are discussed in the context of both tumor initiation and tumor maintenance to delineate the immunological evolution of IBD‑CRC. The review further examines the relationship between inflammation‑induced response constraints and the limited efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors, and summarizes the mechanistic barriers to immunotherapy in IBD‑CRC. Collectively, these observations suggest that relieving inhibition through a single target may be insufficient to restore effective antitumor immunity. Finally, the potential value of natural plant‑derived interventions characterized by multi‑component, low‑intensity, and long‑term modulation is discussed, particularly with respect to reducing inflammatory burden and improving immune metabolic states, thereby providing a conceptual basis for the development of combination immunotherapeutic strategies.

  > Baicalin (BA), a bioactive flavonoid, and levofloxacin hydrochloride (LH), a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, both hold potential for ulcerative colitis (UC) treatment, yet the mechanistic differences in their regulation of gut microbiota and metabolome remain elusive. These distinct mechanisms provide new evidence for developing microbiota-based personalized strategies for ulcerative colitis treatment.

  Zhang H
  Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE | 2026-10-23
  [10.3791/71825](https://doi.org/10.3791/71825) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Adjunctive nutraceutical strategies in ulcerative colitis: From molecular pathways to clinical outcomes.

  > [!abstract]-
  > The overall aim of this narrative review is to synthesize epidemiological evidence linking ultra-processed food (UPF) consumption to the onset and progression of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), examine the proposed pathophysiological mechanisms, and evaluate contemporary clinical nutritional strategies. Epidemiological data demonstrates a robust, dose-response association between high UPF intake and an increased risk of incident IBD, predominantly Crohn's disease (CD). Furthermore, in diagnosed patients, UPF consumption correlates with elevated clinical activity, inflammatory relapses, and adverse clinical outcomes. Experimental and observational findings suggest four potential pathways linking UPF-related exposures to altered gut immune homeostasis. First, UPF-rich dietary patterns may be associated with microbiota dysbiosis and reduced short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) availability. Second, experimental studies suggest that selected emulsifiers can alter intestinal mucus organization and epithelial tight-junction integrity. Third, increased intestinal permeability may facilitate exposure to luminal bacterial products and downstream inflammatory signaling through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB). Fourth, advanced glycation end products (AGEs) may contribute to intestinal inflammatory signaling and epithelial stress, although their proposed effects on energy sensing and autophagy in IBD require further validation. Current clinical guidance supports healthy dietary patterns with limited UPF intake and individualized nutritional care, while evidence that UPF restriction alone reduces IBD flares remains insufficient. The review concludes that mitigating the clinical burden of IBD requires integrating personalized nutritional interventions managed by specialized dietitians, while highlighting the necessity for further research into specific food additives and precision nutrition strategies.

  Nearest seed: Enterococcus faecium MMX derived from traditional fermented mare's milk alleviates colitis by modulating the LCN2-NLRP3 pyroptosis axis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic intestinal disorder closely linked to gut microbiota dysbiosis and metabolic disturbances. This study investigated the alleviating effects and mechanisms of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis C-2 (C-2) on dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced ulcerative colitis (UC) in mice. The results demonstrated that C-2 significantly ameliorated pathological phenotypes in DSS-induced UC mice, including reduced weight loss, lowered disease activity index (DAI) scores, restored colon length, and diminished inflammatory damage of colonic tissue. Meanwhile, C-2 effectively repaired the intestinal barrier by enhancing the expression of tight junction proteins (Claudin-1, Occludin, ZO-1) and MUC2, thereby reducing lipopolysaccharide in serum and rebalancing pro-inflammatory (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) and anti-inflammatory (IL-10) cytokines in the colon and serum. Notably, C-2 reshaped the gut microbiota structure, significantly increasing microbial diversity (Shannon index) and enriching beneficial bacteria such as Bifidobacterium and Prevotellaceae. Metabolomic analysis further revealed that C-2 modulated the gut tryptophan metabolism pathway, influencing the levels of tryptophan-derived indole metabolites including indole-3-propionic acid (IPA) and indole-3-acrylic acid (I3A), which were associated with activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)/Cyp1a1 signaling pathway to enhance barrier integrity and suppress inflammation. Moreover, C-2 intervention restored short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) levels. In summary, C-2 alleviates UC in mice through synergistic mechanisms including optimizing gut microbiota composition, boosting SCFA production, and enhancing tryptophan-derived metabolites. Collectively, these findings support the potential of C-2 as a candidate probiotic intervention strategy for IBD.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  > Actionable reporting in radiology is a concept that has gained traction since its inception in 2014. The notion is simple: radiology reports should easily translate into clinical action, particularly when the study is abnormal. This is not so simple. But the process of producing an actionable report is as crucial as the report itself. The process should result in a radiology report that consistently results in information being provided to the referrer that results in improved patient care. This review article presents an evidence-based critique of the concept, demonstrates the numerous pitfalls, illustrates points of gold-standard practice (illustrated as ‘Rules’) and presents a coherent model of the overall process of actionable reporting, which flanks the crucial central element of an actionable report.
  >
  >
  > Key Points
  >
  > Question
  > The actionable reporting process is highly dependent on cooperation and coordination of many healthcare staff, IT systems, and different departments, with the radiologist at the heart of it; it should be seen as a multistep process where each step is crucial and interdependent with the overall goal of better patient outcomes via better systems of work.
  >
  >
  > Findings
  > The skilful production of an actionable report is central to this process and remains the responsibility of the individual radiologist.
  >
  >
  > Critical relevance statement
  > Fundamentally, actionable reporting represents the current best practice in radiology reporting. It should be the goal of every radiologist to ensure their reports are actionable and their reporting workflow matches that of actionable reporting.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Immune-mediated systemic diseases frequently produce oral and orofacial findings involving the mucosa, salivary glands, periodontium, temporomandibular joints, and masticatory or oropharyngeal function. These findings may precede systemic diagnosis, reflect disease activity or damage, result from treatment, or identify patients requiring urgent referral. This expert narrative review provides a phenotype-based diagnostic and management framework for oral and orofacial manifestations of major immune-mediated systemic diseases. We integrate disease-specific patterns with key mimics, required confirmatory investigations, diagnostic red flags, and referral urgency. Ulcerative, lichenoid/interface, vesiculobullous, sicca/salivary, periodontal, temporomandibular, vascular/ischemic, infectious, and drug-related presentations are distinguished according to whether diagnosis is primarily clinical or requires further investigation. This lesion-first approach is intended to improve early recognition and multidisciplinary coordination across rheumatology, internal medicine, immunology, dentistry, oral medicine and dermatology.

- [ ] **Thiopurines and vitamin K antagonist resistance: warfarin inhibits thiopurine S-methyltransferase.**

- [ ] Read later
  Bayoumy AB, Russell DA, Totah RA
  Journal of thrombosis and thrombolysis | 2026-10-25
  [10.1007/s11239-026-03415-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11239-026-03415-z) | Score: 0.92
  Nearest seed: Ulcerative colitis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Vitamin K antagonists (VKAs) inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, reducing the synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors and increasing bleeding risk. Thiopurines, such as azathioprine (AZA) and 6-mercaptopurine (MP), are immunosuppressants commonly used in inflammatory bowel disease and other immune-mediated disorders. Case reports suggest a dose-dependent relationship between thiopurines and reduced INR levels, potentially contributing to VKA resistance. We present a case of a female patient with Sjögren's disease, Factor V Leiden thrombophilia and intermediate thiopurine S-methyltransferase (TPMT) metabolism who experienced VKA resistance associated with high 6-thioguanine nucleotide (6-TGN) levels. Despite phenprocoumon therapy, therapeutic INR levels were not maintained after multiple higher dosages (37.5 mg per week) of phenprocoumon which were much higher than patients' regular dose (15 mg/week). After reducing AZA dosage, her 6-TGN levels decreased significantly, and therapeutic INR was restored, suggesting a direct relationship between elevated 6-TGN levels and VKA resistance. An in vitro inhibition assay showed that warfarin inhibits TPMT, which could have contributed to higher 6-TGN levels. Potential mechanisms for thiopurine-induced VKA resistance include increased prothrombin activity, but may also theoretically be caused by alterations in gut microbiota reducing vitamin K synthesis, and interference with enzymes like VKORC1. Literature highlights cases where thiopurines lowered INR, leading to thrombotic events, particularly in female patients, suggesting a potential hormonal influence. Other cases also described that stopping thiopurines led to high INR levels, which led to bleeding. This case underscores the need for therapeutic drug monitoring in patients using both VKAs and thiopurines. Gender-specific factors may further contribute to VKA resistance and warrant additional research. Clinicians should remain vigilant in managing this complex interaction to optimize patient outcomes.
