- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Amera Elzubeir, Anjana Kumar, Kate D. Lynch
  Alimentary Pharmacology &amp; Therapeutics | 2026-10-21
  [10.1111/apt.71045](https://doi.org/10.1111/apt.71045) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Assessing the effectiveness of biologics in biologic-naïve patients with isolated small bowel Crohn's disease by double-balloon endoscopy.

  > [!abstract]-
  > ABSTRACT
  >
  > Background
  > Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) associated with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) is a distinct phenotype (PSC‐IBD). Data on the safety and effectiveness of advanced IBD therapies on IBD outcomes in PSC‐IBD remain limited.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > A systematic review and meta‐analysis assessed the safety and effectiveness of advanced therapies in adults with PSC‐IBD. MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane databases and Web of Science were searched to July 2026. The primary outcome was IBD clinical remission. Secondary outcomes included clinical response, corticosteroid‐free remission, mucosal healing, adverse event‐related treatment discontinuation and colorectal neoplasia. A random‐effects model was used for pooled estimates.
  >
  >
  > Results
  >
  > Five retrospective case‐series (
  > n
  > = 394) evaluating infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab and tofacitinib were included (median follow‐up of 18.4 months). Pooled clinical remission rates were 24.7% (95% CI: 17.2%–33.0%) at 3 months and 28.6% (95% CI: 16.3%–42.6%) at 6–12 months. Corticosteroid‐free remission was achieved in 45.4% (95% CI: 33.1%–57.9%) and mucosal healing in 25.6% (95% CI: 13.6%–39.5%). Tofacitinib was associated with a clinical response rate of 59.3% (95% CI: 40.0%–77.2%) and low adverse event rate, in a single available study. Adverse event‐related discontinuation occurred in 9.1% (95% CI: 4.4%–15.1%), while 31.8% (95% CI: 25.5%–38.5%) experienced primary non‐response or secondary loss of response. The pooled proportion of colorectal neoplasia was 10.5% (12/112; 95% CI: 5.3%–17.1%). Five patients developed colorectal cancer (5/112; 4.3%) and two (2/177; 0.8%) cholangiocarcinoma.
  >
  >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > Advanced IBD therapies demonstrate modest but encouraging effectiveness and an acceptable safety profile in PSC‐IBD; however, the available evidence is limited by retrospective study designs, small sample sizes and substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity. Prospective controlled studies using standardised IBD outcomes are needed to inform optimal therapeutic strategies in this high‐risk population.
  >
  >
  > Trial Registration
  > PROSPERO: CRD42024580296

- [ ] **TRAM-AI: A Framework for Transparency in Reporting of Medical Imaging                     Datasets for Artificial Intelligence Development**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Kartik Bose, Pankaj Gupta, Charles E. Kahn
  Radiology: Artificial Intelligence | 2026-10-21
  [10.1148/ryai.260538](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260538) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (CLAIM):                     Explanation, Elaboration, and Examples

  > [!abstract]-
  > Purpose To develop and evaluate TRAM-AI, a consensus-based checklist for verifying whether medical imaging datasets described as publicly accessible are functionally usable for AI development. Materials and Methods A modified Delphi study was conducted. A steering committee generated candidate checklist items through a targeted literature review. An international expert panel ( n = 33) rated items on criticality and feasibility across two Delphi rounds. Consensus was defined as ≥ 75% agreement on both dimensions. The checklist was pilot-validated in 10 publicly available datasets. An advisory group of four experts from Latin America and sub-Saharan Africa evaluated cross-regional applicability. Results Of 16 candidate items, 14 were retained in the final checklist; one item was removed because of feasibility concerns, and another was merged with an existing item because of conceptual redundancy. All 14 final items achieved consensus for both criticality and feasibility. The final TRAM-AI checklist comprises 14 items across four domains: Dataset Access and Persistence (3 items), Technical Interoperability (4 items), Metadata and Reproducibility (4 items), and Ethical, Legal, and Maintenance considerations (3 items). In pilot validation, all items were applicable to all 10 datasets, compliance was 90% for trusted repository, de-dentification, and subject-level mapping, 10% for archive validation, and 30% for partition keys. All four advisory group members endorsed the checklist. Conclusion TRAM-AI provides a practical framework for verifying the functional accessibility of medical imaging datasets, complementing existing methodological reporting standards. Further evaluation and broader validation across diverse geographic settings are warranted. ©RSNA, 2026

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background
  > Mucosal healing in ulcerative colitis (UC) is believed to follow a proximal-to-distal pattern. We evaluated colonic healing distribution in patients with moderate-to-severe UC receiving adalimumab.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Patients from the SERENE UC (Study of a Novel Approach to Induction and Maintenance Dosing with Adalimumab in Patients with Moderate to Severe Ulcerative Colitis) trial with full colonoscopies and segmental biopsies at screening, weeks 8, and 52 were included for endoscopic and histologic analyses if baseline Mayo endoscopic subscore (MES) or Robarts Histopathology Index (RHI) score, respectively, was &gt;0 in the ascending, transverse, and/or descending colon. Data were analyzed using a mixed model for repeated measures to account for clustering of segments within a patient.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > Endoscopic and histologic analyses comprised 76 and 79 patients, respectively. Score reductions were significantly greater vs the rectum in the ascending (week 8: MES, mean −0.78 [95% confidence interval (CI), −1.03 to −0.52]; RHI, mean −4.70 [95% CI, −6.92 to −2.49]; week 52: MES, mean −0.63 [95% CI, −0.91 to −0.35]; RHI, mean −2.39 [95% CI, −4.64 to −0.15]), transverse (week 8: MES, mean −0.69 [95% CI, −0.94 to −0.43]; RHI, mean −3.57 [95% CI, −5.62 to −1.51]; week 52: MES, mean −0.64 [95% CI, −0.89 to −0.38]; RHI, mean −2.88 [95% CI, −4.99 to −0.77]), and descending colon (week 8: MES, mean −0.40 [95% CI, −0.67 to −0.13]; RHI, mean −2.55 [95% CI, −4.94 to −0.16]; week 52: MES, mean −0.52 [95% CI, −0.70 to −0.34]; RHI, mean −3.47 [95% CI, −5.37 to −1.56]). Endoscopic improvement/remission and histologic remission were significantly more likely in the ascending and transverse colon than the rectum.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Our findings demonstrate that endoscopic and histologic healing is more challenging in the rectum compared with proximal colonic segments.

  Nearest seed: Application of large language models for assigning clear cell likelihood score v2.0 from free–text MRI reports: a feasibility study

  > [!abstract]-
  > The Brain Tumor Reporting and Data System (BT-RADS) standardizes posttreatment MRI response assessment in patients with diffuse gliomas but requires complex integration of imaging trends, medication effects, and radiation timing. An end-to-end multiagent large language model and convolutional neural network system for automated BTRADS classification was developed and evaluated. Five hundred nine consecutive posttreatment glioma MRI examinations (March 2017-July 2021) from a single center were retrospectively evaluated.   >
  > Study Type

  > Statistical Tests
  >

  >
  >
  >

  > n
