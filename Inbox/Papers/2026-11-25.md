- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Miotto DASM, Grunewald STF, Shehan TSM
  European journal of pediatrics | 2026-11-24
  [10.1007/s00431-026-07640-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-07640-9) | Score: 0.96
  Nearest seed: "The role of red cell distribution width in inflammatory bowel disease evaluation: a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis".

  > [!abstract]-
  > UNLABELLED: Anemia is a common extraintestinal manifestation of pediatric inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet reported prevalences vary widely, partly because case definitions differ, and no pediatric pooled estimate exists. We aimed to quantify the prevalence, etiologic composition, severity, and 1-year trajectory of anemia in pediatric IBD under age/sex-specific criteria. We searched six databases from inception to 15 June 2026 for studies in patients under 18 years. The prespecified primary analysis estimated prevalence at diagnosis in representative cohorts using World Health Organization (WHO) age- and sex-specific hemoglobin thresholds; a comprehensive estimate combined all eligible cohorts regardless of definition. Proportions were pooled by random-effects generalized linear mixed models; heterogeneity was summarized by I2 and prediction intervals. Sixty-three cohorts (15,994 children) gave an overall prevalence of 61.7% (95% CI 56.4-66.7); in the 10 WHO-defined primary cohorts (2559 children), it was 60.8% (52.5-68.4, 95% prediction interval 31.9-83.6). Prevalence varied nearly 28 points on definition alone (40.5% with fixed cutoffs vs 68.9% with age-appropriate criteria). Among anemic children, iron deficiency predominated (78.0%; five cohorts, very low certainty). Prevalence was similar in Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis, higher in active disease (74.8% vs 48.5%), and fell to 37.0% at 1 year. CONCLUSION: These definition-standardized estimates quantify a burden guidelines recognize: averaged across heterogeneous settings, two-thirds of children anemic at diagnosis, mostly from iron deficiency, and more than a third at 1 year. Because the case definition, not setting, was a significant source of variation, age- and sex-specific thresholds are essential for screening, management, and comparable research. TRIAL REGISTRATION: PROSPERO CRD420261436312. Reported according to the PRISMA 2020 statement. WHAT IS KNOWN: • Anemia is a common extraintestinal manifestation of pediatric IBD, but reported prevalence ranges from about 40% to 80% and no pediatric-specific pooled estimate exists. WHAT IS NEW: • This first pediatric-specific meta-analysis provides pooled, WHO age/sex-standardized reference estimates: in 63 cohorts (15,994 children), two-thirds were anemic at diagnosis and more than a third at 1 year. • The anemia case definition-not country income-was a significant, prespecified source of between-study variation; age- and sex-specific thresholds are essential for comparable estimates.

  Nearest seed: Bowel urgency in inflammatory bowel disease: A concept analysis.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and Aims
  > Urgency and fecal incontinence are disabling yet under-recognized symptoms in pediatric ulcerative colitis (UC). The IMPRECTO prospective multicentre cross-sectional study investigated the prevalence of urgency and fecal incontinence in children aged 6-18 years with UC. Researchers evaluated contributing factors, psychosocial impacts, and the quality of physician–patient communication regarding these symptoms.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Children with UC followed in 11 French tertiary centers were consecutively enrolled between September and November 2024. Each patient completed 2 questionnaires, incorporating patient-reported outcomes (PROs) and the IMPACT-III quality-of-life instrument. A complementary survey evaluated the clinical practices of pediatric gastroenterologists.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 191 patients were included. At enrolment, urgency and fecal incontinence were documented in medical records in 9.0% and 2.7% of patients, respectively. In contrast, PROs indicated higher frequencies, with urgency and fecal incontinence occurring at least monthly in 41.8% and 16.4% of patients, respectively. Both symptoms correlated with higher disease activity, while 32.5% of symptomatic patients reported experiencing urgency/fecal incontinence even during perceived disease remission. Higher urgency/fecal incontinence frequencies were associated with impaired daily life, reduced school attendance, and lower IMPACT-III scores. Communication gaps were evident: 20.8% of symptomatic children never discussed their symptoms during follow-up, while 24.9% desired to have these symptoms addressed. Only 47.8% of physicians routinely assessed urgency and 21.7% assessed fecal incontinence.
  >
  >
  > Conclusions
  > Urgency and fecal incontinence remain highly prevalent and burdensome in pediatric UC, yet are under-recognized in clinical practice. Systematic assessment appears essential to improve disease evaluation and patient–physician communication, and support patient-centered management.

  > [!abstract]-
  > OBJECTIVE: Automated identification and extraction of recommendations for additional imaging (RAI) from radiology reports could facilitate closed-loop tracking and follow-up completion. We compare a prompt-engineered GPT-4.1 large language model (LLM) versus a validated encoder-only transformer deep learning model (bidirectional encoder representations from transformers [BERT]) for detection of RAI in abdominopelvic computed tomography (CTAP) reports, and evaluate GPT-4.1 for extraction of actionable RAI elements. METHODS: This retrospective study included CTAP reports from a multi-institution system in 2023. LLM and BERT were used to identify RAI from radiology reports; LLM also extracted RAI actionable elements (modality, body part, time frame, rationale). Recall, precision, F1, and accuracy for RAI detection were determined using human-annotated reference standard. Paired comparison between each model's accuracy used McNemar's test. LLM recall, precision, F1, and accuracy for actionable element extraction were computed. Element prevalence was compared using chi-square test. RESULTS: 2000 random reports for unique patients (58% women; mean age 58.5 ± 17.5) included 215 RAI within 206 reports. For RAI detection, GPT-4.1 recall, precision, F1 and accuracy were 95.6%, 98.5%, 97.0% and 99.4%, respectively; BERT recall, precision, F1 and accuracy were 93.7%, 96.5%, 95.1% and 99.0% (p = 0.16). Recommendation element recall were as follows: 100.0% for time frame, 93.9% for body part, 90.2% for rationale, and 94.2% for modality. Precision were 100.0%, 95.7%, 94.6% and 86.1%, respectively. 29/215 (13.5%) RAI were complete (specified all actionable elements). Prevalence of actionable elements varied (p < 0.001), highest for rationale (215/215, 100%), followed by body part (213/215, 99.1%), modality (191/215, 88.8%), and time frame (31/215, 14.4%). CONCLUSION: GPT-4.1 demonstrated high accuracy for extraction of RAI and enabled accurate extraction of actionable elements. LLM integration into radiology workflows can facilitate automatic closed-loop communication and tracking of recommended imaging, which may reduce diagnostic delays.

  Nearest seed: Agentic AI in Medicine: Challenges for Responsible Development and the Case for Clinical Testing Harnesses.

  > [!abstract]-
  > AI-driven clinical decision support systems can prepare executable orders, communicate with patients, and initiate multistep workflows, raising unresolved questions about delegated clinical authority. This viewpoint proposes a 5-level clinical autonomy gradient: informational support (A0), advisory decision support (A1), human-confirmed execution (A2), bounded delegated action (A3), and adaptive autonomous management (A4). Classification applies to a task-action pair within its deployment workflow. Execution authority and case-specific human authorization anchor assignment; decision influence, temporal reach, and scope adaptability describe the configuration. The action-enablement boundary (A1-A2) introduces executable action preparation requiring explicit, informed, case-specific authorization before release. The delegation boundary (A2-A3) permits execution without contemporaneous, case-specific authorization. We adapt an automation scope of practice to record permissions and operating conditions, and an autonomy safety case to link authority to deployment-specific evidence and residual risks. Proposed controls address effective human review, interacting systems, proportionate governance, and withholding, downgrading, or suspending actions when supporting conditions fail. Applications to documented systems and hypothetical scenarios illustrate classification reasoning without establishing reliability or delegated-action safety. Greater autonomy is optional; A1 or A2 may remain appropriate. Independent evaluation must assess classification agreement, feasibility, implementation burden, and effects on care.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Artificial intelligence (AI) discourse in infectious disease (ID) has focused on benchmarking large language model chatbots against clinicians, a useful but narrowing inquiry. Agentic AI is a different paradigm: goal-directed systems that pursue clinical objectives over time, integrating data, invoking tools, monitoring results, and adapting without repeated prompting. This Viewpoint defines the anatomy of an agent, situates it within a 3-level framework (predictive, conversational, agentic), and examines how agents could transform 6 domains of ID practice: clinical care, antimicrobial stewardship, clinical microbiology, infection prevention and control, research, and epidemiology and pandemic preparedness.
