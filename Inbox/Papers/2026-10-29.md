  > [!abstract]-
  > BACKGROUND AND AIM: Mirikizumab was approved in China for the treatment of Crohn's disease (CD) in 2026. This study evaluated the induction and long-term maintenance of continuous mirikizumab treatment for up to 152 weeks in Chinese patients with moderately to severely active CD. METHODS: This was a post hoc, exploratory, and descriptive subpopulation analysis of Chinese patients from the VIVID program, where patients with moderately to severely active CD received intravenous (IV) mirikizumab induction followed by subcutaneous (SC) maintenance dosing. At week 52, endoscopic responders continued SC dosing, while non-responders received IV re-induction at VIVID-2 entry, then resumed SC dosing. Key efficacy endpoints included clinical response by Patient-Reported Outcome, endoscopic response, clinical remission, biomarkers, and quality-of-life-related endpoints. Safety was assessed through week 152 (starting from VIVID-1). RESULTS: In VIVID-1, 164 Chinese patients were randomized and received study treatment; 152 patients with simple endoscopic score for Crohn's disease ≥ 7 (or ≥ 4 for isolated ileal disease) were included in the primary analysis set (PAS). Among the PAS population, 83, 40, and 29 patients received mirikizumab, ustekinumab, and placebo, respectively. At week 52, the mirikizumab-treated group achieved higher endoscopic response-composite (37.3% vs. 0%) and Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI) clinical remission-composite (41.0% vs. 6.9%) than placebo (p < 0.001), and higher efficacy rates than ustekinumab. In VIVID-2, efficacy was sustained through week 152 among endoscopic responders, including CDAI remission (88.5%) and corticosteroid-free CDAI (88.5%). Among endoscopic non-responders, 45.5% regained endoscopic response at week 104 following IV re-induction, with CDAI remission rates of 94.1% at week 152. No unexpected safety findings were observed in the Chinese subpopulation, and the overall VIVID program reported a safety profile consistent with the global population. CONCLUSIONS: In Chinese patients with moderately to severely active CD, mirikizumab demonstrated favorable efficacy and safety as both induction and long-term maintenance therapy, consistent with the global population, supporting its potential as a long-term therapeutic option in China.   > Abstract
  >
  > Importance
  > Comparative data on risankizumab and upadacitinib in Crohn’s disease are limited.
  >
  >
  > Objective
  > We aimed to compare the real-world effectiveness of the 2 therapies, measured by treatment persistence, and to assess whether it differed according to disease location.
  >
  >
  > Design
  > Retrospective cohort study.
  >
  >
  > Setting
  > Nationwide CREdIT prospective registry.
  >
  >
  > Participants
  > Pediatric and adult patients with Crohn’s disease.
  >
  >
  > Exposures
  > Initiation of risankizumab or upadacitinib.
  >
  >
  > Main Outcomes and Measures
  > The primary outcome was treatment persistence, defined as time from therapy initiation to drug discontinuation, with censoring at the last follow-up, and evaluated using weighted Cox models clustered by patient. Missing covariates were handled using multiple imputation, and treatment groups were balanced using inverse probability weighting (IPW) based on propensity scores.
  >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 560 treatment observations were included (264 risankizumab and 296 upadacitinib), most representing advanced lines of therapy. Following IPW, upadacitinib was associated with a higher risk of treatment discontinuation than risankizumab (Hazard ratio [HR] 1.86, 95% CI, 1.02-3.39). The interaction between upadacitinib therapy and isolated colonic involvement was significant (P = .049).
  >
  >
  > Conclusions and Relevance
  > In this nationwide real-world cohort of predominantly advanced treatment-experienced patients with Crohn’s disease, risankizumab was associated with greater treatment persistence than upadacitinib. The superiority of risankizumab was observed mainly in patients with ileal involvement, and these findings suggest that disease location may be an important consideration when choosing between the 2 therapies.

  > [!abstract]-
  > Abstract
  >
  > Background and Aims
  > A significant number of patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) undergo stoma-forming surgery. For many, an ostomy is not curative, and disease exacerbations requiring ongoing therapy may be required. Despite this, IBD patients with stomas are frequently excluded from clinical trials due to the heterogeneity in outcomes and the absence of validated measurement tools. The TOAST-IBD project aimed to develop a Core Outcome Set (COS) for IBD patients with stomas undergoing interventional trials to address this issue.
  >
  >
  > Methods
  > Candidate outcomes were identified through a systematic literature review and patient focus groups.   >
  >
  > Results
  > A total of 81 outcomes were identified and categorized into five themes. In total, 76 stakeholders (73.1% response rate) from 17 countries participated in the Delphi process, carrying 37 outcomes forward.   >
  >
  > Conclusion
  > An IBD Stoma COS has been produced by all key stakeholders. This COS will standardize outcome reporting and support creation of validated measurement instruments, facilitating the inclusion of this underserved group in future clinical trials.
  >
  >
  > Registration
  > This study has been registered with the COMET Initiative as study 2855 (https://www.comet-initiative.org/Studies/Details/2855) and followed its recommended methodological procedures. The systematic review has been registered with the PROSPERO database (PROSPERO-ID: CRD42023442388).

- [ ] **Automation Bias and Deskilling in Radiology AI: A                     Confidence-stratified Triage Model for Workflow Integration**
- [ ] Relevant
- [ ] Read later
  Nalan Karunanayake, Javin Schefflein, Lawrence H. Schwartz
  Radiology: Artificial Intelligence | 2026-10-28
  [10.1148/ryai.260591](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260591) | Score: 0.95
  Nearest seed: Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (CLAIM):                     Explanation, Elaboration, and Examples

  > [!abstract]-
  > Radiology is adopting artificial intelligence (AI) faster than it is studying what AI does to radiologists’ perceptual expertise and interpretive skill. The question has partly shifted from whether these tools perform well on benchmark tasks to what happens to clinical judgment when use of these tools becomes routine. Two risks accompany this transition: automation bias, the tendency to follow AI suggestions without adequate scrutiny, and deskilling, the erosion of perceptual and interpretive ability through disuse, typically revealed when AI support is withdrawn. Incorrect AI output degrades accuracy regardless of experience, the benefit of assistance varies unpredictably across readers and tasks, and performance falls after AI withdrawal; neither risk has been studied longitudinally in radiology. A confidence-stratified triage model is proposed in which calibrated AI confidence, not findings alone, routes cases to low-, intermediate-, or high-confidence streams: output withheld until independent reading, shown with uncertainty signals and a checklist, or shown with random blinded audit. Demonstrated local confidence calibration is a precondition for deployment, and stream thresholds and audit rates should be weighted by task and potential clinical harm, not error probability alone. Rather than applying one workflow to every case, the model redistributes radiologist attention to where AI error is most likely and most consequential, operationalizing four principles: keeping the radiologist in the decision loop, making AI uncertainty visible, inserting targeted cognitive friction, and governing the human-AI interaction as a system. A research agenda to determine whether these countermeasures reduce automation bias and skill erosion in practice is also proposed.
