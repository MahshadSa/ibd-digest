# Work disability in patients with newly diagnosed inflammatory bowel disease: Results from the population-based IBSEN III cohort.

DOI: [10.1093/ibd/izag213](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag213)

## Abstract

BACKGROUND: This study aimed to compare the proportion of Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC) patients receiving work disability (WD) benefits with the corresponding proportion in the background population at diagnosis and after 1 year, and to assess associations between selected variables and WD. METHODS: Adults from a population-based inception cohort with WD data at diagnosis and/or 1-year follow-up were included. WD was defined as receiving work assessment allowance (AAP) or disability pension (DP). Relative risks (RRs) for AAP and DP were calculated vs. national registry proportions stratified by age and sex, overall RRs were age standardized. Multivariable logistic regression assessed associations between WD and selected variables. RESULTS: At diagnosis, risk of AAP was increased for UC (RR, 1.5; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.1-2.1) and CD patients between 30-39 years old (RR, 2.8; 95% CI, 1.1-5.8). AAP risk was increased at 1-year for both UC (RR, 1.6; 95% CI, 1.1-2.4) and CD (RR, 3.6; 95% CI, 2.4-5.2). DP risk was comparable with the background population. Higher general self-efficacy and health-related quality of life were associated with lower odds of WD, whereas disease-activity variables were not. Older age, comorbidities, and lower education were also associated with increased odds of WD in UC. CONCLUSIONS: After 1 year, patients with UC and CD had increased risk of AAP. Lower general self-efficacy and poorer health-related quality of life were associated with higher WD, suggesting a potential window for targeted interventions to reduce AAP and prevent or delay DP.
