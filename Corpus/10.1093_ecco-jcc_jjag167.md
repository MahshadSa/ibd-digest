# Treatment patterns and determinants of persistence with advanced therapies in adults with newly diagnosed Crohn’s disease: an interim analysis of an inception cohort registry study (iCREST-CD)

DOI: [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag167](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag167)

## Abstract

Abstract

Background and Aims
Multiple advanced therapies are available for the treatment of Crohn’s disease (CD). Treatment patterns in newly diagnosed patients and the factors associated with persistence remain unclear. We present interim results of the iCREST-CD registry describing treatment patterns and persistence with advanced therapies.


Methods
Patients aged ≥16 years with newly diagnosed CD and who had no prior exposure to advanced therapies were enrolled. Persistence of advanced therapies was estimated using Kaplan–Meier analyses, and associated factors were identified using multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. Persistence was also compared between patients who initiated therapy early or later after diagnosis.


Results
Of 672 patients (68.2% male; mean age 29.4 ± 13.1 years), 498 (74.1%) initiated advanced therapy: infliximab (IFX; 121), adalimumab (ADA; 244), ustekinumab (UST; 107), or vedolizumab (VDZ; 26). Advanced therapy was initiated within 1 year of diagnosis in 404 (60.1%). The 36-month treatment persistence rates for IFX, ADA, UST, and VDZ were 77.5%, 75.7%, 79.8%, and 49.0%, respectively. After propensity score adjustment using inverse probability of treatment weighting, the early initiation was associated with greater persistence than delayed initiation, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (hazard ratio, 0.61; 95% confidence interval, 0.35-1.09; P = .067).


Conclusions
A high prevalence of early advanced therapy use and a high level of persistence with first-line advanced therapy were identified in this national cohort. Early initiation was associated with greater treatment persistence than delayed initiation, suggesting the potential clinical relevance of earlier advanced therapy use in routine practice. These findings require confirmation in other prospective studies aiming to evaluate early intervention strategies.


Registration
The study was registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (umin.ac.jp/ctr Identifier: UMIN000032237) prior to initiation.
