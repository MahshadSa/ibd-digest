# Sustained Efficacy and Safety of Mirikizumab Through 152 Weeks in Chinese Patients with Moderately to Severely Active Crohn's Disease: A Subpopulation Analysis of the VIVID-1 and VIVID-2 Phase 3 Trials.

DOI: [10.1007/s40259-026-00810-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40259-026-00810-z)

## Abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIM: Mirikizumab was approved in China for the treatment of Crohn's disease (CD) in 2026. This study evaluated the induction and long-term maintenance of continuous mirikizumab treatment for up to 152 weeks in Chinese patients with moderately to severely active CD. METHODS: This was a post hoc, exploratory, and descriptive subpopulation analysis of Chinese patients from the VIVID program, where patients with moderately to severely active CD received intravenous (IV) mirikizumab induction followed by subcutaneous (SC) maintenance dosing. At week 52, endoscopic responders continued SC dosing, while non-responders received IV re-induction at VIVID-2 entry, then resumed SC dosing. Key efficacy endpoints included clinical response by Patient-Reported Outcome, endoscopic response, clinical remission, biomarkers, and quality-of-life-related endpoints. Safety was assessed through week 152 (starting from VIVID-1). RESULTS: In VIVID-1, 164 Chinese patients were randomized and received study treatment; 152 patients with simple endoscopic score for Crohn's disease ≥ 7 (or ≥ 4 for isolated ileal disease) were included in the primary analysis set (PAS). Among the PAS population, 83, 40, and 29 patients received mirikizumab, ustekinumab, and placebo, respectively. At week 52, the mirikizumab-treated group achieved higher endoscopic response-composite (37.3% vs. 0%) and Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI) clinical remission-composite (41.0% vs. 6.9%) than placebo (p < 0.001), and higher efficacy rates than ustekinumab. A greater decrease in C-reactive protein and fecal calprotectin from baseline was observed through week 52 in mirikizumab versus ustekinumab. In VIVID-2, efficacy was sustained through week 152 among endoscopic responders, including CDAI remission (88.5%) and corticosteroid-free CDAI (88.5%). Among endoscopic non-responders, 45.5% regained endoscopic response at week 104 following IV re-induction, with CDAI remission rates of 94.1% at week 152. No unexpected safety findings were observed in the Chinese subpopulation, and the overall VIVID program reported a safety profile consistent with the global population. CONCLUSIONS: In Chinese patients with moderately to severely active CD, mirikizumab demonstrated favorable efficacy and safety as both induction and long-term maintenance therapy, consistent with the global population, supporting its potential as a long-term therapeutic option in China. VIVID-1: NCT03926130; VIVID-2: NCT04232553.
