# Effects of Lipid-lowering Drug Targets on Multisystem Mucosal Barrier Diseases: A Mendelian Randomization Analysis.

DOI: [10.2174/0118715303521549261104113617](https://doi.org/10.2174/0118715303521549261104113617)

## Abstract

INTRODUCTION: Lipid metabolism is closely linked to epithelial barrier integrity, but the causal effects of different lipid-lowering drug targets on mucosal barrier diseases across the digestive tract and ocular surface remain unclear. METHODS: Using a drug-target Mendelian randomization approach with genome-wide association study and FinnGen data, we evaluated causal relationships between seven lipid-lowering drug targets- low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR), 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMGCR), proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9), Niemann-Pick C1-like 1 (NPC1L1), lipoprotein lipase (LPL), apolipoprotein C-III (APOC3), and angiopoietin-like 3 (ANGPTL3) and mucosal barrier diseases. RESULTS: Genetically proxied triglyceride lowering mediated by both LPL activation and ANGPTL3 inhibition was significantly associated with reduced risk of gastroduodenal ulcer and gastric ulcer (ORLPL = 0.790 and 0.701; ORANGPTL3 = 0.660 and 0.523). Genetically proxied low-density lipoprotein cholesterol lowering mediated by LDLR activation was significantly associated with reduced risk of inflammatory bowel disease, ulcerative colitis, and Crohn's disease (OR = 0.732, 0.756, and 0.670), with the protective effect significant only in small-intestinal Crohn's disease (OR = 0.658). HMGCR inhibition reduced cholelithiasis risk (OR = 0.644), while PCSK9 inhibition increased it (OR = 1.248). DISCUSSION: The restriction of the LDLR effect to small-intestinal Crohn's disease is consistent with a role for ileal bile acid reabsorption and cholesterol handling in mucosal immune regulation. The opposing biliary effects of HMGCR and PCSK9 suggest that targets that lower LDL-C may act through distinct mechanisms. CONCLUSION: Lipid-lowering drug targets show target- and organ-dependent differential effects on mucosal barrier diseases, supporting individualized drug selection and monitoring for mucosal-related adverse effects during long-term lipid-lowering therapy.
