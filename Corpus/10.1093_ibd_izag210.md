# Association of colonic motility on magnetic resonance enterography with endoscopic disease activity and chronic structural damage in ulcerative colitis.

DOI: [10.1093/ibd/izag210](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag210)

## Abstract

BACKGROUND: Colonic motor dysfunction has been described in ulcerative colitis (UC) but has been less systematically assessed using quantitative imaging. We examined whether motility changes are associated with endoscopic disease activity and imaging features of disease chronicity. METHODS: This prospective cross-sectional study included 49 adults with known UC who underwent magnetic resonance enterography and colonoscopy and 30 control participants. Colonic motility was quantified using GIQuant software. Endoscopic activity was graded by Mayo endoscopic subscore (MES) (0-3). Associations between motility and MES were assessed by ordinal regression and diagnostic performance by area under the curve. Imaging features of chronicity were recorded in segments with low endoscopic activity (MES ≤1). RESULTS: GIQuant values were lower in patients with UC than control subjects in every colonic segment (mean difference -205.9 to -110.1 units; all false discovery rate-adjusted P ≤.001). Higher motility was associated with lower MES (odds ratio per 1-SD increase, 0.38; 95% confidence interval, 0.28-0.52). The area under the curve was 0.83 for MES ≥1, 0.70 for MES ≥2, and 0.73 for MES ≥3. In segments with MES ≤1, chronicity features were associated with lower GIQuant values, with the largest differences observed for loss of haustration and increased pericolic fat. CONCLUSIONS: GIQuant-derived bowel wall motion is lower in UC and inversely associated with endoscopic activity. Correlations with chronic magnetic resonance enterography features remain present in segments with MES ≤1. Prospective validation with standardized colonic cine magnetic resonance imaging and concurrent symptom measures is needed before clinical use.
