# Longitudinal timepoint assessment of patient-reported clinical disease activity in ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease: suitability of a rapid Patient-Reported Outcome 2 (PRO-2) assessment

DOI: [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag153](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag153)

## Abstract

Abstract

Background & Aims
Patient-reported outcomes (PRO) are essential for monitoring disease activity and treatment response in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). The conventional PRO-2 requires several days of prospective symptom recording, increasing participant burden and attrition in longitudinal research. We evaluated whether a “rapid PRO-2”, using 2 days of data collected retrospectively and prospectively in a single day, could serve as a reliable and pragmatic alternative to a 4-day PRO-2.


Methods
Using data from the ongoing IBD-RESPONSE cohort (n = 684 Crohn’s disease [CD]; n = 548 ulcerative colitis [UC]), we compared 4-day PRO-2 and rapid PRO-2 scores at baseline and 14 weeks post-therapy to assess disease activity, clinical (non) response and remission.


Results
At baseline, 87.1% (596/684) of CD and 88.7% (486/548) of UC patients remained in the same active/inactive disease activity category (Cohen’s kappa: CD 0.715; UC 0.762; both P &lt; .001). At week 14, 77% (CD; 195/252) and 83.1% (UC; 193/232) remained in the same non-response/response/remission category (Cohen’s kappa: CD 0.653; UC 0.743; both P &lt; .001). Discrepancies mainly involved adjacent category shifts (ie, between response and remission, or non-response and response), with minimal re-classification between remission and non-response (n = 7 CD and n = 2 UC). Greater symptom variability across the 4 days increased the likelihood of switching between remission and non-response (odds ratio [95% confidence interval], CD 5.0 [3.65–6.85]; UC 7.8 [5.6–11.0], both P &lt; .001). Responsiveness to clinical change was comparable between instruments.


Conclusions
The rapid PRO-2 retains essential symptom data and demonstrates strong agreement with the 4-day PRO-2, offering an alternative in longitudinal studies to minimize burden and attrition.
