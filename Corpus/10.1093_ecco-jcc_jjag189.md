# Risk of perianal penetrating complications among adult patients with non-penetrating Crohn’s disease initiating advanced therapies: a retrospective cohort study

DOI: [10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag189](https://doi.org/10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjag189)

## Abstract

Abstract

Background & Aims
We conducted a retrospective cohort study comparing the risk of perianal penetrating complications with advanced therapies (AT) in patients with non-penetrating Crohn’s disease (CD).


Methods
Using an administrative claims database (OptumLabs Data Warehouse), we identified adult patients with CD who initiated treatment with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) antagonists, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, IL-23p19 antagonists or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors between 2016 and 2022. Patients with any CD-related penetrating complication, abdominal or perianal, during the baseline 1 year and up to 3 months post AT initiation were excluded. We compared on-therapy incidence and risk of developing perianal penetrating complications across different ATs, through multinomial propensity score-based inverse probability weighting.


Results
We identified 10 482 patients with non-penetrating CD who initiated TNF antagonists (n = 4463), vedolizumab (n = 2372), ustekinumab (n = 3027), IL-23p19 antagonists (n = 480) and JAK inhibitors (n = 140), of whom 360 (3.4%) developed perianal penetrating complications over a mean follow-up of 18.511.2 months. After adjusting for confounding variables, TNF antagonists (hazard ratio [HR], 0.56 [95% CI, 0.42-0.74]) and ustekinumab (HR, 0.62 [0.46-0.83]) were associated with lower risks of developing perianal penetrating complications compared with vedolizumab. Among advanced therapy-naive patients (n = 8240), TNF antagonists (HR, 0.66 [0.49-0.89]) and ustekinumab (HR, 0.69 [0.48-0.99]) were associated with lower risk of perianal penetrating complications compared with vedolizumab. Among patients with prior AT exposure (n = 2242), TNF antagonists (HR, 0.26 [0.08-0.85]) and ustekinumab (HR, 0.46 [0.30-0.72]) were associated with lower risk of perianal penetrating complications compared with vedolizumab.


Conclusions
Among patients with non-penetrating CD starting an AT, TNF antagonists and ustekinumab were associated with a lower risk of developing perianal penetrating complications compared to vedolizumab.
